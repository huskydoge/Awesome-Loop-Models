# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-08-17

## Outcome

Three papers were added. All three make computation-depth reuse explicit: they
reapply the same learned block or operator within one model forward process, or
directly analyze a model with that architecture. Papers with merely sequential
state recurrence, repeated full-model calls, or unverified cross-stage parameter
sharing remain outside the collection.

## Added

### RecurrentGPT (`2608.15062`)

RecurrentGPT places a single shared Transformer core between fixed prelude and
coda blocks. The core is iterated `R` times, while a learned gate conditioned on
the current hidden state and prelude output modulates each recurrent update.

Why it was added: the paper explicitly turns physical parameter sharing into a
recurrent-depth axis inside one language-model forward process. This is the
collection's central loop-model mechanism, rather than ordinary token-time
recurrence.

Reader takeaway: under isoFLOPS, a model with three distinct layers matches a
12-layer GPT-2 Small baseline and leads the compared recurrent-depth baselines in
all nine reported scale-by-budget cells. At the largest reported scale it uses
63% fewer parameters and 59% less peak decoding memory, with a 10% increase in
compiled generation latency. The result is a parameter-memory trade rather than
a universal speed-up.

### BRAID (`2608.14856`)

BRAID uses a weight-tied iterative graph neural network whose gated recurrent
update mirrors damped best-response dynamics in interdependent security games.
The repeated learned update predicts a Nash equilibrium directly from game and
graph parameters.

Why it was added: the same learned graph update is explicitly reused over
computation depth in a single forward pass. The application is domain-specific,
but its mechanism is a clear shared neural algorithmic processor.

Reader takeaway: BRAID reports up to a 43x per-instance speed-up over iterative
best-response solving and also evaluates how well the learned equilibrium map
recovers sensitivity to cost and edge-weight perturbations. Its evidence is
therefore stronger than equilibrium-value prediction alone, though it remains
limited to the studied security-game families.

### Behaviour is an incomplete measure (`2608.16085`)

This analysis studies a 30M-parameter reasoner that repeatedly applies one
four-layer block within a single forward pass. It tracks behavioural acquisition
and hidden-state probe accessibility across symbolic and verbal training
surfaces.

Why it was added: physical reuse of the recurrent block is explicit, and the
paper's main subject is how measurements evolve inside that recurrent-depth
computation.

Reader takeaway: under the frozen acquisition criterion, verbal three-hop
competence takes 13,055 logical epochs versus 70 on the symbolic surface, a
186.5-fold difference. Linear probes can recover some future-answer information
before behavioural arrival under preregistered controls. The author correctly
does not interpret this accessibility as proof of the computation learned during
training and calls for causal intervention.

## Near misses

- **MODAL** (`2608.15096`) uses repeated deep-unrolling stages, but the paper only
  explicitly shares parameters across modalities. Its parameter count rises from
  12.5M at `K=1` to 34.9M at `K=4`, which is inconsistent with verified
  recurrent-depth weight tying.
- **CG-GLORE** (`2608.15246`) unrolls 15 reconstruction stages, but does not state
  that the learned GLORE regularizer is shared across them. The repeated inner CG
  operation is a non-learned physics solver, so it does not establish learned
  recurrent depth.
- **Intervention-Aware Clinical World Model** (`2608.13518`) updates a latent
  state along an event sequence. That is ordinary sequence-time recurrence, not
  reuse over computation depth.
- **RRFC** (`2608.15694`) feeds a generator's previous full prediction back into
  another generator call, including diffusion settings. It is repeated
  full-model inference rather than an internal recurrent-depth block.
