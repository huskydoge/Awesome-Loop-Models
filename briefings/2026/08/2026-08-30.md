# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-08-30

## Outcome

Two papers were added. One analyzes how an iterated weight-tied Transformer enters
different dynamical phases; the other uses a shared causal Transformer block to scale
computation under limited-data pretraining. Both reuse learned internal computation
within one model forward process.

## Added

### Dynamical phase selection controls compute scaling in looped transformers (`2608.26556`)

This paper treats a looped Transformer as a nonlinear dynamical system. Networks with
the same architecture, objective, and perfect held-out accuracy can settle into
different initialization-selected phases. In the fold phase, a local normal-form
reduction predicts relaxation time and spectral-gap amplitudes without fitted
parameters, including a workload tail proportional to `N^-2`; a Neimark-Sacker phase
instead produces bounded nonstationary motion with no analogous fold law.

Why it was added: the analyzed model explicitly iterates one weight-tied
attention-plus-MLP block, and the paper's central contribution is an explanation of
how that recurrent-depth computation determines test-time compute.

Reader takeaway: loop architecture and task accuracy alone do not determine inference
cost. The dynamical phase selected by training can change both convergence behavior
and the tail of per-instance iteration counts. The evidence is precise but currently
limited to small synthetic decision models rather than language-model-scale workloads.

### Squeezing More from Limited Data with Recursive Transformers (`2608.26973`)

RecursiveGPT combines factorized vocabulary maps with a causal Transformer block whose
attention and MLP weights are reused for 16 or 24 depth steps. This separates
trainable-parameter capacity from per-token compute in the 10M--100M-word pretraining
regime, where simply increasing model size or repeating more epochs can worsen
generalization.

Why it was added: the shared block is repeatedly applied inside each token forward,
making recurrent depth the paper's main architectural scaling axis.

Reader takeaway: the 27.6M-parameter recursive model beats matched standard models on
all three main 10M-word evaluations, while the 404.1M recursive model slightly exceeds
a 1.22B standard model on the 100M-word average. The method saves parameters, not
compute: its recurrent models execute substantially more FLOPs, and deeper recurrence
does not improve performance monotonically.

## Near misses

- `2608.26673` uses hierarchical recurrent control together with prediction-error-based
  online latent inference. Its recurrence is organized around temporal control and
  energy-style iterative inference rather than recurrent learned depth.
- `2608.27259` uses a shared evolution-operator structure for world-model transitions,
  but the abstract does not establish repeated application of one learned operator
  within a single forward process.
- `2608.27265` identifies causal K/V-cache carriers in latent chain-of-thought models,
  without introducing or analyzing an explicitly reused recurrent-depth block.
- `2608.27360` performs iterative privacy-utility refinement around a frozen voice
  cloning model rather than reusing an internal learned module.
- `2608.27141` studies persistent state in autonomous LLM-agent loops, which are outside
  the internal model-forward scope.
