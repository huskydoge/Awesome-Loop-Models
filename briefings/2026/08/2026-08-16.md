# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-08-16

## Outcome

Two papers were added. **Nanbeige4.2-3B on Apple Silicon** examines the concrete
memory and deployment costs of running a looped language model on unified-memory
hardware. **DFM Mimir v1** applies the HRM-Text architecture to a permissibility-
focused Danish-English training recipe at one-billion-parameter scale.

## Added

### Looped-transformer deployment on Apple Silicon (`2608.13987`)

Nanbeige4.2-3B sends the hidden state through the same complete layer stack a
second time before producing logits, giving two effective executions of every
physical layer within one model forward process. The paper studies the resulting
attention-memory pressure and fixes several MLX deployment issues rather than
proposing a new loop architecture.

Why it was added: its subject is explicitly the systems behavior of reused
internal Transformer layers. Chunked prefill limits the size of temporary
attention-score blocks while retaining the model's two-pass computation.

Reader takeaway: on an Apple M2 Max with 32 GiB unified memory, the largest
reported LongBench-Pro prompt rises from 4,096 tokens with naive prefill to
11,231 with chunked prefill, about 2.74x. The outputs are reported as bit-
identical, but the method trades capacity for speed: measured prefill throughput
is 22.8% lower at 1,024 tokens and 40.9% lower at 2,048 tokens. This is a
hardware-specific deployment result, not an extension of the model's trained
context window or a reduction in total quadratic attention work.

### Permissible bilingual HRM-Text training (`2608.13517`)

Mimir is a 1B language model using two slow H cycles, each containing three fast
L updates followed by one H update. The learned L module is therefore reused six
times and the learned H module twice during a forward process. Its training mix
contains 161 datasets and about 70.5B tokens per epoch, emphasizing Danish and
English instruction data, mathematics, reasoning, and tool use.

Why it was added: Mimir directly deploys hierarchical recurrent depth as the
central architecture of a language model trained from scratch. It also retains
HRM-Text's response-only objective and PrefixLM attention while moving to a
Gemma-4 tokenizer and chat template.

Reader takeaway: Mimir averages 69.0 across the reported English suite versus
66.1 for HRM-Text, 64.1 versus 46.9 on Math and Code, and 56.8 versus 21.7 on
Danish tasks. These comparisons demonstrate a capable bilingual HRM application,
but they do not isolate recurrence: Mimir changes the tokenizer, chat format,
training data, and recurrence schedule together. PrefixLM also makes prior-token
KV states dependent on the complete prompt, which limits straightforward
incremental cache reuse when a conversation grows across turns.
