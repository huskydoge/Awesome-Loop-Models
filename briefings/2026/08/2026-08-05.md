# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-08-05

## Outcome

Two recent papers were added. **LoopMTP** proposes training and aggregation
mechanisms for a vertically looped language model. **The Ignition Is Real**
provides a controlled mechanistic study of answer commitment across recurrent
depth.

## Added

### LoopMTP (`2608.03624`)

LoopMTP reuses one 12-layer Transformer stack for multiple iterations. Its main
idea is to give iteration `t` a distinct training role: the hidden state at that
iteration is softly aligned, through cosine similarity, with the output
embedding of the token `t` positions ahead. Unlike conventional multi-token
prediction, this does not require a full vocabulary projection and
cross-entropy loss at every loop.

The architecture also retains all intermediate loop states and combines them
with a content-conditioned learned gate. Iteration indexing, per-iteration
normalization, and fixed `1/T` residual scaling are used to distinguish loop
roles and stabilize repeated application of the shared stack.

Why it was added: the same learned Transformer stack is explicitly reused
within one model forward process. The paper reports up to an 8.1% relative
average-accuracy improvement over a non-looped baseline and stable training up
to 15 loops at roughly 260M parameters.

#### What the ablations establish

The paper directly compares training with and without the latent MTP alignment
loss. MTP improves general-task and QA/math/code results, especially at larger
loop counts, and makes representations from successive loops less similar. It
also compares learned aggregation against taking only the final loop and
uniformly averaging loops; learned aggregation is strongest, particularly on
math at larger recurrence depths.

The paper does **not** independently ablate iteration indexing,
per-iteration LayerNorm, or `1/T` residual scaling. Indexing is also confounded
with the per-iteration norms, which themselves expose the loop identity.
Consequently, the experiments do not establish whether indexing or residual
scaling is more important. Mechanistically, residual scaling is the more direct
stability control, while indexing is more plausibly responsible for loop-role
specialization; that distinction remains a hypothesis rather than a reported
causal result. The appendix further shows that weight decay alone can produce
up to a 53% relative GSM8K difference at a fixed loop count, so stability should
not be attributed to residual scaling alone.

### The Ignition Is Real, and It Lives at the Readout (`2608.03263`)

This paper reproduces a 30M-parameter recurrent-depth reasoner and measures each
iteration through both its vocabulary readout and hidden-state geometry. It
reports that answer commitment occurs as a sharp, difficulty-dependent readout
transition: the decisive iteration moves later as compositional depth grows,
and the winning logit margin jumps by 5.8--8.0 logits before remaining stable.

The hidden state also changes sharply in raw geometry and then largely freezes
in direction, while later movement is predominantly radial and nearly invisible
to the readout. The paper withdraws an earlier velocity-trough claim after
normalization controls showed it was coordinate-dependent, and it does not
confirm the stronger decoder-coordinate version of the hidden-state snap.

Why it was added: the object of analysis is explicitly a recurrent-depth model
that reuses an internal learned Transformer block. The result is relevant to
mechanistic interpretation of loop models, but its scope is deliberately
narrow: a synthetic closed-world reasoner rather than a standard large language
model.

## Checked but not added

- **Loop-Mamba** (`2608.02346`) is a plausible recurrence hit, but the abstract
  is centered on image restoration and does not make the canonical same-forward
  reuse of a learned internal block sufficiently explicit for inclusion yet.
- **TimeRLM** (`2608.03391`) performs recursive external language-model and code
  interactions rather than recurrent reuse of an internal learned module.
- **GradCuit** (`2608.02585`) optimizes latent states at test time; its abstract
  does not establish recurrent reuse of a learned internal block.
