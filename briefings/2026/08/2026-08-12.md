# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-08-12

## Outcome

Two papers were added. **Retrofitting Recurrent Depth into a Pretrained Language
Model** converts the middle of a dense pretrained Transformer into a recurrent,
weight-tied computation path. **ODE-Based Transformer Decoders for Iterative
Sign Language Translation** strengthens a latent refinement decoder by repeatedly
evaluating the same learned decoder function through Runge--Kutta updates.

## Added

### Recurrent-depth retrofit (`2608.11233`)

The paper splits Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct into a six-layer Prelude, a 12-layer
weight-tied Recurrent Block, and a six-layer Coda. Later loops reuse the middle
block and combine its carried state with the Prelude representation through a
learned re-entry bridge; one loop reproduces the original dense path.

Why it was added: the same learned middle block is explicitly reapplied over
computation depth within one model forward process. The paper also tests two
ways to install that loop after pretraining: full-block adaptation and a
frozen-base rank-16 LoRA budget.

Reader takeaway: on controlled rule-composition tasks, the adapter and full
block reach 83.8% and 84.0% overall accuracy. The recurrent model retains 53%
accuracy beyond depth 10 versus 2.5% for a matched scratchpad model, but these
results do not establish general natural-reasoning gains. The reported 7.6x
speed-up is a batch-1, single-hardware comparison against the scratchpad arm at
depth 14, not a general throughput claim.

### ODE-inspired iterative decoder (`2608.11352`)

This ACVR 2026 workshop paper replaces ordinary residual refinement updates in
a sign-language translation decoder with RK-2 and RK-4 formulations. Every
Runge--Kutta stage is a repeated application of the same decoder function, and
the decoder is also reused across latent refinement iterations.

Why it was added: the contribution directly changes how one learned internal
decoder operator is reused during a single translation forward process. The
method adds function evaluations rather than decoder parameters, making it a
clear loop-model application rather than generic iterative decoding language.

Reader takeaway: the strongest matched results reach 22.96 BLEU-4 on
PHOENIX-14T and 19.34 on CSL-Daily, both above the reproduced IPSLT baseline.
The efficiency trade-off is configuration-dependent: the best PHOENIX variant
reduces measured latency, while the best-BLEU CSL configuration incurs modest
extra FLOPs and latency. The evidence therefore supports stronger refinement
dynamics, not a universal speed-up.

## Near misses

- **Chain-of-Thought Shows the Path to a Tree** (`2608.11716`) reuses a decoder
  across autoregressive reasoning tokens, not as recurrent depth inside one
  forward process.
- **Consolidator** (`2608.11701`) learns a shared memory-write operator across
  context boundaries; its reuse is a sequence-memory mechanism rather than an
  internal depth loop.
- **Semantic Lenia** (`2608.11657`) applies external logit-space dynamics across
  generated tokens. It does not recurrently reuse an internal learned block.
