# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-10-22

## Outcome

Three papers were added, and each reuses a learned block inside one forward pass. One is a
training study: it measures exactly how much gradient truncated backprop throws away in
published looped language models. The other two bring looping to new settings: the
denoiser of a uniform diffusion language model, and a sequential recommender that decides
per item how many times to apply its shared block.

One addition cites Benhao Huang's work.
[How Much Gradient Does Truncated Backprop Lose in Looped Transformers?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28871)
cites Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II: Rethinking at Fixed Points. It uses the two
released Part II models as base models in its fine-tuning and training-cost tests, and cites
the work as related work for its truncated-backprop cost measurements and its finding that
truncated training makes an input-injected loop settle.

## Added

### How Much Gradient Does Truncated Backprop Lose in Looped Transformers?
[arXiv:2610.28871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28871) · Code: [Aur-Shalev-Merin/lost-gradient-looped-transformers](https://github.com/Aur-Shalev-Merin/lost-gradient-looped-transformers)

**TL;DR:** One full backward pass gives the share of weight gradient that truncated backprop
drops at every truncation length; on trained looped models this share is set near the
output, barely changes with the loop count, and falls during fine-tuning.

- **Setup:** Nine looped language models: Huginn-3.5B, Parcae-1.3B, -370m and -140m,
  Ouro-1.4B, two SmolLM2-135M conversions made by the author, and the two released
  Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II models. Sixteen studies were pre-registered.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Each loop's backward signal is pushed through its own weight
  Jacobian, so the dropped contributions for every truncation length k come from one pass.
  This lost gradient is compared with a geometric rule based on the backward signal's
  fitted shrink per loop, followed through 600-step GSM8K fine-tunes, and tested as a
  predictor of truncation's training cost.
- **Results:** From 32 to 64 loops the lost gradient moved by at most 0.013 on Parcae-140m
  and 0.011 on Parcae-370m. On Ouro the backward signal grows, and keeping two of its four
  loops still loses 81% of the final output's gradient. After 300 fine-tuning steps the lost
  gradient was 0.07–0.22 lower on three of four models, also on held-out problems. As a
  predictor of training cost it tied a rule that needs no measurement (average error 0.037
  against 0.036 on two new models).
- **Caveat:** Training-cost results come from short fine-tunes of already-trained models,
  where truncation costs little in absolute terms, so they are a narrow proxy for choosing
  the backprop window in pretraining. The cross-model test used only two small models.
- **Why added:** It studies how loop models are trained, measuring gradient flow through the
  reused block of published looped language models.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II, used as two base models
  for the fine-tuning and training-cost tests, and as related work on truncated-backprop
  cost and on truncated training making an input-injected loop settle.

### Looped Uniform Diffusion Language Models
[arXiv:2610.30066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30066) · Code: [SaitejaUtpala/looped_udlm](https://github.com/SaitejaUtpala/looped_udlm)

**TL;DR:** Looping a two-block slice of a uniform diffusion denoiser inside each denoising
step reaches the non-looped model's validation NLL with up to 3.3 times fewer training FLOPs.

- **Setup:** The 170M-parameter Duo++ small diffusion Transformer (12 blocks, GPT-2
  tokenizer), trained on OpenWebText and FineWeb-Edu, with the training objective and sampler
  left unchanged. The non-looped baseline trains for 1M steps.
- **Mechanism / Method:** The denoiser is split into a one-block head, a two-block middle
  slice that is applied repeatedly, and a tail. The loop count is sampled at every training
  step from a uniform, FLOP-proportional or noise-proportional distribution, up to 12. At
  inference the loop count is fixed or chosen adaptively, stopping once the hidden state
  barely changes.
- **Results:** Looped models match the baseline's validation NLL with 2.8 times fewer
  training FLOPs on OpenWebText and 3.3 times fewer on FineWeb-Edu. At the same number of
  denoising steps, generative perplexity is up to 9.8% lower, and zero-shot perplexity on
  seven out-of-domain datasets is 11.5% lower on average. On ten multiple-choice tasks,
  adaptive looping matches 12 fixed loops with 26.0% fewer iterations, and training with
  FLOP-proportional sampling cuts another 12%.
- **Caveat:** All results use one 170M model with one looped slice, and extra loops add
  compute at every one of the many denoising steps.
- **Why added:** A shared block of the denoiser is applied repeatedly within each denoiser
  call, so the loop is inside one forward pass rather than across denoising steps.

### Think Harder Where It Matters: Item-Level Adaptive Recursion for Sequential Recommendation
[arXiv:2610.30128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30128) · Code: [Elpmis117/LoopRec](https://github.com/Elpmis117/LoopRec)

**TL;DR:** LoopRec reapplies one weight-shared recommender block and lets a learned gate stop
each history item at its own depth, improving every fixed-depth backbone it was tested on.

- **Setup:** Three Amazon Reviews 2023 categories (Software, Office, Patio) and four
  backbone families: SASRec, BSARec, NextItNet and LRURec. A diagnostic on a three-layer
  SASRec finds that 76.14% of items settle after one layer while 6.72% need all three.
- **Mechanism / Method:** After an initial pass, a router reads each item's state plus a
  recursion-index embedding and decides to exit or continue; only continuing items pass
  through the shared block again, up to a maximum number of recursions. A load-balancing
  loss and a router z-loss keep depth usage spread and gates soft early in training.
- **Results:** All 48 backbone, dataset and metric comparisons improve and 44 are
  significant. NDCG@20 rises 2.19–5.68% on SASRec and up to 37.02% on NextItNet (Patio,
  0.0192 to 0.0263). Against fixed-depth SASRec references the selected compute is 0.407 to
  0.755 times, though encoder latency falls only to 0.869 to 0.995 times.
- **Caveat:** The main baselines are the original backbones; how their parameters and
  compute compare with the looped models is not stated as one control. The gain over a
  sequence-level looped baseline without routing is small on Office.
- **Why added:** One learned block is reused across recursions within one forward pass, with
  learned per-item halting.

## Near misses

- **Parallelizable Probabilistic Latent Reasoning** ([2610.29385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29385)):
  models distributions over continuous latent reasoning steps with a JEPA-style objective,
  but no learned layer or block is reused within a forward pass.
- **PACE** ([2610.29545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29545)): quantizes the recurrent state of
  linear-attention language models to four bits; this is ordinary sequence recurrence, not a
  reused block.
- **Recurrent Vision Transformers for Event-Based Spacecraft Pose Estimation**
  ([2610.25622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25622)): carries a recurrent memory across event
  frames over time, without feeding a computed state back through the same layers in depth.
