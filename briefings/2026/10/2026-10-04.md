# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-10-04

## Outcome

Twelve papers were added. Each applies a shared learned block, layer pool, or recurrent
cell several times within one forward pass. Seven are about looped language models and
looped mixture-of-experts, two analyze what shared loops compute and how recursive
reasoners should be trained, and three apply looping to vision-language models,
text-to-image diffusion, and world-model planning.

## Looped language models

### Training looped language models cheaply

[Closing the Loop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00673) trains a 1.4B looped language model
from scratch on 310B tokens instead of Ouro's 7.7T. Mid-training, learning-rate warmup,
and stronger exit-gate regularization keep recurrent training stable. Against a dense
model with the same parameters, data, and tokens, the looped model wins on all 12
benchmarks, including +14 points on GSM8K. The paper also converts Qwen3-1.7B-Base into
a looped model with only a learned input-mixing scalar and a smoothed exit loss.

### Sharing the KV cache across recursions

[The Surprising Effectiveness of Shared Memory in Looped Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02383)
lets only the first recursion write a KV cache; later recursions read it and keep a
short local window. At 150M to 1B parameters this improves quality instead of hurting
it. With five recursions, the gated-DeltaNet hybrid lowers FineWeb-Edu perplexity by
1.12 to 1.82 compared with a same-size standard Transformer while using 76 to 79 percent
less context memory.

### Contrastive decoding across loops

[Decoding Looped Transformers Better for (Almost) Free](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02185)
treats an earlier loop as the weak model in contrastive decoding. LoopCD needs no
training and works in logit space or hidden-state space. It raises Ouro-2.6B-Thinking's
AIME 2024 pass@1 from 61.88 to 73.33 percent and Huginn's HumanEval pass@1 from 22.56 to
31.71 percent, and lets models halve their loops while matching full-depth decoding.

### Looped mixture-of-experts

[Scaling Laws for Looped Mixture of Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40316) fits a
scaling law in which the effective-parameter gain from looping is bounded and grows
with MoE sparsity. At matched training compute on trillion-token runs, a looped MoE with
law-derived recurrence matches a non-looped MoE about twice its size on reasoning
benchmarks.

[Looping Beyond Twice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01153) explains why looped MoE models
usually stop at two loops: hidden-state variance grows with each loop, and routers pick
the same experts every time. LOOM scales residual updates, re-injects the input
embedding at every loop, uses a separate router per loop, and adds a looping residual.
Models from 100M to 1.7B train stably at 9 to 12 loops
([code](https://github.com/hed-ucas/LOOM)).

### Chain-of-thought monitorability

[On the Chain-of-Thought Monitorability of Looped Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02741)
evaluates Ouro at different loop depths and against non-looped models matched by size,
layer count, or effective depth on eight MonitorBench tasks. Deeper loops reduce
monitorability on some stress tests, but looped models are not systematically less
monitorable than matched non-looped models.

## Analysis

### What each loop computes

[Shared Weights, Selected Computations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39892) studies looped
Transformers trained on graph walks. Successive loops do not always advance by one step.
A learned linear map applied to the hidden state entering a frozen loop can choose which
transition the shared layers perform, and activation patching shows that attention
routing carries this control ([code](https://github.com/wjjpku/howloop)).

### What limits recursive reasoners

[What Limits Recursive Reasoning Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39967) compares
HRM, TRM, and URM-style training in one pipeline over six algorithmic domains. An
intermediate gradient horizon, large physical batches, and bounded, gated recurrent-state
updates matter; an explicit hierarchy does not. The resulting 13.6M-parameter model
reaches 71.2 percent on out-of-distribution arithmetic, compared with 36.2 percent for
the strongest baseline.

### Random layer order

[Random Recursive Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00541) keeps a small pool of learned
layers and samples one layer per recursive step. This allows variable depth and Monte
Carlo inference at test time, and it matches or beats fixed-order and HRM/TRM baselines
on image classification and logic puzzles, often with 50 to 75 percent fewer parameters.

## Applications

### Looped vision-language models

[LoopVL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38426) builds a vision-language model on an
HRM-Text-style backbone with two 16-layer modules that are looped, running 128 layer
calls from 32 unique layers. It outperforms similarly sized and larger non-recurrent
models on multimodal understanding and visual reasoning benchmarks
([code](https://github.com/Tier-Flow/LoopVL)).

### Looped diffusion transformers

[Looped Diffusion Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40305) loops a shared middle
group of MMDiT blocks inside each denoising step. Deep supervision across loops and
self-modulating attention make looping help. A 260M looped model beats a model 6.5 times
larger at 4.9 times lower inference compute, and more loops help more than more
denoising steps at a fixed budget ([code](https://github.com/OpenSenseNova/Looped-DiT)).

### Adaptive depth in a world model

[DeepJEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00368) makes each imagined transition of a JEPA
world-model planner a weight-tied recurrent cell with a learned decision to continue.
Across five visual-control settings it matches or beats the best fixed-depth planner
while averaging 1.00 to 1.26 updates per transition, spending extra updates mainly at
contact events.

## Near misses

- [Universal interpolation for deep residual self-attention networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01981)
  proves that two frozen, randomly initialized attention blocks applied in a chosen order
  can interpolate any finite set of sequence mappings. The reused blocks are random rather
  than learned, so it falls outside the strict scope.
- [T-Router](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39109) adds a depth-recurrent controller that
  reuses stored block changes of a frozen backbone for parameter-efficient RL. The
  backbone layers are not looped.
- [Port-Hamiltonian Latent Deliberation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37351) constrains
  latent reasoning flows to avoid drift at long horizons, but it does not clearly reuse a
  learned network block within one forward pass.
- [Fixed Universal Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31423) shows that fixed
  transformers can simulate others through their input embedding. It does not loop
  layers.
