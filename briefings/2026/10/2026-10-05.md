# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-10-05

## Outcome

Five papers were added. Each reapplies a shared learned block or core several times
within one forward pass. Two bring looping into generative models (diffusion language
models and flow-matching image transformers), one makes looped language models cheaper
to deploy, and two apply looped encoders to audio tagging and video restoration.

## Looped generative models

### Token-adaptive loops inside each denoising step

[ALoDLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04198) splits a Qwen3 backbone into a prelude, a
shared recurrent core, and a coda, and runs an inner loop over the core inside every
denoising step. Confident tokens commit early and are fed back as token embeddings,
while unresolved tokens keep their latent states for another pass; a learned exit gate,
trained through a conditional ELBO over token-wise exit schedules, ends the loop.
Converted from Qwen3 by SFT on 5B tokens, it averages 65.5 at 1.7B and 80.3 at 8B over
eleven reasoning, math, and coding benchmarks, above WeDLM-8B by 5.2 points and above
the Qwen3 AR models of the same size ([code](https://github.com/amazon-science/ALoDLM)).

### Looping past the training depth

[LiFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05538) loops a depth-conditioned DiT core inside each
flow-matching velocity evaluation and supervises every loop with a point on a straight
path from the model's first estimate to the flow-matching target. Because the targets are
indexed by a continuous depth coordinate, the model can run more loops at inference than
in training: an XL/2 checkpoint improves from FID 17.07 at its training depth to 9.31 at
sixteen loops. On ImageNet 256x256, LiFT-L/2 beats a dense DiT-XL/2 by 3.34 FID with about
60 percent fewer parameters and 52 percent fewer inference FLOPs.

## Deploying looped language models

[Loopy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05265) shows that the ranking of post-training
quantization settings for a shared recurrent core changes with loop depth, because
errors from the quantized core compound across loops. It therefore selects one shared
rotation and channel-scaling configuration by final loss at the target depth, using
progressive full-depth evaluation to keep calibration cheap. Across Ouro, Huginn, and
Recurrent-Llama it gives the best W4A8 and W4A4 results, including a 36.5 percent lower
LAMBADA perplexity than SpinQuant on Ouro-1.4B at W4A4.

## Applications

### Audio tagging with class-token loops

[LAST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01926) runs a shared stack of audio spectrogram
transformer blocks once over all tokens and then reuses the same blocks to update only
the class token against cached audio keys and values. On AudioSet, ten passes reach 0.345
mAP, 2.1 percent relative above a twelve-layer sequential transformer with 49.4 percent
fewer parameters and 42 percent fewer MACs; looping all tokens with the same blocks gets
worse with depth.

### UHD video restoration

[LoopMoEVR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05109) refines latent video features with one
parameter-shared loop conditioned on degradation-aware low-rank loop embeddings and expert
modulation, and a depth predictor picks four or five iterations per input. With about
0.884M parameters, one model handles dehazing, deraining, denoising, and low-light
enhancement and attains the best mean PSNR (27.87 dB) on UHV-4K-AIO.

## Near misses

- [Recurrent Latent Visual Search for GUI Grounding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05185)
  feeds aggregated visual evidence back into Qwen2.5-VL as the next input embedding. Each
  search step is another pass of the full model, not a reused internal block.
- [What to Preserve in Recursive Computation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04303) proposes
  a predictive-sufficiency constraint for recursive compression across memory, control,
  and self-improvement settings, without a looped learned block as the subject.
- [LatentIndex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04635) shares a latent indexer cache across
  layers of sparse-attention models; it shares cached representations, not layer weights.
