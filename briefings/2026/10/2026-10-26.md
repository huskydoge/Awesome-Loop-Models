# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-10-26

## Outcome

Three papers were added, all about how looped language models use their extra depth. One
asks what lets a looped model keep running loop steps past the count it was trained with.
One tests whether learned halting heads deliver the compute savings they are credited with.
The third builds a production-scale MoE model that serves several depth budgets from one
set of weights.

None of the additions cites Benhao Huang's work.

## Added

### Agreement rather than normalisation keeps depth reusable in looped language models
[arXiv:2610.31943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31943) · Code: none released yet (the paper
says it will follow review)

**TL;DR:** The paper argues that extra loop steps stay usable because the loop a model runs
at inference matches the loop it was trained with, not because the state is normalised
between steps.

- **Setup:** Released Huginn-3.5B, Ouro-1.4B and Ouro-2.6B (trained budget of 4 loop steps),
  plus 19M-76M looped models trained from scratch on synthetic Boolean-circuit and
  pointer-chasing tasks, with 16 seeds per configuration on the normalisation comparison.
- **Mechanism / Method:** For Ouro, the between-step normalisation is removed without new
  parameters while the readout path stays bit-identical. From scratch, a dedicated RMSNorm
  between loop steps is switched on or off before training. Probes, partial refits and a
  looped-versus-stacked comparison at a fixed 24 layer applications follow.
- **Results:** Without the norm, Ouro-2.6B on the circuit task falls from 0.530 at one step
  to 0.000 from the trained budget on, while the state norm grows by at most a factor of
  2.021. Trained from scratch, the arm without the norm matches or beats the normalised arm
  at the trained depth (0.999 against 0.963) and holds 0.932 at eight times the budget against
  0.584. Refitting the readout recovers about a third of the lost accuracy; refitting it with
  the last block layer recovers all of it.
- **Caveat:** The causal evidence comes from small models on two synthetic tasks. The
  released models fail the competence gate on some tasks. The looped-versus-stacked
  advantage vanishes on one task and shrinks on the other once the overshoot of the trained
  budget is matched.
- **Why added:** It studies weight-tied looped models that apply one block repeatedly within
  a forward pass, and explains when that reused depth extrapolates.

### Do Halting Heads Really Save Compute in Looped Language Models?
[arXiv:2610.32246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32246) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** Pretraining looped language models with halting heads up to 1.3B parameters, the
paper finds that the reported savings mostly disappear: standard heads run nearly every
iteration, and batched decoding erases the early stops of the heads that work.

- **Setup:** Prelude-core-coda looped models at 140M, 370M, 770M and 1.3B, trained from
  scratch with a cap of 8 iterations on FineWeb-Edu and Common Corpus, against a
  sampled-depth baseline and fixed-depth specialists. Released Ouro models are also tested.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A PonderNet-style halting head gives a stop probability after each
  iteration. Heads trained on the probability-weighted mixture of states are compared with
  heads that score every iteration, and tokens stop once their cumulative halting
  probability crosses a threshold.
- **Results:** Mixture-loss heads predict 3.61-3.81 iterations, yet tokens run 7.6-7.9 of 8.
  Per-step heads stop tokens after about three iterations and beat the baseline at that depth
  by about 0.02 BPB, but a batched pass runs 4.4-4.7 iterations. At that depth the lead is a
  tie at 140M and 370M and 0.003 BPB at 770M. Ouro-2.6B's tokens skip about a tenth of their
  iterations, but a pass over 32 tokens skips about one percent.
- **Caveat:** Evaluation is in bits per byte only, the per-step model is trained only up to
  770M, and several cells use a single seed.
- **Why added:** Halting is adaptive depth control over one reused block, and the paper
  measures what it actually saves, with a test that bounds a head's gain before training it.

### Accordion: Elastic-Depth Inference via Fixed-Prefix Layer Looping
[arXiv:2610.32492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32492) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** Accordion is a 6B-parameter MoE language model that serves five depth budgets from
one set of weights by running a growing prefix of its middle layers twice, and its deepest
budget scores 3.01 points higher across 37 tasks.

- **Setup:** Accordion-6B-A0.5B (24 layers, 128 routed experts) is pretrained on 3.6T tokens,
  sampling one budget per micro-batch with plain next-token loss. Supervised fine-tuning and
  reinforcement learning keep all five budgets, and three domain experts are merged and
  distilled into one model.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Only layers 7-18 can loop. Budget k (0, 3, 6, 9 or 12) runs each of
  the first k layers of that window twice in succession with the same weights. Small
  zero-initialised second-pass gates are the only added parameters, and the expert router is
  shared across passes.
- **Results:** Raising the budget from 0 to 12 lifts the 37-task average from 47.05 to 50.06
  and improves 36 of 37 tasks. Budget 6 keeps 89% of that gain for about 21-24% more time
  per output token. At 1.61B scale the shared model stays within 0.003 nats of separately
  trained budget-specific models under equal training tokens.
- **Caveat:** Each configuration is trained once, the comparison with budget-specific models
  is only at small scale, and the second pass keeps its own KV cache (1.5 times the memory at
  budget 12).
- **Why added:** The same learned layers are executed twice within one forward pass, with
  the loop count sampled in training and chosen per request at inference.

## Near misses

- **Continuous-depth Deep Gaussian Processes** ([2610.30890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30890)):
  models depth as a stochastic differential equation with a Gaussian-process prior over the
  drift; there is no reused learned neural layer or block.
- **J-Comm** ([2610.32871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32871)): passes hidden states and
  Jacobian cues between two language-model agents; no layer is reused within a forward pass.
- **RSI-Opt** ([2610.32026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32026)): an agent loop that iteratively
  improves model-training code, which is out of scope.
