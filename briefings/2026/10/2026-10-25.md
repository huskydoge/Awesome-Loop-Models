# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-10-25

## Outcome

Two papers were added. One applies looping where a vision-language model first sees the
image: it re-runs part of the vision encoder, conditioned on the question, and learns when to
stop. The other is an analysis paper: it asks why looped and depth-grown language models seem
better at learning from context, and traces the answer to how their induction heads form.

Neither addition cites Benhao Huang's work.

## Added

### Loop to Look Better: Learning Adaptive Depth for Question-Guided Perception in Vision-Language Models
[arXiv:2610.30824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30824) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** LoopLook re-applies a middle-to-late window of a pretrained VLM's vision encoder,
guided by the question and stopped by a learned exit head, and improves all four backbones
it was tested on for a 5.1–6.5% increase in time to first token.

- **Setup:** Four backbones (Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Qwen3-VL-4B, InternVL3.5-4B, LLaVA-1.5-7B),
  fine-tuned for one epoch on 100K Visual CoT samples with the language model frozen. Only
  LoRA on the vision tower and the new modules are trained (10–15M parameters, 0.16–0.40% of
  each model). Evaluation covers eight benchmarks.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A window of encoder layers chosen from layer-wise CKA structure
  (layers 17–30 on Qwen2.5-VL-3B) runs up to four times with shared weights. At each loop a
  shared modulation network turns the pooled question, the pooled visual state and a
  loop-step embedding into scale and shift terms. A sigmoid exit head decides when to stop,
  and a token-wise Mixture-of-Looks router fuses the states from all loops.
- **Results:** On Qwen2.5-VL-3B, MMStar rises from 55.9 to 64.5, HallusionBench from 54.3 to
  62.4 and the eight-benchmark average from 59.3 to 63.9. The averages for the other
  backbones rise from 63.3 to 65.6, 62.1 to 65.3 and 45.2 to 48.5. On a three-benchmark
  average, LoopLook scores 68.5 with 1.89 loops on average, against 61.5 for a
  parameter-matched single-pass fine-tune and 63.7 for one trained with 1.5 times the steps.
- **Caveat:** The ablations use one backbone and three benchmarks, with no reported seeds.
  A fixed four loops scores slightly higher than adaptive exit (69.1 against 68.5), so the
  learned stop saves compute rather than adding accuracy. BabyVision drops on Qwen3-VL-4B
  (17.8 to 14.4).
- **Why added:** The same learned vision-encoder layers are re-applied several times within
  one forward pass, with learned halting over the reused window.

### How Depth Growth and Looping Shape In-Context Learning in Language Models
[arXiv:2610.31421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31421) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** Looped and depth-grown language models rely more on context than dense models
with the same inference FLOPs, learning in context earlier and leaning harder on induction
heads that spread across their repeated layers.

- **Setup:** Dense, depth-grown (MIDAS and LIDAS) and looped language models at 360M, 1.7B
  and 3B, trained on the same data in the same order with matched final inference FLOPs and
  effective depth. The looped models use a prelude, a shared block applied a fixed number of
  times, and a coda; the 3B model runs 36 layer applications with 28 unique layers. Controlled
  tasks (RegBench formal languages and Omniglot few-shot) complement the language models.
- **Mechanism / Method:** The paper measures how the per-position loss falls with context,
  few-shot GSM8K, and in-context versus in-weights learning on Omniglot. It traces induction
  heads through training and ablates induction-ranked and function-vector heads.
- **Results:** In the Omniglot tasks, looped and grown models learn in context earlier and keep
  that ability when fixed labels could also be memorized, with the looped model memorizing
  far less. In LIDAS, 10 of the 12 strongest final induction heads trace back to two early
  heads. Removing induction-ranked heads hurts few-shot performance more in grown and looped
  models than in dense ones. On the nine-task multiple-choice average the looped model scores
  54.21, 63.25 and 60.53 at the three sizes, against 54.69, 62.97 and 61.33 for dense.
- **Caveat:** Each language-model arm has a single training seed, and unique parameters and
  training compute are not matched across arms. The looped model has higher absolute loss than
  dense at 3B.
- **Why added:** It is a mechanistic study of weight-tied looped language models, in which one
  block is reused within each forward pass, compared directly with untied dense models.

## Near misses

- **Parameters efficient language model with latent cross-attention for IoT intrusion
  detection** ([2610.30222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30222)): compares a weight-shared
  Transformer block with stacked layers at depths 2 and 4, but the final model uses a single
  layer, so the reuse is a side comparison rather than the method.
- **OneLatent** ([2610.30675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30675)): compresses chain-of-thought
  into learnable latent tokens for a recommendation model; no layer or block is reused within
  a forward pass.
- **Graph-Recurrent Learning of Emergent Collective Dynamics**
  ([2610.31350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31350)): a gated recurrent graph network that
  carries a hidden state across time steps, which is ordinary sequence recurrence.
- **ToM2Vec** ([2610.29934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29934)): generates continuous
  theory-of-mind tokens autoregressively; there is no internal reused block.
- **baseVultra** ([2610.30154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30154)): a new gated RNN cell for
  streaming sequence modeling, which is ordinary sequence recurrence.
