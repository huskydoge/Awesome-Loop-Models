# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-10-27

## Outcome

Nine papers were added. Five are about making looped language models cheaper to run, train
or serve: running later loops in parallel, reinforcement learning with exact log-probabilities,
and two different ways to shrink the key-value cache across loops. One adds a gated state
that persists across loops, one patches attention queries between loops of Ouro to see how
later loops repair two-hop retrieval, and one retrofits a pretrained model to run both a
single pass and a looped pass. The last two bring looped blocks to CLIP and to generative
recommendation.

Two additions cite Benhao Huang's work, both as related work only. *LSTM-Style Looped
Transformers with Shared KV Caches* contrasts its shared donor cache with the terminal KV reuse
studied in "Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II: Rethinking at Fixed Points". *Cross-Loop
Query Handoff* lists the same report among methods that share the KV cache across loops.

## Added

### LoopFold: Parallelizing Recurrent Depth in Looped Transformers
[arXiv:2610.35556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35556) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** LoopFold predicts the inputs of later loops so that several loop depths run as one
parallel batch, and its accuracy keeps rising with depth while latency falls.

- **Setup:** 1.4B LLaMA-style looped models trained from scratch on 30B Pile tokens, with
  410M ablations, scored on Pile perplexity and eight zero-shot tasks against vanilla looped
  models, Parallel Loop Transformers (PLT) and Hidden Decoding.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A shared backbone is applied repeatedly. A lightweight linear
  predictor extrapolates the first loop's update to give provisional inputs to later loops,
  several depths are evaluated in one batched backbone call, and a corrector adjusts each later
  loop once the earlier results arrive. Everything trains on the final next-token loss.
- **Results:** Average zero-shot accuracy rises from 53.41% at depth 5 to 56.38% at depth 9.
  Against a nine-loop vanilla model it gains 2.20 points with 46% lower decoding latency, and
  at matched latency it scores 56.38 against 53.69 for a five-loop vanilla model.
- **Caveat:** It spends extra parallel FLOPs and key-value storage, the largest model is 1.4B,
  and there is no untied baseline.
- **Why added:** It applies one shared backbone repeatedly per token and changes how those
  loop iterations are scheduled.

### StateLoop: Persistent State for Looped Transformers
[arXiv:2610.35544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35544) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** StateLoop gives each token an extra gated state that persists across loop
iterations, which lowers perplexity more the deeper the model loops.

- **Setup:** 1.4B looped models (24 layers, four loops) trained on 30B Pile tokens and 410M
  models with three to seven loops, compared with vanilla looped models, a parameter-matched
  vanilla variant, Hyperloop, MeSH, PonderLM, Parcae and pause tokens.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A selective-SSM-style gate decides how much of the previous state
  to keep and how much of the backbone output to write; the state is projected back and added
  to the backbone output for the next iteration and the final readout. All modules are shared
  across iterations, with optional attention or Mamba-2 mixing along the token axis.
- **Results:** Pile perplexity falls from 7.79 to 7.61 and average zero-shot accuracy rises
  from 53.31 to 54.28 (54.75 with a Mamba-2 mixer). The perplexity gain grows from about 0.14
  at three loops to 0.30 at seven, for 1.42% extra latency.
- **Caveat:** The gains are about one point at 1.4B, and the depth study is only at 410M.
- **Why added:** It is a design for the state carried across iterations of a weight-shared
  backbone.

### Recursive CLIP: Vision-Language Distillation with Looped Transformer Blocks
[arXiv:2610.34563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34563) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** CLIP encoders built from a few transformer blocks applied repeatedly keep much of
CLIP's zero-shot and transfer ability, especially when trained by distillation.

- **Setup:** ViT-B/16-depth CLIP models made of N distinct blocks applied M times (N·M = 12),
  either in the vision encoder only (rCLIP) or in both encoders (r²CLIP), trained on CC3M and
  CC12M with plain contrastive loss or distillation from a DFN-2B ViT-B/16 teacher.
- **Mechanism / Method:** The block stack is reused M times with no iteration embeddings, at
  the same compute as the untied ViT-B. Output-level distillation losses are used, since
  matching intermediate activations does not suit recursive students.
- **Results:** One block looped 12 times has 71.65M parameters against 149.62M and reaches
  56.7% ImageNet zero-shot after distillation (68.8% with six blocks looped twice), against
  72.5% for the distilled untied ViT-B. Activation matching keeps only 50% of reference
  accuracy, against 76-95% for output-level objectives.
- **Caveat:** Most results come from one training run, and heavy sharing still loses a lot on
  fine-grained tasks such as aircraft classification.
- **Why added:** The same transformer blocks are applied several times within one forward pass,
  compared with an untied model at equal depth and FLOPs.

### ScaleRLT: Exact Log-Probabilities and Fast Deferred Rollouts for Reinforcement Learning on Looped Transformers
[arXiv:2610.34427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34427) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** ScaleRLT makes on-policy RL on a looped language model both faster and exact by
batching deferred deep loops in rollouts and replaying that same schedule for the learner's
log-probabilities.

- **Setup:** GRPO on GSM8K with Ouro-1.4B (24 layers applied in four loops), on L40S, GH200
  and B300 GPUs from one GPU to four nodes, in FP32 and bf16.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Rollouts read most tokens after two loops and run the deeper loops
  in deferred batches. The learner's log-probability takes its value from a no-grad replay of
  the rollout's own schedule and its gradient from the dense pass, so the two stay identical.
- **Results:** A dense recomputation misses a 1e-5 match on about 18% of tokens at 4096 tokens
  in FP32, while the replay matches bit for bit in all 167 FP32 and 28 bf16 cells. Deferral
  raises rollout throughput 1.47-1.56x, and a two-node update drops from 195.6 s to 72.2 s.
- **Caveat:** The mixed-depth readout that deferral needs trails the full four-loop readout by
  7.6-9 points on GSM8K, and each policy is trained with one seed.
- **Why added:** It addresses post-training and loop-level scheduling for a model that reuses
  one block of layers several times per token.

### Cross-Loop Query Handoff: How Later Loops Redirect Two-Hop Retrieval in the Ouro Looped Language Models
[arXiv:2610.34639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34639) · Code: [Muuzzziiy/cql-handoff](https://github.com/Muuzzziiy/cql-handoff)

**TL;DR:** Swapping attention queries from a later loop into the first loop of Ouro is enough
to fix most two-hop questions that only later loops got right.

- **Setup:** Ouro-1.4B and Ouro-2.6B (four loops of shared weights) on synthetic in-context
  two-hop scenes and on 2WikiMultiHopQA and MuSiQue, with dense models as reference points.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Queries at the same layer, head and final prompt position are
  transplanted from loop 3 into loop 1, with crossed donor swaps, key and value transplants and
  query freezes as controls.
- **Results:** Loop-3 queries rescue 89.8% and 96.9% of the target questions, against 23.7%
  and 20.6% for shuffled donors. Late keys and values rescue only 26.2% and 33.2%. On natural
  questions the rescue gain is 14.2 points in Ouro-2.6B and not significant in Ouro-1.4B.
- **Caveat:** Only two models from one family are studied, the strongest evidence is on
  synthetic scenes, and the authors note that their dense controls do not isolate looping.
- **Why added:** It is a mechanistic study of how a weight-shared block composes information
  across its loop iterations.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** lists "Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II" (arXiv:2610.06833)
  in related work, among methods that share the KV cache across loops.

### Prefix Keys and Values Matter Late: RIPE Caching for Self-Speculative Decoding in Looped Language Models
[arXiv:2610.34436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34436) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** Only the late loops of a recurrent-depth model need accurate prefix keys and values,
so a cache that stores mostly the deepest loop keeps self-speculative decoding accurate at a
fraction of the memory.

- **Setup:** Huginn-0125 and Raven-Llama (prelude, a core block run 32 times, coda) on GSM8K
  and AlpacaEval on one GH200, in nine pre-registered batches.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Perturbation tests show which loops' prefix keys and values matter,
  and the per-loop planes are seen to converge toward the last one. RIPE stores the deepest
  plane for old positions and every plane for a recent window, which also makes batched
  verification independent of the draft block.
- **Results:** Loops 25-32 are e^15.0 (Huginn) and e^12.8 (Raven) times more sensitive than
  loops 2-9. On Huginn a 64-position window removes 89% of the high-error tail positions with
  11.9% of the full cache's memory. Self-speculation reaches 2.09x with the full cache and
  2.58x with the deepest plane only.
- **Caveat:** RIPE with a recent window was checked by teacher-forced replay, not inside the
  speculative decoder, and the tail statistics rest on four prompts.
- **Why added:** It is a KV-cache design for models that reuse one core block many times per
  token.

### LoopGR: Parameter-Efficient Scaling for Generative Recommendation via Recurrent Depth
[arXiv:2610.34157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34157) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** LoopGR reruns a shared decoder and a light context updater over several cycles
for each generated item, matching or beating larger generative recommenders with up to 52%
fewer parameters.

- **Setup:** Small T5-style generative recommenders over semantic IDs on Amazon Beauty, Sports
  and CDs and a Tencent industrial dataset, against sequential and generative baselines such as
  SASRec, TIGER, HSTU and RPG.
- **Mechanism / Method:** At each generation step the same decoder runs for several high-level
  cycles, and between them a shared updater refines the encoder context with cycle embeddings.
  Recurrent trajectory self-distillation then trains a low-budget student from the high-budget
  teacher.
- **Results:** On Beauty, recall@5 is 0.0563 against 0.0550 for RPG, and on the industrial
  set 0.5691 against 0.5477 for BARGE. At about 0.5 GFLOPs it uses 1.3M parameters against
  2.7M for an encoder-decoder baseline, and cutting 16 cycles to 4 takes latency from 211 ms to
  50 ms.
- **Caveat:** The models are tiny, the margins are small, and no seed variance is reported.
- **Why added:** A shared decoder block and updater are reused within the computation of each
  output step, not just across autoregressive steps.

### One Model, Two Paths: Balancing Single-Pass and Looped Performance in Language Models
[arXiv:2610.33517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33517) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** When a pretrained model is adapted to also run its middle blocks twice, training
only the looped path damages the single pass, and weighting both losses fixes most of that.

- **Setup:** Two 134M-parameter, 8-block Llama-style models continued on FineWeb-Edu under
  fixed block-application and wall-clock budgets, with ARC-Easy, PIQA and HellaSwag.
- **Mechanism / Method:** The looped path runs two prelude blocks, the middle four blocks twice
  and two coda blocks, with all weights shared with the single pass. Joint cross-entropy weights
  on both outputs are tuned, plus a distillation term from the frozen original model.
- **Results:** Loop-only training improves looped NLL by 0.009 over ordinary continued
  training but worsens single-pass NLL by 0.171; a 5% single-pass weight cuts that gap to
  0.036. The frozen-original variant meets all four validation criteria, against none for
  plain joint CE.
- **Caveat:** Results are at 134M scale on English web text, do not hold on WikiText, and
  task gains are inconsistent.
- **Why added:** It retrofits a pretrained model to reuse its middle blocks within one forward
  pass and studies the cost to the original path.

### LSTM-Style Looped Transformers with Shared KV Caches
[arXiv:2610.33360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33360) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** A looped transformer with an LSTM-style gated carry and one shared key-value cache
for its whole core nearly matches an untied model at small token budgets with 40% fewer
parameters and far fewer caches.

- **Setup:** modded-nanogpt-style models with a core of four blocks applied three times, at
  widths 384-1024 on 1.05-4.19B FineWeb tokens, each against a 15-layer untied control.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A gated carry runs across iterations of the core, and every core
  block and iteration reads one donor key-value cache, cutting persistent cache sets from 15 to
  4. A late consistency loss aligns the cache seen in parallel training with the one built
  during continuation.
- **Results:** At width 1024 it uses 40.4% fewer parameters at nearly matched FLOPs and comes
  within 0.0002 nats of the untied control at 1.05B tokens. The consistency loss removes
  81-89% of the continuation penalty across three widths, and filled-cache peak memory is
  2.77-2.87x lower.
- **Caveat:** Untied models win at matched FLOPs as tokens grow, most runs use one seed, and
  shared-cache decoding is slower.
- **Why added:** It reuses a core of transformer blocks within one forward pass and compares
  that against untied models at matched FLOPs and parameters.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** cites "Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II: Rethinking at
  Fixed Points" as concurrent related work on terminal KV reuse across iterations, to contrast
  its own donor cache shared across distinct core blocks.
