# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-10-18

## Outcome

Four papers were added. Each reuses a learned block inside one forward pass. One speeds up
decoding for existing looped language models. One retrofits depth recurrence into a
pretrained vision-language model. One turns natively looped LMs into multiple-choice
decision models with per-question depth. One loops frozen vision-encoder blocks so that
visual processing depends on the question.
[AdaLoop: Thinking with the Vision Encoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21323) cites Benhao
Huang's blog Towards Looped Models Done Right (Part I) in its related work, as a study of
architectural design choices for looped models.

## Added

### LoopAhead: Speculating Ahead for Faster Decoding in Looped Language Models

[arXiv:2610.21594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21594) · Code: none stated

**TL;DR:** A small drafter reads early-loop states of a frozen looped LM to propose several future tokens, and intermediate loops decide which draft branches to keep running; decoding stays lossless and is up to 7.82x faster than autoregressive decoding.

- **Setup:** frozen Ouro-1.4B, Ouro-2.6B and Ouro-1.4B-Thinking (four recurrences) and three Raven retrofits (32 recurrences over a shared core), on GSM8K, MATH500, HumanEval, MBPP and BBH. Baselines are full-depth autoregressive decoding, LoopSpec and an adapted DFlash.
- **Mechanism / Method:**
  - A two-layer lookahead drafter predicts several future tokens from early-loop hidden states and a native anchor proposal.
  - Candidate nodes advance through recurrent depths asynchronously in the same batch.
  - Disagreement at intermediate loops suspends or resumes branches while keeping their KV states.
  - Full-depth verification makes the final token decisions.
- **Results:**
  - Up to 7.82x speedup (Raven-TinyLlama, HumanEval, greedy).
  - Faster than LoopSpec and DFlash on all 30 greedy model–benchmark pairs; on Ouro-2.6B GSM8K, 5.21x vs 3.19x and 2.24x.
  - Under sampling it is fastest on all 10 pairs, with 16.2–36.8 percent more throughput than LoopSpec.
  - The drafter adds 11.1M parameters on Ouro, against 277.9M for DFlash.
- **Caveat:** each target model needs its own trained drafter and scheduling choices. Gains over the training-free LoopSpec are modest on some pairs. Evaluated one request per GPU, not under concurrent serving.
- **Why added:** an inference-systems method that pipelines loop iterations of weight-tied looped LMs.

### Retrofitting Recurrent Vision-Language Models from Pretrained Backbones

[arXiv:2610.22075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22075) · Code: none stated (release promised) · [Project page](https://madhavesdios.github.io/recurrent-vlms)

**TL;DR:** Pruning TinyLLaVA's language backbone and looping a block of the remaining layers gives a VLM that beats the unpruned model on nine benchmarks while storing 23.3 percent fewer language parameters.

- **Setup:** TinyLLaVA-1.4B (TinyLlama-1.1B with CLIP ViT-L/14). Layers 4–9 are removed and the remaining 16 split into a 4-layer prelude, an 8-layer recurrent core and a 4-layer coda. Recovery uses existing data only: 60B text tokens, projector alignment, then visual instruction tuning.
- **Mechanism / Method:** at each loop step a shared linear adapter merges the core's state with the prelude output and projects it back into the block. The text-only and instruction-tuning stages distill from the original non-recurrent VLM.
- **Results:**
  - Nine-benchmark average 58.13, against 55.80 for the unpruned model trained with all three recovery stages, 52.91 for the pretrained model and 52.41 for the pruned single-pass model at equal parameters.
  - It also beats a recurrent LM converted into a VLM on the same data (55.59).
  - Gains are concentrated on reasoning benchmarks (CV-Bench-2D 53.9 vs 42.2, RealWorldQA 50.5 vs 46.4); basic VQA is on par.
  - The language model plus adapter has 844.4M parameters against 1,100.3M.
- **Caveat:** one small backbone and one seed. The fixed-depth model only works at its trained depth, and depth-sampled training shows no test-time scaling. Each token traverses 56 layers against 22, so compute and KV memory go up.
- **Why added:** retrofits a prelude–core–coda loop into a pretrained VLM and compares it with the untied model and a parameter-matched pruned model.

### LEV: Thinking Fast and Deep with Looped Decision Models

[arXiv:2610.20951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20951) · Code: [OpenLAIR/lev](https://github.com/OpenLAIR/lev) · [Models](https://huggingface.co/collections/songdj/lev) · [Project page](https://openlair.github.io/lev/)

**TL;DR:** LEV answers multiple-choice questions in one forward pass of a natively looped LM, reading the options after every loop step; accuracy climbs with depth, mostly on hard questions, and a small stopper picks the depth per question.

- **Setup:** LoRA fine-tunes of Ouro-1.4B, Ouro-2.6B and Nanbeige4.2-3B on 31,329 records. Dense controls are Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3.5-2B and Qwen3.5-4B trained with the same data and recipe. Evaluation uses held-out panels, a new LevBench and 231 public JevBench items.
- **Mechanism / Method:** the same decoder stack runs up to four times. A shared pointer head scores the options after each step, and all steps are trained jointly. A per-depth logistic stopper predicts whether the current answer matches the deepest one, with its threshold fitted on unlabelled requests.
- **Results:**
  - LEV-2.6B on JevBench: 63.2 percent at depth 1, 71.0 at depth 2, 75.3 at depth 3 and 76.2 at depth 4.
  - On-demand depth saves 15–24 percent of computation for under one point (locked test, LEV-2.6B: 63.7 vs 64.6 at 0.78 of the cost).
  - Below 50 ms, the 1.4B and 3B models are 5.4–8.8 points more accurate than dense models and cascades of the same latency.
- **Caveat:** the advantage is in latency, not operations. The paper says each loop step runs the whole backbone. The gain over a fixed mix of depths is small (+0.2 to +1.0 points). A loop retrofitted into a dense Qwen model barely changes accuracy.
- **Why added:** adaptive halting over a whole-stack weight-tied loop, with accuracy scaling with test-time depth.

### AdaLoop: Thinking with the Vision Encoder

[arXiv:2610.21323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21323) · Code: none stated

**TL;DR:** AdaLoop re-applies a span of frozen vision-encoder blocks to an evolving visual state, modulated by the question, and beats parameter-matched single-pass baselines on procedural visual-reasoning tasks across three VLM families.

- **Setup:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B, PaliGemma 2 3B and a re-trained LLaVA-1.5-7B, trained on the new procedural VisRO-Core6 suite (Count, Flow, Glyph, Metro, Maze, Path) and a LLaVA-NeXT subset. Baselines are SFT, a single-pass AdaLN model with the same trainable parameters, SFT+GRPO and LIVR.
- **Mechanism / Method:**
  - The encoder is split into a prelude that runs once, a loop body of consecutive frozen blocks, and a coda that produces visual tokens.
  - Question-conditioned AdaLN shift, scale and gate modulate the loop body. The modulation is the same at every iteration, so extra depth adds no parameters.
  - Training applies deep supervision at every depth (trained depth 3). Inference can stop once two consecutive answers agree.
- **Results:**
  - In-distribution accuracy for SFT, single-pass AdaLN and AdaLoop: 49.9, 60.0 and 64.6 on Qwen2.5-VL-3B; 62.6, 72.4 and 75.2 on PaliGemma 2; 50.4, 57.7 and 61.8 on LLaVA-1.5.
  - Out-of-distribution gains follow the same pattern (Qwen: 32.4 vs 30.2).
  - Looping without modulation helps little (49.9 to 50.9), while question-conditioned modulation gives the full gain.
- **Caveat:** mostly synthetic tasks, with mixed changes on general VQA. Matching is by trainable parameters and optimizer updates, not inference FLOPs. Headline adaptive-depth numbers apply the stopping rule offline to fixed-depth runs.
- **Why added:** a shared, frozen block span reused within one visual forward pass, applied to multimodal reasoning.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** yes. It cites Towards Looped Models Done Right (Part I) in related work as a study of architectural design choices for looped models.

## Near misses

- [Trading Depth for Time in Recurrent Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21605)
  (revised v2) adds compute by inserting latent thought tokens that pass through the full
  backbone between vocabulary tokens; this is temporal recurrence rather than a reused
  internal block. Two-loop transformers appear only as a baseline.
- [Depth Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18809) changes how untied layers aggregate
  representations across depth; no layer is reused.
- [Phase Kernel Lifts Capacity of Dense Associative Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21493)
  studies energy-descent retrieval in Hopfield networks, not a looped learned block.
- [Does Latent Chain-of-Thought Actually Reason?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21182) analyses
  COCONUT-style continuous chains that feed hidden states back through the full model.
- [CoTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20642) mixes soft and discrete token embeddings during
  autoregressive decoding, without training and without a reused internal block.
- [Rationale Distribution Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19871) generates latent
  rationales with a flow model for a frozen LM to decode from.
