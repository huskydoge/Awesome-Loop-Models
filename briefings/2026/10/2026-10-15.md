# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-10-15

## Outcome

Five papers were added. Each reuses a learned block inside one forward pass. One
explains why looped transformers lose correct answers when run longer than they were
trained. One reallocates compute between the two visits of a looped mixture-of-experts.
One applies a looped attention layer to forecasting dynamical systems. One trains a
timestep-free recurrent reasoner with a diffusion curriculum, and one analyses the
fixed-point dynamics of the Continuous Thought Machine's internal ticks.
[Diffusion as a Training Curriculum for Timestep-Free Iterative Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01449)
cites Benhao Huang's Equilibrium Reasoners (EqR) as related recursive-reasoning work and
as the source of its Maze-Unique and Maze-Hard benchmarks.

## Added

### Learning to Compute Is Not Learning to Retain or Reuse in Looped Transformers

[arXiv:2610.19768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19768) · Code: none stated

**TL;DR:** Looped transformers run past their trained depth often lose answers they had already computed correctly. Supervising a random position per example, instead of always the last one, fixes this.

- **Setup:** a single shared two-layer transformer block (width 128) is applied with additive input injection, one loop per input symbol, to execute Mealy machines given in context. A simulator supplies intermediate targets and exact counterfactuals. One released recurrent maze network is also tested.
- **Mechanism / Method:** the paper compares training objectives (final-only, all-prefix, sampled-prefix, fixed vs randomized exit, progressive training with truncated gradients) and uses hidden-state swaps, resets and freezing to tell failed computation apart from lost retention or a state that cannot be reused.
- **Results:**
  - Final-only models are 100 percent correct at loop 32 but keep 60.8 percent of answers at loop 33 and 22.3 percent at loop 64.
  - Sampled-prefix supervision keeps 99.8 percent at loop 64.
  - With the same expected number of loops, randomized exit (Jitter-8) keeps 85.6 percent at loop 64 against 30.0 percent for a fixed exit (Slack-4).
  - Truncated-gradient progressive training learns the 32-step task in only 1 of 3 seeds.
  - Swapping one hidden vector redirects all 237 of 237 eligible later positions to the exact counterfactual.
  - Freezing positions as soon as their answer appears drops accuracy to about 4–6 percent; a two-loop delay restores 99.8 percent.
- **Caveat:** one synthetic task family and one small architecture; budgets are matched in expected loops, not total FLOPs.
- **Why added:** a mechanistic study of a fully weight-tied looped transformer and of how its training objective affects extrapolation past the trained depth.

### LatentLoop: Scaling Looped MoE Models with Asymmetric Compute Allocation

[arXiv:2610.19975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19975) · Code: [lcqysl/LatentLoop](https://github.com/lcqysl/LatentLoop) · [Project page](https://latentloop-project.github.io/)

**TL;DR:** The first visit of a shared looped block matters less than the second, so LatentLoop runs it on a 2x-compressed sequence and spends the saved compute on width; it beats both looped and non-looped MoE baselines at every matched budget tested.

- **Setup:** Qwen3-style sparse MoE models at 1.3B, 3B and 7B total parameters, trained on FineWeb-Edu across 15 budget configurations. Baselines are a non-looped MoE and a looped MoE whose middle half of layers is visited twice with all parameters shared.
- **Mechanism / Method:**
  - Learned per-visit residual coefficients settle near 0.5 for the first visit and 1 for the second.
  - LatentLoop mean-pools adjacent tokens for the first visit, keeps full resolution for the second, and writes the first visit's update back through learned per-channel coefficients.
  - Routers and experts are shared across visits; attention parameters are separate for each visit.
- **Results:**
  - Lowest validation loss at all 15 budgets: at 7B/140B tokens, 1.8482 vs 1.8546 for the looped baseline and 1.8640 for the non-looped one.
  - The fitted scaling curves predict 19.0 percent compute savings against the non-looped MoE and 7.9 percent against the looped MoE at equal loss.
  - The 7B five-shot average over nine benchmarks is 63.40 vs 62.55 for the looped baseline and 62.19 for the non-looped one.
  - GPU time is 9–13 percent lower than the looped baseline.
- **Caveat:** gains over the looped baseline are small at larger scales (about 0.006–0.01 NLL); results come from a single seed.
- **Why added:** a sharing and compute-allocation design for looped MoE models, compared against untied models at matched parameters and FLOPs.

### Test-Time Forecasting: Recurrent Depth Enables In-Context Dynamics Prediction

[arXiv:2610.19552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19552) · Code: none stated (supplementary material only)

**TL;DR:** Repeating one learned linear-attention layer performs gradient descent on a dynamic-mode-decomposition objective. Small looped forecasters trained only for one-step prediction then generalize to unseen ODE and PDE dynamics.

- **Setup:** small models trained on affine linear systems, the forced Duffing oscillator, advection–diffusion, KdV and Kuramoto–Sivashinsky, with in-distribution and out-of-distribution splits along a difficulty parameter.
- **Mechanism / Method:** the implicit variant (iTTF) is an encoder, a single weight-tied linear-attention layer with short convolutions looped many times, and a decoder. Loop counts are sampled during training. An explicit variant (eTTF) instead runs inner gradient steps on fast weights.
- **Results:**
  - On linear systems, spectral error falls steadily as loops increase.
  - On the Duffing oscillator, iTTF stays in one basin at 1 loop but covers the chaotic attractor at 32 loops.
  - A nonlinear encoder and decoder cut spectral error almost fourfold at matched nRMSE compared with a test-time regression baseline.
- **Caveat:** synthetic low-dimensional systems with very small models. Most results are reported only in figures. The eTTF variant is test-time optimization rather than a shared learned block.
- **Why added:** an expressivity argument for what a single looped attention layer computes, applied to scientific forecasting.

### Diffusion as a Training Curriculum for Timestep-Free Iterative Reasoning

[arXiv:2609.01449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01449) · Code: none stated

**TL;DR:** Adding a persistent hidden state to a timestep-free denoiser turns it into an anytime recurrent solver that reaches 99.90 percent exact solve on Sudoku-Extreme; diffusion matters as a training curriculum, not as an inference sampler.

- **Setup:** Sudoku-Extreme, Maze-Unique and Maze-Hard. The update is a looped weight-shared Transformer block (Sudoku) or a looped local convolutional block (mazes), applied inside each step of an outer recurrence that carries a hidden state.
- **Mechanism / Method:** the network gets neither the diffusion timestep nor the iteration index, so one shared update can run to any depth. Training uses persistent rollouts with re-corrupted targets and truncated backpropagation; inference stops once the decoded output stays unchanged.
- **Results:** 99.90 percent exact solve on Sudoku-Extreme and 98.93 percent on Maze-Unique, with accuracy rising well past the trained rollout length. Holding noise at its maximum during inference still solves nearly perfectly, while annealed corruption remains necessary during training.
- **Caveat:** a workshop paper whose outer loop is framed as denoising; it was a near-miss on 2026-09-02, and v2 makes the inner looped block explicit. No comparison against untied models.
- **Why added:** a shared looped block runs inside each forward step of a TRM-like recurrent reasoner; added after maintainer review.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** yes. It lists Equilibrium Reasoners among recursive reasoning models that keep a hidden state and uses EqR's Maze-Unique and Maze-Hard benchmarks.

### Riding the Fixed-Point Curve: Image Classification in the Continuous Thought Machine

[arXiv:2610.19220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19220) · Code: none stated

**TL;DR:** Each internal tick of an ImageNet Continuous Thought Machine is an attracting but strongly non-normal map, and its thought trajectory follows a moving fixed point one tick behind.

- **Setup:** the released ImageNet CTM checkpoint, without retraining. A ResNet backbone runs once; the same learned synapse model, neuron-level models, query and readout maps are then reused for a fixed number of ticks.
- **Mechanism / Method:** with the synchronization memory held fixed, each tick becomes an ordinary map on state space, whose Jacobian spectra, fixed points and attention schedule are analysed and compared against untrained and random-core controls.
- **Results:** every tick map has spectral radius below 1 but strong non-normality (balanced amplification); attraction exists at initialization and training increases non-normality. Shuffling queries across images at every tick costs only 7 percent of accuracy, and image-independent increments predict the next query with held-out R^2 = 0.98.
- **Caveat:** one checkpoint, classification only, non-archival workshop extended abstract.
- **Why added:** a mechanistic analysis of a weight-tied internal recurrence; added after maintainer review as the catalog's first Continuous Thought Machine entry.
