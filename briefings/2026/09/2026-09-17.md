# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-09-17

## Outcome

One paper is now included in today's briefing. It compares tied recurrent growth,
untied growth, and boundary operators under separately tuned compute-optimal recipes.

## Added

### Model growth and scaling exponents (2609.19107)

The study compares a prelude-core-coda Transformer family. Its strict-scope arm,
Loop-Grow, repeatedly applies one shared core within a forward pass and increases that
tied loop count partway through training. It also studies an Untied-Grow control whose
added passes have distinct weights.

Why it belongs: the tied Loop-Grow arm directly tests how reusing one learned core
across recurrent depth changes compute-optimal scaling. The untied arm is an important
control, not itself the loop mechanism.

Reader takeaway: on the measured single-epoch ladders at 1e20 FLOPs, tied Loop-Grow
reaches a 1.36x compute multiplier over Vanilla, while Untied-Grow reaches 1.55x. In
the 100M-token, ten-epoch setting, increasing the loop count matches the best
weight-decay-tuned model-size scaling loss with 2.2x less compute. A 7.4B Untied-Grow
run at 1.23e21 FLOPs lands on the extrapolated scaling curve, where its controlled
comparison to Vanilla is 1.8x. The paper also notes a roughly 20x compute gap to an
estimated GPT-3 13B CORE reference, but the models use different data and evaluation
pipelines; that number is indicative, not a controlled gain from looping or model
growth.
