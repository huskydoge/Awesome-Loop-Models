# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-09-01

## Outcome

Two papers were added. Both make repeated learned computation inside one model forward
process explicit: one constructs an autonomously halting Universal Transformer for
symbolic circuits, and the other evaluates a shared-block recursive code model against
parameter- and depth-matched controls throughout training.

## Added

### Universal Transformers for Circuit Computations (`2608.31067`)

This paper builds a tiny Transformer iteration that performs one level of circuit
reduction, then reuses that same learned computation until the input expression is
fully reduced. A positional mechanism identifies the currently reducible subexpressions,
attention routes their operands, an MLP evaluates them, and an autonomous criterion
halts after exactly the circuit depth.

Why it was added: the model repeatedly applies one internal learned Transformer step,
making recurrent depth and adaptive halting central rather than incidental.

Reader takeaway: a model with only 280 learnable parameters for the Boolean task can
be trained on depths 1--2 and reach 100% accuracy on Boolean algebra, modular
arithmetic, and ListOps at out-of-distribution depths 3--30. The result is unusually
strong and interpretable, though it relies on fully parenthesized, highly structured
symbolic inputs.

### Evaluating Tiny Recursive Models Across Training for Code Generation (`2608.29376`)

The study compares a roughly 28M-parameter TRM-AR with two controls built from the same
Transformer layer: a two-layer parameter-matched model and a twenty-layer
depth-matched model. TRM-AR recursively reuses one two-layer block to reach effective
depth 20, and the comparison tracks both teacher-forced fit and free-running Python
generation across 40 epochs and three seeds.

Why it was added: the paper directly analyzes a shared internal block reused across
depth, with unusually transparent parameter-, depth-, and training-trajectory controls.

Reader takeaway: the recursive model recovers about 45% of the validation-loss gap and
57% of the generation-quality gap between the controls at equal parameters, but it is
roughly 175 times slower per step than the two-layer control. The depth-matched untied
Transformer is stronger at its validation optimum, so the observed advantage is
parameter efficiency and resistance to overfitting rather than greater capability.

## Near misses

- `2608.30072` gives a relevant theory of self-consistent representations refined by
  repeated applications of a shared block. It is useful watchlist material, but its
  main contribution is a broad statistical account of token prediction rather than a
  loop-model architecture or evaluation.
- `2608.29160` repeats selected frozen Transformer layers inside each flow-matching
  denoiser call and is mechanically in scope. It remains a near miss because the paper
  is centered on test-time image-generation refinement in a diffusion-adjacent domain,
  outside the collection's current core emphasis.
- `2608.31069` performs autoregressive reasoning rollouts in continuous embedding space;
  it does not establish recurrent-depth reuse of an internal learned block.
- `2608.30959` alternates separate Locator and Critic agents in an external refinement
  loop rather than reusing learned computation inside one model forward.
