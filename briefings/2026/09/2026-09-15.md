# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-09-15

## Outcome

Seven papers were added. Together they show recurrent depth operating as adaptive
token computation, controlled parameter sharing, fixed-point graph reasoning,
trajectory-supervised forecasting, spiking inference, and learned optimization.

## Added

### T-LoopFormer (2609.15160)

T-LoopFormer lets each token continue or exit a shared Transformer loop according
to its hidden state. Recursion-wise KV caches keep autoregressive decoding aligned
without recomputing tokens that have already exited.

Reader takeaway: at the reported 24x setting, it reaches 20.13/19.14 perplexity
and a ten-task average of 44.20, compared with 20.17/19.22 and 43.8 for the untied
24-layer baseline, at 0.197 versus 0.202 ms per token.

Resource: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15160), [code](https://github.com/YuMingQian1234/T-LoopFormer).

### Looped and stacked ECG encoders (2609.15498)

This controlled study compares one bViT block applied twelve times with twelve
independently parameterized ViT layers under the same ECG training protocol.

Reader takeaway: the looped encoder uses 8.9 times fewer parameters while matching
PTB-5 performance and improving the reported PTB-44 AUC from 0.8381 to 0.8472.

Resource: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15498).

### Recurrent GNNs with set aggregation (2609.15932)

The paper studies recurrent GNNs that repeatedly apply one equal-weight block until
stabilization. It gives effective compilers in both directions between a verifiable
network class and the Boolean closure of modal reachability and safety properties.

Reader takeaway: the result identifies the exact fixed-point logic supported by
finite-vocabulary stabilization without counting logic or an external halting signal.

Resource: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15932).

### FlowTSFM (2609.13640)

FlowTSFM turns encoder depth into a recurrent quantile-transport path: one Transformer
block is shared across steps and every intermediate state receives trajectory-aware
supervision.

Reader takeaway: the 38.8M-parameter model remains within 1.8--4.6% MASE of stronger
baselines while using about three times fewer parameters than 119.5M Chronos-2;
the authors note that objective and step-count ablations remain future work.

Resource: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13640).

### URCHIN (2609.13899)

URCHIN resolves each token by recirculating spikes through one shared horizontal
connectome. The same weights support parallel state-space training and event-driven
recurrent inference.

Reader takeaway: the model reports roughly 15--50 times less pretraining compute than
the compared GPT baselines, but its Strict-Small aggregate remains below those baselines;
the result is an efficiency-oriented reference rather than a broad accuracy win.

Resource: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13899).

### Finite-time separation in recurrent GNNs (2609.13920)

This theory paper analyzes a recurrent GNN that reuses one message-passing operator
with persistent Gaussian perturbations. It proves exact second-moment decompositions,
finite-horizon near-collision bounds, and almost-sure noncollision.

Reader takeaway: the guarantees clarify how persistent noise preserves node-level
separation at finite depth, although the controlled experiments use fixed sampled
weights rather than trained recurrent networks.

Resource: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13920).

### SONAR (2609.13688)

SONAR unrolls a sparse-view CT objective for K stages while reusing two learned
image-domain neural operators to correct measurement and pseudo-measurement residuals.

Reader takeaway: the paper reports a 1.87 dB PSNR gain at 62 views and a 7.63 dB
gain under zero-shot transfer to a 512 by 512 reconstruction grid over its strongest
comparators, with additional clinical photon-counting CT evaluation.

Resource: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13688).

## Near misses

- [Recurrent Dynamic Range Extension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13135) repeatedly
  invokes a complete image-to-image network rather than reusing an internal learned
  module within one forward process.
- [Discrete Beckmann Transport Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15903) iterates a
  learned map, but the recurrence belongs to the excluded diffusion/flow generation
  boundary.
- [Temporal Recurrence Favors Fewer Layers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12531) studies
  recurrence over streaming time rather than recurrent computation depth.
