# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-09-28

## Outcome

Eleven papers were added. Every one applies the same learned block, map, or
message-passing operator several times within one forward pass. Six are about looped
language models and their efficiency, two study fixed-point or equilibrium dynamics, and
three apply looped networks to ARC puzzles, reinforcement learning, combinatorial
optimization, and visual tracking.

## Looped language models

### Why looped models stop generalizing past their training depth

[Beyond the Training Horizon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33144) compares two ways of
training recurrence in looped Transformers on polynomial iteration, finite-state
composition, and knowledge-graph traversal. With attention analysis, decoding of
intermediate states, and causal interventions, it finds two mechanisms. One scheme
repeatedly updates a state at a fixed readout position. The other pushes intermediate
states forward across positions as an advancing frontier. Both break down at depths past
training, because the readout, the progress cues, or state propagation stop being
reliable, and self-correction is limited. In both models, recurrent states encode the
content and also whether that content is still usable for later steps.

### Loop Dropout for shared adapters

[Loop Dropout](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34218) finds that a LoRA adapter shared across
all loops of a looped language model is more effective at late loops. During training,
Loop Dropout randomly skips the adapter at some loops and rescales the remaining
applications to keep the expected update strength. This improves mathematical reasoning,
instruction tuning, and code generation. At inference, the adapter runs at every loop, so
the cost is unchanged.

### Depth-asynchronous self-speculative decoding

[Shallow Queries, Mature Values](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34538) speeds up
self-speculative decoding in looped Transformers by letting shallow drafts read the
full-depth keys and values already cached for the verified prefix. This is close to
the cross-depth KV-cache sharing used in recurrent-depth models such as Huginn. The
paper's added evidence is a channel ablation: on Huginn at draft depth 1, replacing only
the prefix values with their full-depth versions raises draft-target agreement from 47.2
to 66.9 percent, while replacing only the keys does not help. Combined with parallel
refinement of several candidate positions, the DAS-Wave decoder gives 4.00 to 6.96 times
mean throughput speedup over full-depth decoding across four recurrent-model checkpoints
on math and code workloads.

### Adaptive looping for test-time scaling

[Improving Test-Time Scaling with Adaptive Looped Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35748)
measures accuracy gained per doubling of decoding FLOPs. Existing looped models have
steeper slopes than their non-looped baselines but lose at matched compute, and many
tokens gain nothing from extra iterations. TaH2 post-trains the backbone together with an
iteration decider supervised by lookahead depth labels. On AIME it improves the slope by
53 percent over the non-looped baseline and beats that baseline's peak accuracy by about
3.4 points at matched compute ([code](https://github.com/thu-nics/TaH)).

### How to loop a mixture-of-experts model

[How to Loop MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35751) introduces Foil. It halves the number
of expert layers, doubles the experts per layer and the number of passes, and gives each
pass its own attention while the experts and routers stay shared. With expert parameters
and compute held fixed, Foil reaches lower pretraining loss than an unflattened looped
MoE baseline, and the gain grows with the degree of flattening at 100B tokens
([code](https://github.com/SR-A-W/how-to-loop-moe)).

## Fixed points and equilibria

### Fixed-point reasoning on the simplex

[Reasoning on the Simplex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33540) iterates a weight-tied map
whose state is the prediction itself: categorical beliefs on a product of simplices.
Because the update is a continuous self-map on a compact convex set, a fixed point exists
for any parameters. A 7M-parameter model reaches 95.1 percent on Sudoku-Extreme and 92.0
percent on Maze-Hard. A 201M language model run for 24 Picard steps beats GPT-2 small on
four zero-shot multiple-choice tasks.

### Graph networks with several equilibria

[Multi-Attractor GNNs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35274) proves that one weight-tied
recurrent message-passing network with multiple equilibria can represent set-valued,
permutation-equivariant maps: different initializations converge to different valid
solutions. The dynamics can be trained from problem energies without solution labels,
and they produce several high-quality solutions on Ising ground states, protein module
detection, and chemical reaction steady states.

## Applications

### CHARM for ARC

[Structured Sparse Memory for Recurrent Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33270)
notes that task-conditioned memory in recurrent ARC models can be more than 30 times the
size of the recurrent backbone. Its compositional sparse embedding cuts learned
task-memory parameters by over 90 percent while improving pass@2. More recurrent depth
helps only when it is balanced with the learning horizon. The full CHARM system reaches
84 percent pass@2 on ARC-AGI-1 and 46.7 percent on ARC-AGI-2 public evaluation
([code](https://github.com/water-vapor/charm)).

### Depth-elastic looped RL policies

[FlexLoop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34488) finds that pretrained looped RL policies act
reliably only near the depth they were trained at. It post-trains them with
adjacent-depth distillation, so shallower recurrent depths also give good decisions and
each state can stop early based on recurrent-depth consistency. It is evaluated on 30
long-horizon goal-conditioned environments.

### Tiny recursive models for combinatorial optimization

[Compute Time Scaling with Recursive Models for Combinatorial Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34585)
uses a graph-aware tiny recursive model that iterates a latent state with adaptive
halting and scales both recursion depth and parallel sampling. It beats every
diffusion-based solver on TSP from 500 to 10,000 cities at lower inference cost and is
strong on maximum independent set.

### LoopTrack for visual tracking

[LoopTrack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33306) replaces the stack of separately
parameterized Transformer blocks in a visual object tracker with a set of shared blocks
applied in a loop. Target-aware looping passes target cues from one loop to the next,
and a gated memory carries target information across frames.

## Near misses

- [Not All Thinking is Created Equal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35643) finds a recurrent
  search circuit in latent-reasoning models, but the abstract does not say whether its
  recurrent steps reuse an internal block or rerun the whole model.
- [Learning When to Recur](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32785) uses token-adaptive
  recursion with early exit in a frozen foundation model, but the abstract does not
  state which learned module is reused.
- [UnfoldCRF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33996) unrolls mean-field inference on a learned
  CRF energy for mask refinement. This is deep unfolding, and the list has so far kept
  such papers out.
- [GenoMorph](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34079) adds adaptive latent reasoning steps to a
  genomic LLM, but reuse of an internal block is not explicit.
