# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-09-20

## Outcome

Today's additions cover three complementary questions: how a recurrent-depth model
should retain information, how repeated internal computation transfers to an applied
audio setting, and how to measure whether recursive language models genuinely use
their depth.

## Added

### Bounded memory for recurrent depth (2609.19521)

This study compares a Block Universal Transformer, an expanding-cache CoTFormer, and
the newly proposed LSTM-UT on Rule 30 cellular automata and delayed recall. All three
operate through repeated shared Transformer computation; LSTM-UT adds bounded gated
memory rather than retaining every earlier recurrent representation.

Why it belongs: the same learned Transformer block is applied repeatedly to an evolving
latent state within one forward pass.

Reader takeaway: when trained through depth six, LSTM-UT retains 99.97% mean cell
accuracy at unseen depth twelve. In a near-parameter-matched delayed-recall comparison,
it reaches 88.94%, versus 79.45% for the current-state-only BUT. The evidence is strong
for the controlled synthetic tasks, but does not yet establish transfer to realistic
reasoning or world-model settings.

Resource: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19521).

### Controlled recurrent refinement for audio detection (2609.19818)

CoRELoop repeatedly applies a frozen SSL audio encoder core, returning the previous
endpoint through learned bridges and gates. Lightweight low-rank factors are shared
across passes, while a step-dependent multiplier and optional halting rule control
the refinement process.

Why it belongs: the encoder is reused as an internal learned module during a single
detector forward process, rather than invoked by an external agent or prompt loop.

Reader takeaway: across fourteen cross-domain test sets, the pooled 24-layer result
improves equal-error rate from 4.85% to 3.74%. Adaptive halting reaches 3.73% at an
average of 1.18 passes, reducing encoder-core evaluations by 41% relative to two fixed
passes. The evaluation is broad for this task, though it remains centered on one frozen
detector family.

Resource: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19818).

### Measuring what recurrent depth contributes (2609.19934)

The Depth Control Protocol separates three effects that ordinary inference-time depth
truncation changes together: the number of block applications, the amount of distinct
computation, and readout calibration. Its controls are specifically enabled by
depth-wise weight sharing.

Why it belongs: the paper directly analyzes language models that repeatedly apply the
same learned layer stack across recurrent depth.

Reader takeaway: naive truncation attributes a 1.4251-nat reduction to latent depth,
but the controlled distinct-depth component is -2.8% in the principal decomposition.
The paper traces much of the apparent loss to application count and readout calibration;
1,000 steps of sampled-depth continuation reduce the calibration share from 29.9% to
5.1%. This is evidence against treating a truncation slope as a clean depth-utilization
metric, not evidence that recurrent depth is universally useless.

Resource: [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19934).

## Near misses

- [SALR (2609.20398)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20398) feeds constructed latent
  reasoning steps back into a generator, but does not establish recurrent-depth reuse
  of an internal learned operator within one forward process.
- [Learning-Induced Dynamical Transition in Recurrent Neural Networks
  (2609.19288)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19288) studies ordinary sequence-time RNN
  dynamics rather than recurrent depth.
- [TriDim (2609.19842)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19842) describes reusable building
  blocks without showing that their weights are shared across repeated depth.
