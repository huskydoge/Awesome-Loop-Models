# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-09-30

## Outcome

Seven papers were added. Each one applies the same learned block or operator several
times within one forward pass. Three are about looped language models: one empirical
study of when recurrence helps and two papers on how the loop update itself should be
designed. The other four bring looped networks to tabular in-context learning,
reinforcement learning, spoken dialogue, and click-through-rate prediction.

## Looped language models

### When recurrence helps, and where to put it

[What Makes Recurrence Effective in Looped Language Models?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36636)
runs controlled experiments on looped language models at inference budgets below, at, and
beyond the training depth. Extra loops can improve reasoning past the training horizon
but hurt knowledge tasks, and harder reasoning problems do not reliably gain more. How
distinct layers and loop iterations are allocated matters beyond effective depth alone,
and non-recurrent output layers make models more robust to running too few loops. The
paper proposes channel-wise history-state injection with timestep conditioning as a
cheap replacement for the usual initial-state injection.

### Loop transitions as optimizer steps

[Looped Transformers as Optimizers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37379) treats the looped
hidden state as a fast weight that is updated through depth. Each loop transition is
written as a local gradient-based update toward an implicit target, built from a
projection, a local objective, and an optimizer rule. Mapping existing transitions into
this framework exposes mismatches in their input maps. Fixing them, and the derived
OperLoop transition with weight decay, adaptive step size, and a delta objective, improve
results over looped and non-looped baselines at matched training FLOPs.

### Adapting the step size of each loop

[Scheduling Recursive Reasoning in Looped Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36653)
notes that recurrent reasoning models apply each shared-parameter update at a fixed unit
scale. It splits the sensitivity of the final loss to that scale into a persistent
progress part and a fluctuation part, and uses the balance between them to set the step
size online. The resulting scheduler, TAPS, raises final accuracy on structured
reasoning tasks without retraining. Used during training, it reaches baseline accuracy
up to 1.56 times faster in wall-clock time.

## Looped networks in applications

### One looped block for tabular in-context learning

[LoopICL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36108) is a tabular foundation model built from a
single transformer block with a cell stream and a row stream, refined by within-column
and cross-column attention. It is trained with varying loop counts and a learned exit
gate. At equal FLOPs it is competitive with TabICLv2 on TabArena and TALENT while using
nearly 90 percent fewer parameters, and users can trade inference cost for accuracy.
Code: [amirbalef/loopicl](https://github.com/amirbalef/loopicl).

### Looped policies for reinforcement learning

[Looped Actor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37432) is a transformer policy that applies a
shared block repeatedly to move a latent state toward a fixed point, with the number of
loops chosen per state. The paper also shows that there are decision problems where a
state-adaptive policy needs asymptotically less computation than any fixed-runtime
optimal policy. Across 22 online and offline tasks in six environments, it matches or
beats an untied baseline with 16 times more parameters. In Boxoban, the number of loops
grows with the number of remaining pushes. Code:
[camail-official/LoopedActor](https://github.com/camail-official/LoopedActor).

### Latent reasoning for spoken dialogue

[Thinking in Depth, Speaking Directly](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37818) introduces
LoopSLM, a spoken language model that reuses a decoder block to refine hidden states,
bringing in acoustic cues at every pass instead of writing out a chain of thought. On
EchoMind it improves reasoning accuracy by more than 20 points over a chain-of-thought
fine-tuned baseline, with 64.5 percent fewer generated tokens and half the latency.

### Recursive towers for click-through-rate prediction

[Beyond Interaction Capacity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37905) proposes RECAP. Its two
cross-network towers share their core interaction weights across recursive steps, and
small route-specific adapters turn one shared backbone into several related predictors
whose outputs are averaged. Together with distillation and weight averaging, this gives
state-of-the-art accuracy on standard CTR benchmarks without adding a full model per
ensemble member.

## Near misses

- [Procedural Core](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37631) trains a small transformer with tied
  middle blocks on procedural data and expands its weights to initialize ordinary untied
  models. The looped model is only an intermediate, so it was not added.
- [Transformers Stop Thinking Too Early](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36585) extends
  reference following in mostly non-looped pretrained transformers with a rank-8 LoRA at
  one layer. It reports looped Ouro results, but looping is not the paper's focus.
- [Port-Hamiltonian Latent Deliberation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37351) studies stable
  latent deliberation flows for test-time compute, but the abstract does not say that a
  learned block is reused inside one forward pass.
