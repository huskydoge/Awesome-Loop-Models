# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-09-02

## Outcome

Two papers were added. Both isolate reused learned computation inside one model
forward process: SMELT revisits the same middle Transformer layers under matched
resource budgets, and Latent Recurrent Thoughts repeatedly applies a small transition
block before a single frozen-LLM decode.

## Added

### SMELT (2609.01343)

SMELT loops the middle half of a sparse Mixture-of-Experts Transformer twice. Its
baseline is matched on per-token FLOPs, non-embedding parameters, and KV-cache size,
avoiding the usual ambiguity between recurrence and simply spending more compute.

Why it was added: the same learned middle-layer block is applied twice within each
forward pass, making recurrent depth the controlled architectural variable.

Reader takeaway: experiments span four sizes up to 54B non-embedding parameters.
Separate Chinchilla-style fits estimate that SMELT saves 6.8--18.0% of training FLOPs
on the compute-optimal frontier. Its downstream advantage is strongest on code and
grows with sample length and the number of in-context examples. Mechanistic analysis
finds that the second visit reduces attention-sink behavior and redirects attention
toward content tokens.

### Latent Recurrent Thoughts (2609.01117)

LRT uses a task-specific proposer to create continuous latent tokens, then repeatedly
applies one TRM-style transition block to fast and slow latent states. The refined
latents are injected into a frozen Qwen3-8B decoder, which is invoked once to produce
the answer.

Why it was added: computation depth comes from recursive reuse of the same learned
transition block, rather than repeated full-model calls or generated reasoning traces.

Reader takeaway: under the same frozen decoder, prompt, data, and training budget,
LRT reaches 56.7% on Countdown-4 versus 5.9% and 8.4% for two prior continuous-latent
baselines. It also improves on Sudoku, HumanEval, MBPP, and StrategyQA. The main
limitation is that the proposer and refiner are trained separately for each task
family; this is not a general zero-shot reasoning model.

## Near misses

- 2609.01449 has a genuine shared recurrent update, but its inference remains framed
  as diffusion/noise-driven iteration, which the collection excludes conservatively.
- 2609.01550 reuses a learned neural-basis dictionary across depth, but constructs
  different effective blocks with depth-specific coefficients instead of repeatedly
  applying one shared transition.
