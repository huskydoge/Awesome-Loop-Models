# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-09-27

## Outcome

Three papers were added. Each one reuses learned computation inside a single forward
pass: a recurrent core in looped language models, a fixed-point message-passing
operator in a graph network, and repeated blocks of pretrained LLM layers.

## Added

### Quantizing looped Transformers

[Quantizing Looped Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30820) asks why standard
post-training quantization breaks on models that reuse their weights across recurrence
steps. It identifies two separate failures. In the first, which the paper calls feedback
exposure, per-channel INT4 on Huginn-3.5B does most of its damage at the non-residual
loop-entry adapter. That layer has no identity path, so its error enters the recurrent
state and is fed back at every later step. Quantizing the residual core hurts much less.
In the second, calibration blindness, one-step GPTQ builds its Hessian only from step-0
activations, so input directions used later in the recurrence get almost no weight.
Accumulating the Hessian across recurrence steps beats both one-step GPTQ and
round-to-nearest on all nine checkpoints from seven looped architectures, and it recovers
bf16-level accuracy on Huginn.

Why it was added: the paper studies weight-shared recurrent-depth Transformers directly,
and its failure modes exist only because the same layers are applied repeatedly.

### A deep-equilibrium graph network with a convergence proof

[Fixed Points Without Fixed Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30277) introduces
SheafDEQ, an implicit graph neural network. Its node representations are fixed points of
learned, edge-dependent neural-sheaf message passing. The paper proves that, under mild
regularity conditions, the model has a unique equilibrium, and that fixed-point iteration
reaches it from any positive initialization. SheafDEQ improves over implicit baselines
with fixed propagation on long-range aggregation tasks and on community detection as the
graphs become more heterophilic.

Why it was added: one learned message-passing operator is iterated to an equilibrium
within a single forward pass, and that implicit layer is the paper's central design.

### Reproducing program-of-layers search

[Programs-of-Layers in LLMs through the Lens of Cortical Areas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31360)
independently reproduces PoLar, which routes each input through its own sequence of
skipped or repeated blocks of pretrained LLM layers. Across five models, the authors
confirm that skipping beats the standard pass, repeating beats skipping, combining both
works best, and harder questions need more repeats. However, the learned single-shot
router's top prediction consistently collapses back to the standard forward pass. The
paper also finds that a few generic programs solve most questions and that
error-correcting programs are brittle
([project page](https://datexis.github.io/RE-PoLar/)).

Why it was added: repeating the same learned layer blocks within one forward pass is the
object of study, and the paper extends a program-of-layers entry already in the list.

## Near misses

- [From Shortcut Learning to Discrete Neural Insertion Sort](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31114)
  is a neural algorithmic reasoning study with per-step processor updates, but the
  abstract does not state that one learned processor is shared across steps.
- [FluidRain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29006) reuses one attention operator across
  scales and neighbouring frames, which is weight sharing across inputs rather than
  repeated depth.
- [Manifold Projection and Iterative Autoencoder Refinement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30288)
  iteratively refines masked-token embeddings, but the abstract does not make clear that
  the same learned modules are reused across refinement steps.
- [Learning to Replace MCMC in Split-Gibbs Diffusion Posterior Sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30539)
  uses a deep-unfolded network inside diffusion posterior sampling. Its iteration is
  diffusion-style inference, and weight tying across unfolded stages is not stated.
