# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-11-17

## Outcome

Four papers were added. Two are decoders that speed up generation from existing looped language
models (Ouro, Huginn, Raven) without retraining them, one studies how to stop the KV cache of a
depth-recurrent model from growing with the number of loops, and one asks when rerunning a block
of layers helps a vision-language embedding model. *When Does Reusing Layers Help Multimodal
Embeddings?* cites Benhao Huang's *Towards Looped Models Done Right* (Parts I and II) in its
related work, as controlled design studies of where recurrence is placed, how inputs are
injected and how recurrent states are supervised. The other three papers do not cite his work.

## Added

### LoopFill: Pipelined Multi-Token Speculative Decoding for Looped Transformers

[arXiv:2611.19649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19649) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** LoopFill drafts several future tokens at once, from suffix lookups and from
shallow-loop predictions in a single draft tree, and lets the shared looped block process
drafts at different loop depths in one batch. It decodes 5.85x faster than greedy
autoregressive decoding on Ouro-2.6B and 5.13x on Huginn-3.5B.

- **Setup:** Ouro-1.4B and Ouro-2.6B (4 loops) and Huginn-3.5B (32 loops) on GSM8K, MATH-500,
  BBH, HumanEval+, MBPP+, Spider and SpecBench, on one GPU in BF16. Baselines are
  autoregressive decoding, WaveFront, LoopSpec, SuffixDecoding, DFlash and LoopAhead, whose
  drafters the authors retrained.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Local and global suffix trees propose continuations, and when a draft
  node reaches an intermediate loop depth, the LM head's top-k tokens are added to the same tree.
  A suffix match can therefore extend a shallow-loop prediction at once, and several future
  tokens can enter the loop pipeline together. Nodes are scored and pruned to a fixed budget,
  and a token is emitted only after it has run all loops, so the output matches autoregressive
  decoding under greedy decoding and under sampling with fixed per-position random draws.
- **Results:** Mean greedy speedup is 5.85x on Ouro-2.6B and 5.13x on Huginn-3.5B, against 4.19x
  and 4.12x for LoopAhead (40% and 25% higher), and LoopFill is fastest on all seven benchmarks.
  At temperature 1.0 the gains shrink to 3.50x against 3.25x on Ouro-2.6B and 2.60x against
  2.37x on Huginn. On SpecBench at temperature 1.0, the throughput gain over autoregressive
  decoding on Ouro-2.6B falls from 4.91x with one request to 2.81x with 16 concurrent requests.
- **Caveat:** The speedups come from the authors' own PyTorch engine on one GPU type. The lead
  over LoopAhead almost disappears at 16 concurrent requests (+1.6% throughput on Ouro-2.6B), and
  a larger draft budget helps single requests but cuts throughput under load.
- **Why added:** The method exploits the reused block of looped models: drafts at different
  loop depths share one batched pass of the same looped layers, and each loop's readout serves
  as a cheap draft.

### LoopSkim: Lossless Multi-Depth Speculative Decoding for Looped Language Models

[arXiv:2611.19896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19896) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** LoopSkim lets every intermediate loop of a looped language model check copied draft
tokens, replacing any draft that a later readout contradicts, and only full-depth readouts
commit tokens. Without training it averages 7.66x over autoregressive decoding under greedy
decoding and 1.73x the speedup of LoopSpec.

- **Setup:** Ouro-1.4B and Ouro-2.6B (4 loops) and three Raven checkpoints retrofitted from
  Llama, OLMo and TinyLlama (32 loops), plus Huginn-0125, on GSM8K, MATH500, BBH, HumanEval+ and
  MBPP+, with held-out and long-context tests. Runs use one H200 with one request at a time.
  Baselines are LoopSpec (run from its official code on SGLang) and CopyLoop.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Draft tokens copied from the context by n-gram lookup run their first
  loops together with the newest token. The readout after the first loops admits the drafts it
  reproduces, and each later checked depth replaces the first draft it contradicts and restarts
  everything after it. Fixed Gumbel noise per position and token makes every check a comparison
  of two tokens, so decoding stays exact under sampling in exact arithmetic. An optional
  16.8M-parameter draft head, trained with the base model frozen, adds a second draft chain.
- **Results:** Speedups over autoregressive decoding (greedy) are 4.44x on Ouro-1.4B, 5.20x on
  Ouro-2.6B and 10.32x to 11.61x on the Raven models, against 2.97x to 5.95x for LoopSpec. Averaged
  over all settings, the ratio of LoopSkim's speedup to LoopSpec's is 1.73x, or 1.99x with the
  draft head. Average speedup is 7.66x under greedy decoding and 4.87x at temperature 1.
- **Caveat:** Each method's speedup is measured against autoregressive decoding in its own
  engine, so the cross-method ratio mixes engines (a same-engine control gives 1.64x to 2.08x).
  Outputs match autoregressive decoding exactly in FP32; in BF16, LoopSkim with the draft head
  matched on 75.5% of Ouro greedy prompts, because rounding depends on matrix shape. The method
  runs 4.74x to 7.74x as many loops as autoregressive decoding, and the gains depend on text
  repeating from the context.
- **Why added:** It relies on the defining property of looped models, that every pass through
  the shared block ends in a usable next-token readout, and turns those intermediate depths into
  verifiers.

### Architectures and KV Replay for Depth-Recurrent Language Models

[arXiv:2611.19446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19446) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** Sharing attention weights across loops does not share their KV states, so a
depth-recurrent model's decoding memory grows with the number of loops. Training a looped
Transformer so that only every third of six loops writes KV states, with the others reusing
them, cuts KV memory 3x while keeping CORE at 18.71 against 18.96 with separate caches.

- **Setup:** About 0.4B-parameter models trained on 5.03B tokens of Nemotron-CC-HQ and scored on
  CORE (PIQA, WinoGrande, ARC, HellaSwag, SciQ), WikiText perplexity and GSM8K. A 24-layer untied
  Transformer with the same parameter count is the baseline.
- **Mechanism / Method:** The paper compares three ways to cut KV memory. Looped hybrids repeat
  a 24-layer stack four times, keep shared attention layers as periodic anchors and replace the
  rest with mLSTM or Gated DeltaNet mixers. A Parcae-style model restricts recurrence to an mLSTM
  core between a Transformer prelude and coda. Recurrent KV replay trains a fully weight-tied
  looped Transformer in which only selected loops write KV states and the others read them, so
  training sees the same shared cache as inference.
- **Results:** With six loops, separate caches give CORE 18.96 and perplexity 31.20. Replay at
  stride 3 gives 18.71 and 31.10, and stride 2 gives 16.76. Compressing the cache only at
  inference falls to 14.48 (stride 2) and 11.66 (stride 3), and training and decoding with
  different strides also breaks the model. The untied baseline scores 14.95. The best Gated
  DeltaNet hybrid reaches 18.79 with an attention anchor every six layers, while mLSTM hybrids
  score 4.70 to 13.56.
- **Caveat:** The authors call the results exploratory rather than resource-matched. Runs use one
  seed and about 5B tokens, and GSM8K scores are near zero. The 3x saving is computed
  analytically; in measured peak memory, the gap between stride 2 and stride 3 is smaller than
  the run-to-run variation.
- **Why added:** Every model studied reuses a shared block across loops within one forward pass,
  and the paper's subject is the KV memory those loops create.

### When Does Reusing Layers Help Multimodal Embeddings?

[arXiv:2611.19827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19827) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** The paper tests eleven ways to rerun a middle block of layers in vision-language
embedding models. An added loop on Qwen3.5-2B loses 0.46 points on MMEB-V2 against its
single-pass control, while a model pretrained with recurrence gains 4.23 points over its
loop-removed control.

- **Setup:** The main backbone is Qwen3.5-2B, with UEmbed and WeChat Multimodal Embedding as two
  further checkpoints of the same architecture and the natively looped LoopVL-1B as a fourth
  model. Models are trained with LoRA on 1.07M image-centred query-candidate pairs from MMEB and
  evaluated on all 78 datasets of MMEB-V2. Every loop design has its own trained single-pass
  control.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A 12-layer prelude and a 4-layer coda run once, and an 8-layer middle
  block runs twice with shared weights, a per-pass LoRA and a learned gate, adding under 1%
  parameters. The paper proves that when the image comes before the query and attention is
  causal, image states stay independent of the query through every pass. It then opens a path
  from query to image in three ways: bidirectional attention, feeding back a pooled state, and
  alternating the order of image and query between passes.
- **Results:** The standard loop scores 64.60 against 64.14 for its control on MMEB-V2 (-0.46).
  The other added loops also lose (-0.36 to -0.77), and a version with an extra generative
  training stage gains 0.36 over a control trained the same way. LoopVL-1B scores 43.19 against
  38.96 for its loop-removed control, with large gains on video and image question answering, but
  stays far below the Qwen3.5 model in absolute terms.
- **Caveat:** Results use one or two seeds, and some effects change sign between seeds. Only
  image data is used, with a frozen vision encoder, and the LoopVL comparison is not
  architecture-matched.
- **Why added:** The looped variants rerun a shared block of learned layers within one forward
  pass. The paper is an application study with mostly negative findings for adding loops after
  pretraining.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** Yes. *Towards Looped Models Done Right*, Parts I and II
  (arXiv:2610.23036 and arXiv:2610.06833), are cited together in the related work as controlled
  design studies that "distinguish where recurrence is placed, how input features are injected,
  and how recurrent states are supervised". They motivate the experiments but are not used as a
  baseline, base model or data source.

## Near misses

- **Latent Lattice Language Models** ([2611.21608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21608)): adds
  per-layer latent states that recur along the sequence, which is ordinary per-layer sequence
  recurrence. An optional variant feeds the top layer's latent into the bottom layer at the next
  token, but it is an extension, not the main model.
- **WEALR** ([2611.19131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19131)): a reinforcement-learning policy
  that chooses where to run a recurrent-depth VLA and its convergence threshold, using offline
  profiles. The contribution is wireless resource management, not the looped model.
- **Halting in Recurrent Sequence Models** ([2611.20377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20377)):
  interprets how RNNs and state-space models decide to emit end-of-sequence; this is ordinary
  sequence recurrence, not a reused block within a forward pass.
