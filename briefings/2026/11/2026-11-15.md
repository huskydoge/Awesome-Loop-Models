# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-11-15

## Outcome

Two papers were added. The Rectified Looped Transformer is an architecture paper about the
state passed between loops of a looped language model. LoopMR is an application paper that
brings the looped-transformer idea to unrolled MRI reconstruction and tests shared against
distinct weights at the same depth. Neither added paper cites Benhao Huang's work. Among the
near misses, *Classical Energies Learn to Reason 1,000x Faster than Looped Models* uses
*Equilibrium Reasoners* as one of its looped-model baselines.

## Added

### Rectified Looped Transformer

[arXiv:2611.16265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16265) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** RLT treats each loop's output as a prediction of the final state and moves the
current state only part of the way toward it. This keeps hidden-state norms bounded and gives
lower perplexity than a standard looped Transformer, most of all when the number of loops at
inference differs from training.

- **Setup:** GPT-2-style looped Transformers following LoopFormer, with three shared layers
  (278M physical parameters, hidden size 2,048) conditioned on loop time and step size. Models
  are trained from scratch on 6.88B tokens of deduplicated Pile with next-token loss on every
  loop. The baseline is the same model with direct feedback (SLT), using the same initial
  weights and data order. Evaluation covers Pile, FineWeb-Edu and OpenWebText perplexity and
  ten zero-shot benchmarks.
- **Mechanism / Method:** The usual looped Transformer feeds each loop's output straight back
  as the next input. RLT instead linearly interpolates between the current state and the
  loop's output, as in rectified flow, with a weight equal to the share of the remaining loops
  that one step covers. States move evenly from the input embedding toward the final
  prediction. It adds no trainable parameters and no extra block evaluations. A theorem shows
  that, under Lipschitz assumptions, state magnitudes stay uniformly bounded and worst-case
  perturbation growth across loops is smaller.
- **Results:**
  - At 4 training and inference loops, RLT lowers perplexity by 4.2% on Pile, 5.5% on
    FineWeb-Edu and 5.2% on OpenWebText (Pile 11.76 to 11.27) and raises the ten-task average
    from 42.38% to 43.30%. At 8 loops, Pile perplexity goes from 11.08 to 10.82, but average
    accuracy falls from 44.36% to 43.53%.
  - Trained at 8 loops and run at 2, RLT cuts perplexity by 35.9%, 48.2% and 47.1% on the
    three corpora. Run at 32, it reaches 17.72 Pile perplexity against 57.72 for SLT, and 42.88%
    average accuracy against 38.05%.
  - RLT keeps its perplexity advantage under W8A16, W4A16 and W8A8 quantization, and it gains
    more than SLT from contrastive decoding and guidance applied at intermediate loops.
- **Caveat:** The only baseline is a direct-feedback looped model at a single 278M scale, with
  no untied Transformer or other feedback schemes such as gated input injection. Downstream
  accuracy gains are small and mixed across depths, and no code is released.
- **Why added:** A shared three-layer stack is applied repeatedly within one forward pass, and
  the paper's contribution is the rule that carries state from one loop to the next.

### One Block Is Enough: LoopMR for Accelerated MRI Reconstruction

[arXiv:2611.14947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14947) · Code: [hym97/LoopMR](https://github.com/hym97/LoopMR)

**TL;DR:** LoopMR reuses one Restormer block at every iteration of an unrolled MRI
reconstruction network and beats PromptMR+, which learns a separate block per cascade, on four
datasets with about one ninth of its parameters.

- **Setup:** Multi-coil accelerated MRI on CC359 brain, fastMRI knee, CMRxRecon2023 heart and
  an in-house 3-D carotid dataset, at several acceleration factors. Baselines are E2E-VarNet,
  PromptMR and PromptMR+. Controlled experiments compare the same block with shared and
  distinct weights at 6, 12 and 24 iterations, and compare CNN and transformer block families
  at two sizes.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Each iteration passes the current image estimate, its
  re-undersampled version, the zero-filled image, their residual and a set of latent images to
  the same Restormer block. The block returns an image update and new latents, followed by a
  soft data-consistency step in k-space. The block and its learned scalars are shared across
  all 12 iterations, and the latents are its only memory of earlier iterations.
- **Results:**
  - LoopMR beats PromptMR+, PromptMR and E2E-VarNet in PSNR and SSIM at every acceleration
    factor on all four datasets (p < 0.01) with 10.3M parameters against 92.9M for PromptMR+.
    The largest margin is on carotid data, +0.84 dB and +0.76 dB PSNR at 6x and 12x.
  - Its lead grows at acceleration factors not seen in training, from 0.18 dB at 4x to 0.92 dB
    at 16x, and it also holds on an unseen dataset.
  - With the same block, the shared model matches or beats the distinct-weight model at every
    depth: SSIM 0.9367, 0.9375 and 0.9373 against 0.9356, 0.9370 and 0.9366 at 6, 12 and 24
    iterations, with 10.2M against 52M, 102M and 202M parameters.
  - Shared CNN blocks do worse than their distinct versions, while shared transformer blocks
    match or beat theirs.
- **Caveat:** The saving is in parameters, not time: inference takes 203-624 ms per slice
  against 100-121 ms for PromptMR+. Training sets are moderate, each model is trained for one
  dataset and mask family, and image quality is measured by PSNR and SSIM rather than reader
  studies.
- **Why added:** One learned block is applied at every iteration of a single reconstruction
  pass, and the paper directly tests shared against distinct weights at matched depth.

## Near misses

- **Classical Energies Learn to Reason 1,000x Faster than Looped Models**
  ([2611.17309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17309)): CRAM solves Sudoku-Extreme and mazes by
  stochastic descent on a learned pairwise Hopfield-Tank energy rather than by reusing a
  learned network block. This is energy-based iterative inference, although the paper compares
  directly against looped reasoners (including *Equilibrium Reasoners*) and warns against
  using these puzzles to judge them.
- **RECALL** ([2611.14763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14763)): refines multi-camera
  calibration with a GRU-based recurrent update. The main text does not state the number of
  steps or show results per iteration, and the loop is a secondary part of a calibration
  pipeline.
- **From Large Language Models to Efficient System One Models via Adaptive Computation**
  ([2611.16321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16321)): prefix sub-models share their final layers
  and head for early exit, but no state is passed through the same layers more than once.
- **Recursive State-Augmented Language Models**
  ([2611.16375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16375)): a persistent latent state across
  observations produces LoRA adaptations of a frozen GPT-2. This is memory across turns, not
  reuse of layers on a state they computed.
