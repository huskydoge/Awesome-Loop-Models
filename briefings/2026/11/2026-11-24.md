# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-11-24

## Outcome

Thirteen papers were added from the weekend's announcements. Three concern building looped
language models: LoopRes retrofits pretrained LLMs into loops, a controlled study shows how the
training loop-count schedule shapes what each pass does, and a distillation study compares
weight-shared looping with cross-token recurrence as ways to enlarge a student. Two are
systems papers, one for pipeline-parallel training of looped models and one for serving them
with self-speculative decoding. Three analyse looped and recurrent-depth models: when stored
facts become readable across loops, when recurrence length-generalizes, and why HRM's wrong
answers stay unsettled. The remaining five apply looping to diffusion, vision, recommendation
and wireless beam prediction. None of today's additions cites Benhao Huang's work.

## Added

### Unlocking the Power of Loops in Pretrained LLMs with Loop Residuals

[arXiv:2611.31094](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31094) · Code:
[NUS-HPC-AI-Lab/LoopRes](https://github.com/NUS-HPC-AI-Lab/LoopRes)

**TL;DR:** LoopRes finetunes pretrained LLMs into prelude-core-coda looped models in which
each looped layer attends over the outputs of earlier loops, and turns looping into large,
consistent gains over SFT where earlier retrofitting methods gave small ones.

- **Setup:** Math and code SFT of Qwen3-1.7B, 4B, 8B and 30B-A3B Base, Qwen3.5-2B-Base and
  Llama-3.2-1B, compared with plain SFT and eight reimplemented looping methods (including
  Huginn-style and Ouro-style loops) at 2x and 4x the SFT compute.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Building on Attention Residuals, each looped layer forms its input as
  attention over the prelude output, earlier loop outputs and the current partial sum. Because
  iterates of the same layers look alike, each source gets a trainable loop-aware key, and a
  residual window limits each layer to the most recent loops. A fused Triton kernel keeps the
  overhead to 4.8% step time and 0.7% peak memory.
- **Results:** On Qwen3-1.7B-Base the average score rises from 43.2 with SFT to 55.8 at 2x
  compute (+29.1%), against +13.9% for the best prior looping method, and to 57.8 at 4x. At 2x
  the 4B model goes from 36.6 to 45.8, the 8B from 42.9 to 51.5 and the 30B-A3B from 55.1 to
  60.6.
- **Caveat:** All gains are measured relative to SFT in a finetuning regime, and returns
  saturate beyond five loops.
- **Why added:** A retrofitting method for looped LLMs with broad backbone and baseline
  coverage.

### DyLoMo-DiT: Dynamic Looped Mixture-of-Experts Diffusion Transformers

[arXiv:2611.30844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30844) · Code:
[OneBoxCream/DyLoMo-DiT](https://github.com/OneBoxCream/DyLoMo-DiT) (project page:
[oneboxcream.github.io/DyLoMo-DiT](https://oneboxcream.github.io/DyLoMo-DiT/))

**TL;DR:** DyLoMo-DiT reuses the middle layers of a diffusion Transformer inside every denoiser
call, spends the saved parameters on sparse experts, and gives low-noise steps more passes,
beating DiT and DiT-MoE at matched parameters and compute.

- **Setup:** Class-conditional ImageNet 256x256 under the DiT protocol (400k steps, FID-50k)
  against DiT and DiT-MoE matched within about 2% in parameters and 1% in compute, plus an
  untied "same recipe, no loop" control; also a 15.48B-total, 1.05B-active text-to-image model.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A front segment runs once, the weight-shared middle segment runs r
  times with residual writes scaled by 1/r, and a back segment runs once. The pass count
  depends on the noise level: three passes for timesteps below 500 and one above.
- **Results:** FID falls from 21.19 to 18.82 (B), 7.42 to 6.41 (L) and 5.57 to 4.22 (XL)
  against DiT, reductions of 11%, 14% and 24%, and by 36-56% against DiT-MoE. The untied control
  reaches 19.45, 6.97 and 5.02. The text-to-image model scores 0.714 on GenEval.
- **Caveat:** A uniform two-pass loop alone does not help (7.93 to 7.94 FID); the gain comes
  from combining the loop with experts and noise-dependent depth.
- **Why added:** Looping inside one denoiser call, with iso-param and iso-FLOP looped-vs-untied
  controls and noise-conditioned depth.

### A Pipeline-Parallel System for Depth-Recurrent Models

[arXiv:2611.30581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30581) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** Loop-PP rebuilds the interleaved 1F1B pipeline schedule and its communication so
that a shared layer stack split across pipeline stages can be visited several times per
micro-batch without duplicating weights.

- **Setup:** Megatron-LM training of looped MoE models from 8B to 60B parameters and a dense
  14B model on 32 GPUs, against an untied unrolled baseline, a replicated tied baseline and a
  lockstep schedule.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Loop-PP unrolls the logical visits into a graph, schedules different
  iterations of the same stage separately within a 1F1B skeleton, and emits static per-rank
  action streams that include the back edge from the last stage to the first.
- **Results:** Peak memory falls by up to 32.8%, and at 60B with two pipeline stages the
  baselines run out of memory while Loop-PP completes. Under an equal GPU budget the best
  throughput rises by up to 12.7% (170.7k against 151.5k tokens/s on the 30B MoE).
- **Caveat:** It is slower than the unrolled baseline at eight pipeline stages, and its
  advantage shrinks as the loop count grows.
- **Why added:** Training infrastructure built specifically for the cyclic stage graph of
  looped models.

### PackLoop: Row-Packed Self-Speculative Decoding for Looped Language Models under Concurrency

[arXiv:2611.30205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30205) · Code: not yet released (the authors
say it will be)

**TL;DR:** PackLoop shows that under concurrent serving a looped language model's decoding
cost is set by per-loop KV-cache reads, and speculates only inside the rows that are read
anyway, so exact self-speculation keeps paying off as load grows.

- **Setup:** Ouro-1.4B and 2.6B (four loops) and their Thinking variants on RTX 3090, RTX 4090
  and H100 GPUs, with 1 to 192 concurrent requests and contexts up to 32K, against wavefront
  and other self-speculative schedules.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Deep loops accumulate blocks of up to eight positions, cheap
  context-copy proposals go into the first loop's row, and the block size follows an
  economic-order-quantity rule. Output is identical to ordinary decoding.
- **Results:** PackLoop beats the best existing schedule at 161 of 164 load points (median
  1.25x, up to 1.76x). On an H100 with 64 requests it runs 1.50x faster than ordinary decoding
  while the wavefront schedule runs at 0.98x.
- **Caveat:** Results for the 32-loop Huginn model come from a replay model rather than
  measurement.
- **Why added:** Serving work specific to looped language models.

### Loop-Count Schedules in Looped Transformers: An Empirical Study

[arXiv:2611.29704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29704) · Code:
[kvoyager/gatedloop-transformer](https://github.com/kvoyager/gatedloop-transformer) (weights:
[konstank/gatedloop-transformer](https://huggingface.co/konstank/gatedloop-transformer))

**TL;DR:** In looped language models with learned residual gates on every extra pass, the
lowest loop count used in training decides whether extra passes act as full layers or as small
corrections.

- **Setup:** 46M-110M-parameter models trained on 2B tokens, each block applied R times with
  shared weights, under three schedules: R fixed at 2, R sampled from 1-6 and R sampled from
  2-4, against parameter-matched and FLOP-matched plain models.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Passes after the first write through learned sigmoid gates. The
  study tracks gate values, the norm and direction of each pass's update, and loss at loop
  counts outside the training range, and tests a static KV variant that halves the cache.
- **Results:** Sampling down to R=1 collapses the gates to 0.1-0.5, while schedules starting
  at R=2 keep them at 0.6-0.9. The R in 2-4 schedule reaches 2.909 nats against 2.951 for the
  parameter-matched plain model and degrades only 0.038 nats at R=8, whereas fixed R=2 jumps
  from 2.919 to 3.749 at R=6.
- **Caveat:** Small models and loss-only evaluation; at equal compute the plain deeper models
  still win by about 0.10 nats.
- **Why added:** Controlled evidence on how training-time depth schedules shape looped models,
  with parameter- and FLOP-matched controls.

### What Makes Recurrence Generalize Across Lengths?

[arXiv:2611.29171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29171) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** Weight-tied recurrent Transformers extrapolate to longer state-tracking problems
only when the input representation and positional encoding stay reusable across lengths.

- **Setup:** A five-entity swap-tracking task trained on 2-10 swaps and tested on 11-19, 20-40
  and 40-80 swaps, with the recurrence run once per swap, comparing operation representations,
  positional encodings and model sizes over three seeds.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A shared block is applied repeatedly with the input embedding
  re-injected at every iteration; inputs use either multi-token templates or atomic operation
  tokens.
- **Results:** A one-layer recurrent model reaches 90.6% exact match on 11-19 swaps against
  28.1% for a compute-matched seven-layer untied model. A larger recurrent model reaches 35.1%
  on 20-40 swaps while a 12-layer untied model stays at 0%. Templates without positional
  encoding fail even in distribution, and absolute positional encoding cuts 11-19-swap accuracy
  to 18.0%.
- **Caveat:** One synthetic task and small models; every model is near chance on 40-80 swaps.
- **Why added:** A compute-matched looped-versus-untied study of length generalization.

### When do stored facts become available in looped transformers?

[arXiv:2611.28808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28808) · Code: not yet released (the authors
say it will be)

**TL;DR:** Looped Transformers make memorized facts readable gradually across loops and earlier
than untied models of the same unrolled depth, and retrieved entity states causally drive
multi-hop answers.

- **Setup:** Synthetic biographies with 20K-100K individuals and multi-hop knowledge-graph
  chains, studied in weight-tied looped models against untied baselines of equal unrolled depth
  and parameter-matched single-pass models, over several seeds.
- **Mechanism / Method:** The paper measures how many bits of each fact can be read out after
  every loop with native readouts and tuned lenses, then probes entity identities and patches a
  probe-defined entity subspace at a single position.
- **Results:** Looped models reach half of their final readable information after 23-65% of
  their depth, against 81-99% for untied models of equal unrolled depth; heavier memorization
  loads delay it. Supervising every loop makes near-final information readable after the first
  loop. Subspace patching yields the donor's answer in 79.07-98.57% of cases.
- **Caveat:** Synthetic tasks, small models and linear readouts.
- **Why added:** A mechanistic account of knowledge retrieval in looped models with untied
  controls.

### When Wrong Answers Remain Unsettled: Failure Dynamics in Recurrent Reasoners

[arXiv:2611.28971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28971) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** When a Hierarchical Reasoning Model gets a Sudoku wrong, its recurrence has usually
not settled, so spending extra budget on more depth or on puzzle symmetries helps more than
injecting noise.

- **Setup:** A 27M-parameter HRM trained on Sudoku-Extreme (41.76% exact on 422,786 test
  puzzles), with replications on a second seed, TRM, Maze, GRAM-guided TRM and N-Queens.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Terminal states are run for another full inference budget and
  compared with an unperturbed control, alongside update-spectrum analyses and
  perturb-and-reselect procedures that use the learned halting head as selector.
- **Results:** 0.95 of 512 solved endpoints keep their answer, against 0.18 of wrong endpoints
  and 0.42 of near misses. At nine times the budget, extending recursion depth gains 5.6
  points, noise reselection 4.8 and Sudoku symmetry augmentation 34.6.
- **Caveat:** The geometric diagnostics rest on one HRM checkpoint and do not transfer to TRM or
  GRAM.
- **Why added:** A failure-mode analysis of hierarchical recurrent-depth reasoners with
  test-time-compute comparisons.

### Bridging Teacher-Student Capability Gaps with Recurrent Computation

[arXiv:2611.28603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28603) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** Giving a distilled student extra computation without extra parameters helps, and
cross-token recurrence keeps benefiting from larger teachers where weight-shared looping
saturates.

- **Setup:** 0.4B, 12-layer students distilled from 0.8B-3.9B teachers during pretraining and
  in math post-training (teacher-forced and on-policy distillation), plus a conversion of
  Qwen2.5-0.5B into looped and recurrent forms with 1.5B-7B teachers.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Looped students apply the full stack two to four times with tied
  weights; recurrent students feed the previous token's final-layer state back into every
  layer for the next token, folding depth into time.
- **Results:** Looped students lower bits per byte but still get worse with the largest
  teacher, while recurrent students improve from 0.860 to 0.851 across the teacher sweep. Under
  on-policy distillation recurrent students reach 47.2% on GSM8K and 34.2% on MATH500, while
  vanilla students stay at 35.7-36.7% on GSM8K.
- **Caveat:** Single seeds at small scale, and much of the recurrent students' GSM8K lead is
  already present without a teacher.
- **Why added:** Looped and depth-into-time students in distillation and post-training, with
  backbone size held fixed.

### LoopSight: Looped Vision Transformers with Gated Prediction Memory

[arXiv:2611.28575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28575) · Code:
[feufhd/LoopSight](https://github.com/feufhd/LoopSight) (announced; not yet released)

**TL;DR:** LoopSight keeps a separate, gated prediction state across the iterations of a looped
vision Transformer, so correct intermediate answers are not overwritten as the shared core keeps
computing.

- **Setup:** ARC-AGI-1 and ARC-AGI-2 with test-time training, Visual Sudoku and ImageNet-100,
  at 4.3M, 10M and 19M parameters, against LoopViT.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A cross-attention gate decides, per patch and channel, how much of
  the core's candidate features to write into the prediction memory, which starts as a running
  average across loops. The shared core is conditioned on the iteration through an asymptotic
  loop encoding, and training samples the loop count.
- **Results:** ARC-AGI-1 pass@2 reaches 67.4, 67.5 and 69.6 at the three sizes (+6.6, +3.7 and
  +3.8 over LoopViT). From 12 to 24 loops, frozen-probe accuracy stays at 94.5-94.3% while
  LoopViT drops from 94.8% to 91.4%.
- **Caveat:** Evaluation is dominated by ARC with a task-specific test-time-training pipeline.
- **Why added:** A recurrent-state design that separates readout from computation in a looped
  vision model.

### loopxDINO: Dense Visual Prediction via Iterative Latent Reasoning

[arXiv:2611.29180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29180) · Code:
[loopxDINO/loopxDINO](https://github.com/loopxDINO/loopxDINO)

**TL;DR:** loopxDINO replaces a stacked segmentation decoder with one weight-tied cross-attention
block looped over cached features from a frozen DINOv2 backbone.

- **Setup:** Semantic segmentation on Pascal VOC 2012 against a six-layer Transformer decoder
  (54.06M parameters) and a convolutional head.
- **Mechanism / Method:** The looped block (7.96M core, 14.85M trainable parameters) updates
  queries each iteration with step-conditioned adaLN, a learned damping factor and feedback of
  intermediate masks as attention biases, and can stop early per input.
- **Results:** Two iterations give 85.66% mIoU and 65.01% boundary IoU against 84.57% and 57.60%
  for the six-layer decoder, with 72.5% fewer trainable parameters; activation memory stays
  nearly flat in the number of iterations.
- **Caveat:** Accuracy falls with more iterations on clean images (77.63% at four, 42.00% at
  16), evaluation covers one dataset, and the halting description is inconsistent across
  sections.
- **Why added:** A vision application of a weight-tied looped decoder with adaptive depth.

### MoR4Rec: Mixture-of-Recursion for Sequential Recommendation

[arXiv:2611.29462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29462) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** MoR4Rec brings Mixture-of-Recursions to next-item recommendation, routing each item
in a user's history through a shared Transformer block a different number of times.

- **Setup:** MovieLens-1M and two Amazon datasets, against ten sequential recommenders
  including GRU4Rec, BERT4Rec and SIGMA, and a vanilla Transformer with the same four logical
  layers.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A unique input layer feeds a shared block whose recursion depth a
  linear router picks per item; keys and values from the first recursion are reused, and the
  attention output is gated.
- **Results:** Average gains over the strongest baseline are +2.28% Recall@5, +2.50% NDCG@5 and
  +2.0% MRR@5; against the vanilla Transformer on MovieLens-1M it uses 42.76 against 44.10
  MFLOPs and 380.5K against 422.0K parameters.
- **Caveat:** Only two recursions and modest efficiency savings.
- **Why added:** Adaptive-depth recursion over a shared block applied in a new domain.

### R2WM: Recursive Reasoning Wireless Model for Long-Horizon mmWave Beam Prediction

[arXiv:2611.28774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28774) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** R2WM applies a Tiny-Recursive-Model-style nested recursion over one small
causal-attention backbone to predict mmWave beams ten steps ahead, beating a GPT-2-based
predictor with a hundredth of its parameters.

- **Setup:** DeepMIMO simulation at 28 GHz, predicting 10 future beams from 40 past ones,
  against LSTM-based predictors, a prompt-as-prefix LLM and a same-size Transformer.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Coupled latent and prediction states are refined by inner and outer
  loops that both call the same two-block backbone, 240 backbone calls per prediction.
- **Results:** Average normalized beamforming gain is 0.9597 against 0.8629 for the LLM
  predictor and 0.8319 for the same-size Transformer, with 7.016M against 749.632M parameters;
  fewer recursions lower the gain to 0.8933.
- **Caveat:** Simulation only, and the recursive model spends far more compute than the
  same-size Transformer.
- **Why added:** A recursive-depth model applied to an external engineering domain.

## Near misses

- **One Million Tokens in 20GB** ([2611.29092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29092)): targets a
  looped MoE model, but the 2-bit KV-cache compression treats each loop visit as an ordinary
  layer and is not specific to looping.
- **Does the Mixer Matter? Paired-Seed Ablations of Tiny Bilingual Language Models**
  ([2611.30623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30623)): includes a three-pass shared-block arm that
  recovers about 40% of the gap to independent depth, but the paper is about mixer and expert
  choices.
- **Specialized Large Language Models for Markovian Reasoning**
  ([2611.28285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28285)): proposes a dual-loop Transformer design but
  never trains one; its only experiment prompts untied pretrained models.
- **Certified Comparative Statics for Conservation-Law Equilibrium Layers**
  ([2611.29050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29050)): the equilibrium is a fixed, non-learned
  biochemical solve; the paper is sensitivity theory, not a learned implicit layer.
- **Yggdrasil: Tree-Routed Recurrent Experts** ([2611.29954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29954)):
  a stacked linear recurrence with routed experts; no state is fed back through the same layers.
- **Recurrent Point Transformer via Test-Time Training**
  ([2611.29059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29059)): carries fast weights from chunk to chunk,
  which is sequence recurrence rather than layer reuse.
- **Can Latent Reasoning Drive LLM Outputs Without a Trace?**
  ([2611.31015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31015)): studies latent reasoning only in standard
  Transformers.
- **RMR-VLA: Recurrent Memory Reasoning for Vision-Language-Action Models**
  ([2611.30145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30145)): carries a text state across repeated model
  calls, not through reused internal layers.
- **Coupled Dynamical System for Sparsemax Attention**
  ([2611.28534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28534)): computes sparsemax by a physical relaxation
  with no learned operator being iterated.
