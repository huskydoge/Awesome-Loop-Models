# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-11-01

## Outcome

Six papers were added. Three are about looped language models themselves: a large controlled
study of where and how often to loop, a faster decoder, and a training-budget study of
backpropagation through the loop. The other three apply one learned update many times inside
the forward pass, for video stereo matching, 3D Gaussian splatting and the decoding of
post-quantum error-correcting codes. None of the six cites Benhao Huang's work.

## Added

### How to Loop Your Transformer: Loop Scaling, Specialization, and Parallel Inference
[arXiv:2610.40325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40325) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** A controlled study of looped decoder-only transformers shows that which part to loop
depends on whether parameters or training FLOPs are the constraint, that more loops eventually
stop helping, and that small per-loop adapters and a repaired Parallel Loop Transformer help.

- **Setup:** Dense decoder-only models trained from scratch, up to 1B parameters, 155B tokens,
  1.07e21 training FLOPs and 32 loops, scored by DCLM bits-per-byte and eight reasoning and QA
  tasks.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Compares full-stack, per-layer and middle-block looping and varies the
  loop count; adds loop-specific low-rank adapters on shared weights; and revisits Parallel Loop
  Transformers, which pass hidden states between loops across adjacent tokens so loop stages can
  run concurrently during decoding.
- **Results:** Full-stack looping gives the best quality at a fixed parameter budget, while
  middle-block looping gives a better return when training FLOPs are constrained. From 100M to
  1B parameters, two-loop models trained at 80 tokens per parameter reach a given BPB with fewer
  training FLOPs than unlooped models at 160. Loop-specific adapters add 1.1-4.3% non-embedding
  parameters at 300M (against 51.4% for doubling depth) and help more as loops increase.
  Keeping the current-token embedding and normalizing it separately from the previous-token
  state restores Parallel Loop Transformer quality.
- **Caveat:** Gains saturate, reverse or become unstable as loops are added, and a doubled-depth
  model still beats the adapter variants at comparable training FLOPs, so the adapters save
  parameters rather than compute.
- **Why added:** Every model studied reuses the same transformer layers several times in one
  forward pass, and the paper compares looped and unlooped models at matched parameters and
  matched training FLOPs.

### CopyLoop: Fast Looped Transformer Decoding via Context Copying and Shallow Screening
[arXiv:2610.38822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38822) · Code: [github.com/dualverse-ai/copyloop](https://github.com/dualverse-ai/copyloop)

**TL;DR:** A training-free decoder for looped transformers proposes continuations copied from
earlier context, discards wrong ones after a shallow loop depth, and pipelines the rest toward
full-depth verification.

- **Setup:** Six checkpoints (Ouro-1.4B and 2.6B with four loops, Raven-TinyLlama and
  Huginn-3.5B with 32, and two Ouro Thinking models) on HumanEval+, MBPP+, GSM8K, MATH-500 and
  an AIME/BeyondAIME panel, against autoregressive decoding, prompt lookup decoding, LoopSpec and
  WaveFront.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A predicted head token is extended with up to K tokens copied from a
  matching span earlier in the context. The block advances to a shallow recurrent depth, where
  each copied token must match its predecessor's shallow prediction; survivors keep looping
  toward full depth while new blocks enter, and full-depth predictions commit tokens in causal
  order.
- **Results:** Speedups over full-depth autoregressive decoding are 2.98-10.70x on base models
  (up to 10.70x on Raven-TinyLlama HumanEval+, 6.04x on Ouro-1.4B) and 2.74-3.52x on Thinking
  models, the fastest method in 14 of 16 base and all eight Thinking configurations.
- **Caveat:** Accuracy is not exactly preserved under BF16 (Ouro-2.6B MATH-500 falls from 46.60
  to 44.00), speedups depend on how much context repeats, and short responses gain little. The
  authors state the idea started from an autonomous-agent finding they then reproduced.
- **Why added:** It speeds up inference for models that apply one shared recurrent block many
  times per token, exploiting the intermediate loop depths.

### Deeper Backpropagation or More Updates? Training Looped Language Models Across Budgets
[arXiv:2610.38422](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38422) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** Whether a short or a long backpropagation window through the loop is better depends
on the training budget, and the short window's advantage shrinks as the budget grows.

- **Setup:** Parcae prelude-core-coda models (144M parameters with a two-block shared core, and
  388M) trained on FineWeb-Edu with sampled loop depths and evaluated at 16 iterations.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Gradients are kept only through the final four (K4) or eight (K8)
  core iterations. The paper compares the two at equal tokens and at roughly equal training
  time, where K4 gets extra updates, at a base budget and one four times larger.
- **Results:** At 4,096 updates K8 beats K4 by 0.0237 nats/token at equal tokens, but K4 with
  extra updates (4,986) reaches 3.2523 test NLL against K8's 3.2700 at matched time. At the
  larger budget that advantage shrinks in every seed, and three of four 144M seeds then favor
  K8.
- **Caveat:** Only two windows, two model sizes and two budgets are compared. The 388M runs
  still favor K4, so the result is a shrinking advantage rather than a consistent reversal.
- **Why added:** It studies how to train a model that reuses a shared core block many times in
  one forward pass, via truncated backpropagation through those iterations.

### DEQ-Stereo: Deep Euilibrium Video Stereo Matching via Adaptive Convergence
[arXiv:2610.39701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39701) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** Video stereo matching refines disparity by solving for a deep-equilibrium fixed point
and stopping at convergence instead of running a fixed number of recurrent updates.

- **Setup:** Online video stereo on SceneFlow, TartanAir, KITTI 2015 and zero-shot Spring,
  against online video and per-frame iterative methods such as TC-Stereo and IGEV-Stereo.
- **Mechanism / Method:** One update operator, a Convolutional Simplified Gated Unit (a ConvGRU
  without the reset gate and with a forget gate as damping), is solved to equilibrium by
  Broyden's method with a tolerance and an iteration cap, trained with implicit gradients plus
  an intermediate-state loss. Temporal State Transfer warm-starts each frame from the previous
  frame's converged hidden state.
- **Results:** 0.45 EPE on SceneFlow, about 10% better than TC-Stereo. On TartanAir, dropping
  the solver raises EPE from 0.39 to 0.45 while the full model runs nearly twice as fast, and
  the warm start cuts runtime by 34% for about a 0.5% change in Bad 3.0.
- **Caveat:** A plain DEQ drop-in with a standard ConvGRU fails to converge, so the result
  depends on the simplified update; there is no code link.
- **Why added:** Disparity is the fixed point of one shared learned update applied until
  convergence within each frame.

### GradSplat: Gradients Inside the Forward Pass for Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting
[arXiv:2610.38951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38951) · Code: [github.com/Jingong-Chen/GradSplat](https://github.com/Jingong-Chen/GradSplat) (release announced)

**TL;DR:** A feed-forward Gaussian splatting model closes much of its gap to per-scene
optimization by taking a few learned gradient steps on its own input views inside the forward
pass.

- **Setup:** Novel-view synthesis on RealEstate10K narrow and wide six-view protocols, plus DL3DV
  and ScanNet++ without fine-tuning, against nine feed-forward methods.
- **Mechanism / Method:** The predicted Gaussians are rendered into the input views, and the
  photometric gradient passes through a learned update (convolutions, four transformer blocks
  and a ConvGRU), shared by every step in its group. Three appearance steps (opacity, color)
  are followed by five shape steps on a log-anisotropy parameterization, unrolled in training.
- **Results:** Best PSNR and SSIM among nine methods on both six-view RealEstate10K protocols,
  reaching 33.683 dB on the narrow and 26.755 dB on the wide protocol. In a step-count ablation
  of the third training stage, removing the steps gives 28.583 dB on the narrow protocol against
  31.844 dB with three appearance and five shape steps, and each extra shape step gains less
  than the one before.
- **Caveat:** Step counts are fixed, and the gains rely on gradients through the rasterizer
  at inference, which adds per-step rendering cost.
- **Why added:** One learned update module is applied repeatedly within a single forward pass,
  in the style of recurrent refinement.

### Recurrent Bipartite Graph Neural Networks for QC-MDPC Error Decoding in McEliece Cryptosystems
[arXiv:2610.38479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38479) · Code: none stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** A small graph network decodes post-quantum QC-MDPC codes by repeating the same
message-passing step with GRU memory at every node, failing less often than classical decoders.

- **Setup:** Decoding failure rate over 20 unseen random QC-MDPC parity-check matrices at
  several error weights, against Bit-Flipping and attenuated Min-Sum; training samples a new
  matrix each epoch with an error-weight curriculum.
- **Mechanism / Method:** One set of variable-to-check and check-to-variable MLPs and node GRU
  cells (110,145 parameters in total) runs for up to 12 iterations on the Tanner graph, stopping
  once the syndrome is satisfied.
- **Results:** At error weight 10 it cuts the decoding failure rate by 63.7% relative to Min-Sum,
  and it needs fewer iterations than Min-Sum on the frames it decodes.
- **Caveat:** A proof of concept on small codes; it is slower per frame than classical message
  passing, and there is no comparison with other neural decoders.
- **Why added:** One learned message-passing step with recurrent memory is reused across
  decoding iterations within a single forward pass.

## Near misses

- **Compute Has Shape: Self-Morphing the Internal Computation of Frozen LLMs**
  ([2610.38382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38382)): a per-input selector chooses between the
  base model, repeating a mid-depth layer span of a frozen LLM, or branching; layer reuse is one
  option among several rather than the paper's core mechanism.
- **Latent Path Exploration via Potential-Guided Subtrajectory Balance**
  ([2610.40283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40283)): stochastic continuous latent reasoning
  passed between full model steps, with no reused internal block.
- **LatentWeave** ([2610.39557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39557)): test-time optimization of a
  multimodal model's hidden states, an external optimization loop rather than layer reuse.
- **Certified Access** ([2610.39073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39073)): a theory of test-time
  compute on a Hamiltonian latent-dynamics memory; it does not study a looped network.
- **Attention, Dynamic Wave and Hybrid Sequence Mixers in Language Models**
  ([2610.39854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39854)): a new recurrent sequence mixer, which is
  ordinary sequence recurrence rather than reuse over depth.
