# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-11-23

## Outcome

Three papers were added. Model Carving learns which attention and MLP sublayers to keep
during pretraining, and in a looped Transformer it learns this separately for each loop, so
weight-tied iterations end up running different subsets of the same layers. Circular
predictive coding turns a shallow network into a loop whose energy relaxation reaches the
fixed point of an equivalent deep equilibrium model, trained with local updates instead of
backpropagation. MoLR brings adaptive recursion to parameter-efficient fine-tuning by
reapplying one small shared map inside each LoRA adapter. None of today's additions cites
Benhao Huang's work.

## Added

### Model Carving: Training Transformers That Optimize Their Own Topology

[arXiv:2611.27962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27962) · Code: not stated in the paper
(project page: [kadenzheng.com/model-carving](https://kadenzheng.com/model-carving/))

**TL;DR:** Model Carving puts a learned scalar gate on every sublayer application, one per
loop in looped Transformers, and removes closed sublayers during pretraining; the carved 1.3B
looped model runs 23% fewer FLOPs per token and reaches a loss its full-depth looped baseline
would need about 1.7x the training compute to match.

- **Setup:** Pretraining of standard Transformers from 140M to 2.7B parameters and of Fully
  Looped Transformers from 140M to 1.3B parameters (each looped four times), compared at equal
  training compute with the full-depth model, a shallower model at the carved model's
  inference cost, and pruned and distilled students. A three-layer, four-loop model is also
  trained on S5 composition, ListOps and modular addition.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Each sublayer writes to the residual stream through a gate, and
  DenseFormer-style learned connections join layers. A pressure term derived from the
  forecast rate of learning pushes gates toward zero, and gates that close are removed during
  the run. In the looped model every loop has its own gates and connections, so the same
  shared sublayer can run in one loop and be skipped in another.
- **Results:** In the looped family the compute multiplier grows from 1.33 at 140M to 1.71 at
  1.3B. The carved 1.3B looped model keeps 147 of 192 sublayer applications, reaches 0.683
  validation bpb against 0.704 for the full-depth model and scores 36.0 on CORE-22 against
  35.2. Across both families carving saves up to 34% of FLOPs per token and decodes up to
  1.6x faster. On the symbolic tasks it carves the looped model down to the computation each
  task needs, in one case an attention-only Transformer.
- **Caveat:** The method is general and the looped family is one of two tested; looped models
  stop at 1.3B, and the carved topology transfers across sizes less reliably for looped models
  (AUC 0.76-0.88) than for standard ones.
- **Why added:** Per-loop gates give the iterations of a weight-tied stack different depth
  profiles while keeping the weights shared, an iteration-dependent sharing scheme evaluated on
  pretrained looped language models.

### Circular Predictive Coding Unlocks Effective Depth

[arXiv:2611.27610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27610) · Code:
[rel-int/CircularEP](https://github.com/rel-int/CircularEP)

**TL;DR:** Circular predictive coding networks close a shallow chain of learned maps into a
loop, so relaxing the energy iterates the same maps until they reach the fixed point of an
equivalent deep equilibrium model, and local equilibrium-propagation updates approximate its
implicit gradient.

- **Setup:** Image classification on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet32, Sudoku-hard, and
  autoregressive language modeling on TinyStories and WikiText-103, compared with
  predictive-coding VGG-5 models of similar size, larger predictive-coding ResNet-18 and
  VGG-15 models, and DEQs of the same architecture trained with implicit gradients.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Identifying the first and last hidden layers of a shallow network
  closes it into a loop. The paper proves that, with contractive maps, the energy has a unique
  zero-energy equilibrium equal to the DEQ fixed point and that training with equilibrium
  propagation approximates the DEQ's implicit gradient. A recursive variant chains several
  relaxations with the same weights, feeding each answer back in, in the spirit of HRM and TRM.
- **Results:** The dual-readout model reaches 93.54% on CIFAR-10, 72.38% on CIFAR-100 and
  39.31% on ImageNet32 with 1.5M (CIFAR) or 7.2M (ImageNet32) parameters, above the
  similar-size predictive-coding baselines and the matching DEQ. On Sudoku-hard the 105k-parameter
  recursive model solves 85.7% of boards from one view and 99.3% over 1,024 relabelled views,
  against 68.2% and 97.2% for a 1.1M-parameter single-relaxation model; harder boards take more
  sweeps. A one-block circular Transformer reaches 110.83 test perplexity on WikiText-103
  against 112.59 for the DEQ.
- **Caveat:** The Sudoku headline uses 1,024 views with self-cost selection, the language
  models are small (8.7M parameters, 48.1M tokens), and inference is energy relaxation rather
  than plain forward iteration.
- **Why added:** The same learned maps are applied around the loop on every relaxation sweep,
  and the model is formally a deep equilibrium model whose effective depth comes from that
  reuse.

### Mixture of Latent Recursions for Low-Rank Adaptation

[arXiv:2611.27823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27823) · Code: not yet released (the authors
say it will be)

**TL;DR:** MoLR reapplies one shared r-by-r transformation to the low-rank latent of each
LoRA adapter, with a per-token router deciding when to stop, and beats LoRA variants on
language and vision fine-tuning at LoRA's parameter count.

- **Setup:** Fine-tuning RoBERTa-base and DeBERTa-v3-large on seven GLUE tasks, Llama-3.2-3B
  and Qwen3-8B on reasoning, math and code benchmarks, and CLIP ViT-B/16 and SigLIP ViT-L/16 on
  seven image classification datasets, against LoRA, DoRA, AdaLoRA, TopLoRA, HydraLoRA,
  MoSLoRA and AuroRA, all at rank 8.
- **Mechanism / Method:** After the down-projection, the latent state is updated residually by
  the same matrix plus a fixed sinusoidal step encoding. A shared linear router scores each
  token at every step and a token stops for good once its score falls below a threshold, which
  is adjusted toward a uniform spread of stopping depths without an auxiliary loss. The default
  maximum depth is two steps.
- **Results:** GLUE averages are 86.51 with RoBERTa-base (LoRA 84.65) and 91.02 with
  DeBERTa-v3-large (LoRA 88.55); reasoning averages are 61.21 with Llama-3.2-3B (LoRA 59.21).
  On image classification it averages 86.63 with CLIP ViT-B/16 against 84.66 for the next-best
  variant. Raising the inference-time depth cap up to the trained maximum improves accuracy
  without retraining.
- **Caveat:** The recursion is short (two steps by default) and acts on a small linear map
  inside an adapter, and the adapter can no longer be merged into the base weights.
- **Why added:** A learned operator is reapplied inside each forward pass with per-token
  adaptive halting, in the style of Mixture-of-Recursions.

## Near misses

- **MIR-Depth: Multi-Range Refinement for Single-View RGB-D Depth Completion of Transparent
  Objects** ([2611.25417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25417)): refines depth with a RAFT-style
  multi-scale ConvGRU update, but the contribution is a task-specific depth-completion
  pipeline rather than a loop-model result.
- **TwoPass: Draft-Guided Few-Step Video Prediction for Robotic Manipulation**
  ([2611.25553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25553)): feeds the network's own clean-video draft
  back as conditioning across solver steps, which is repeated full-model calls in a flow-matching
  sampler, not reuse of internal layers.
- **A Control-Theoretic Approximation to Predictive Coding Dynamics**
  ([2611.26319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26319)): relates predictive coding to deep feedback
  control in feedforward networks; there is no loop of reused layers.
