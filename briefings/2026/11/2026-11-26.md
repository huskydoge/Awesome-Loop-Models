# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-11-26

## Outcome

Ten papers were added. Three study looped mixture-of-experts language models: a large
budget-matched scaling study, a routing regularizer for shared expert pools, and LoopTitan,
which finds that parameter revisit itself buys almost nothing once shape and optimizer are
controlled. Mixture-of-Loops Transformer adds cross-loop key-value memory. Floodgates and
FlyingStart speed up decoding of existing looped language models. Two recipes post-train looped
models without labels, one to improve accuracy and one to exit earlier. Two papers apply looped
models to multi-view reconstruction and to embodied world modeling.

Three of today's additions cite Benhao Huang's work:

- LoopTitan reimplements the Part I recipe of "Towards Looped Models Done Right" as one of nine
  published looped baselines, lists its public code in a capability matrix, and cites Part II in
  related work.
- FlyingStart contrasts itself with Parts I and II in related work. It notes Part I's finding
  that a random initial state gives mixed returns, and that Part II changes training.
- Learning from Recurrence cites Equilibrium Reasoners (Huang, Geng, Kolter) in its
  introduction, as evidence that recurrent models can learn genuine iterative computation.

## Added

### LoopTitan: What Does Parameter Revisit Buy a Mixture-of-Experts?

[arXiv:2611.34821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34821) · Code:
[datamllab/LoopTitan](https://github.com/datamllab/LoopTitan) (project page:
[datamllab.github.io/LoopTitan](https://datamllab.github.io/LoopTitan/); models:
[NaturalScale](https://huggingface.co/NaturalScale))

**TL;DR:** A controlled decomposition of a state-of-the-art looped MoE recipe finds that
reusing layers adds almost nothing at matched parameters, FLOPs and KV cache; the gains come
from network shape, residual scale and the optimizer.

- **Setup:** MoE models with 1.3B stored and 0.2B active parameters trained on 5B DCLM tokens,
  extended to 10B and 20B tokens and to a 3.0B model. Training uses AdamW and Muon, and test
  bits-per-byte is the metric. Nine published looped recipes are reimplemented as baselines.
- **Mechanism / Method:**
  - The looped MoE recipe is taken apart one change at a time, with revisit last.
  - A non-revisit control runs the same computed depth with independent layers and half the
    experts each.
  - A proposition shows residual scaling acts as a lower learning rate on revisited layers.
    That helps under AdamW, whose per-layer step sizes are uneven, and does nothing under Muon.
- **Results:**
  - The non-revisit control keeps the whole gain over the standard MoE. Revisit costs 0.7 and
    0.6 mBPB under AdamW and Muon, within noise.
  - Switching the baseline to Muon alone gains 23.6 mBPB, 3.7 times the looped recipe's gain
    under AdamW.
  - At fixed parameters with added compute, one extra visit gains 11.5 mBPB under Muon, and a
    deeper untied MoE recovers 90.2% of that.
- **Caveat:** It is small scale (at most 3.0B stored parameters and 24B tokens) on one dataset
  with a fixed visit count; the authors call it a phase 1 report.
- **Why added:** A carefully controlled looped-versus-untied study of layer revisit in MoE
  language models.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** The Part I recipe of "Towards Looped Models Done Right" (tied core
  with input injection) is reimplemented as one of the published looped baselines, and its
  public code is listed in the paper's capability matrix. Part II is cited in related work as
  evidence that more visits help at equal parameters.

### Scaling Looped MoE Language Models under Matched Budgets: Information Flow and Parameter Allocation

[arXiv:2611.34276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34276) · Code:
[lxi01766/Scaling-Looped-MoE-Language-Models-under-Matched-Budgets-Information-Flow-and-Parameter-Allocation](https://github.com/lxi01766/Scaling-Looped-MoE-Language-Models-under-Matched-Budgets-Information-Flow-and-Parameter-Allocation)
(reproduction experiments)

**TL;DR:** Better information flow plus reinvesting the parameters saved by looping into routed
experts lets looped MoE models beat non-recurrent baselines at matched parameters and FLOPs
after 1T tokens.

- **Setup:** A 69B-total MLA MoE and a 51B-total KDA MoE are trained for 1T tokens and
  evaluated on MMLU, CMMLU, C-Eval and BBH. A 200B-token comparison on a common backbone
  includes Parcae and SMELT.
- **Mechanism / Method:**
  - A prelude-core-coda design runs the core twice. The first pass starts from the normalized
    prelude output, and later passes use gated fusion that controls state retention and input
    injection separately.
  - A parameter-free cross-stage residual feeds the prelude output to the coda.
  - The single-pass core is set to 25-35% of the unrolled depth, and the parameters saved by
    sharing go into extra routed experts.
- **Results:**
  - After 1T tokens the averages rise from 67.70 to 68.86 for MLA and from 65.42 to 67.71 for
    KDA.
  - On the common backbone, validation perplexity is 8.31, against 8.36 for Parcae, 8.36 for
    SMELT and 8.49 for the baseline.
- **Caveat:** Only two passes are studied, there are single runs, and inference efficiency is
  not measured.
- **Why added:** A large-scale, budget-matched scaling and design study of looped MoE language
  models.

### DuoMI: Dual Mutual Information Regularization for Expert Reuse in Looped Transformers

[arXiv:2611.33952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33952) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** Two mutual-information penalties on routing make the two layers that share an
expert pool in a looped MoE reuse the same experts more consistently.

- **Setup:** A looped OLMoE in which adjacent middle layers share expert pools, trained on
  FineWeb-Edu and evaluated on 15 downstream tasks. It is compared with the unregularized looped
  model, adapted routing methods and the unshared OLMoE.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A round-level term limits how much aggregate expert usage depends on
  the round. A token-level term keeps the share of experts each token switches between rounds
  inside a target band. Both are computed from soft routing during training only, so inference
  Top-k routing is unchanged.
- **Results:** The average rises from 50.08% to 51.82%, above the unshared OLMoE's 51.15%;
  the best alternative routing method reaches 50.76%.
- **Caveat:** One backbone, one run per configuration, and gains concentrated in SST-2 and BBH.
- **Why added:** Shared expert modules are reused at two depths within one forward pass, and
  the method targets that reuse directly.

### Mixture-of-Loops Transformer

[arXiv:2611.34923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34923) · Code:
[hustvl/MoLT](https://github.com/hustvl/MoLT)

**TL;DR:** MoLT changes what a looped Transformer passes between loops: each layer reads keys
and values from other depths and loops, which improves pretraining at matched parameters.

- **Setup:** 700M and 1.5B dense models and a 3B MoE trained for 400B tokens against OLMo3,
  plus 100B tokens of continued pretraining of Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base.
- **Mechanism / Method:**
  - Mixture-of-Loops Attention reads keys and values across depths and loops in one softmax.
  - MoLT-R keeps the residual stream across loops. MoLT-KV restarts each loop from the
    embeddings and reads the previous loop's keys and values at earlier positions, which allows
    parallel training and single-traversal decoding.
  - U-MoLT loops an encoder and runs a cross-only decoder once.
- **Results:**
  - At 700M the average accuracy rises from 56.6 (OLMo3) to 59.1 for U-MoLT.
  - On the 3B MoE, accuracy rises from 60.1 to 62.2 and mean perplexity falls from 13.99 to
    13.24.
  - U-MoLT-700M reaches OLMo3's 400B-token validation loss at 232.8B tokens.
- **Caveat:** Comparisons match parameters but use about twice the training FLOPs, and only two
  loops are studied.
- **Why added:** A new design for the state and memory carried between loops of a looped
  Transformer, validated at pretraining scale.

### Looped Models are Fast Multi-Token Predictors

[arXiv:2611.33896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33896) · Code:
[MisakiTaro0414/floodgates](https://github.com/MisakiTaro0414/floodgates)

**TL;DR:** Floodgates drafts several future tokens in parallel with one loop of a looped
language model and verifies them with the full loop count, giving lossless speedups of up to
7.5x.

- **Setup:** Seven public looped models (four Ouro variants, Huginn-3.5B and two retrofitted
  Llama and TinyLlama models) on GSM8K, MATH-500, HumanEval+, MBPP+ and BBH. Baselines include
  LoopSpec, WaveFront, DAS and Jacobi decoding.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A block of mask tokens is appended and passed once through the shared
  block. It attends to the final-loop key-value cache computed during the previous verification.
  Small LoRA adapters, active only while drafting, are trained on the model's own outputs, and
  the unchanged full-depth model verifies the drafts.
- **Results:**
  - Speedup reaches up to 7.5x over plain decoding, averaged over each model's benchmarks.
  - On Ouro-1.4B GSM8K it is 5.04x with greedy decoding.
  - Its drafter reaches 5.48x with 61M trained parameters, against 4.32x for a 278M DFlash
    drafter.
- **Caveat:** Under sampling it is fastest in 16 of 24 settings, and each model needs its own
  LoRA drafter.
- **Why added:** A decoding system that relies on shallow loops of a weight-shared block.

### FlyingStart: Learned Cross-Token Warm Starts for Fixed-Point Decoding in Recurrent-Depth Language Models

[arXiv:2611.34492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34492) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** FlyingStart starts each token's recurrence from a linear prediction based on the
previous token's converged state rather than from noise, so convergence-based exits fire
earlier with unchanged accuracy.

- **Setup:** Huginn-0125 plus three retrofitted recurrent-depth models, evaluated on GSM8K, SVAMP,
  MultiArith and ASDiv against the state-change exit and other training-free exits.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A linear map is fitted in closed form by ridge regression on the
  frozen model. It costs under 5% of one loop. Because the iteration and exit rule are
  unchanged, a contraction argument guarantees the same fixed point from any start and predicts
  how many loops are saved.
- **Results:**
  - FlyingStart matches the strongest training-free baseline with 14-19% fewer decoding layers.
  - Batched throughput rises by 16-18% in two serving engines, and by a further 10-14% on top of
    WaveFront self-speculative decoding.
  - The analysis predicts 1.8 saved loops on Huginn against 1.7 measured.
- **Caveat:** It only helps models with a convergent fixed point, not Ouro, and it needs
  per-model calibration.
- **Why added:** Initialization of the recurrent state in fixed-point decoding for
  recurrent-depth models.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** Related work cites Part I of "Towards Looped Models Done Right" for
  the finding that a random initial state gives mixed returns in controlled pretraining. It cites
  Part II among methods that change training, against FlyingStart's inference-only warm start.

### Learning from Recurrence: Internalizing Depth-Induced Preferences in Looped Language Models

[arXiv:2611.33468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33468) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** Training only the shared block of a looped language model, through a single pass, on
its own deeper-loop continuations improves both shallow and deep accuracy without labels.

- **Setup:** OLMo-2-1B is retrofitted with a four-block shared core and mid-trained for 50B
  tokens. It is evaluated on six math benchmarks against a non-looped control matched on data
  and tokens, and against distillation and self-training baselines.
- **Mechanism / Method:** The frozen model generates greedy continuations at a deeper teacher
  depth, with no reference answers or correctness filter. Only the shared core (18% of
  parameters) is then trained on these continuations through a shallow depth, one pass by
  default.
- **Results:**
  - On GSM8K, one-pass accuracy rises from 40.86% to 48.52% and four-pass accuracy from 56.18%
    to 62.70%.
  - The mean relative gain over the non-looped control reaches up to 46.2%.
  - Using the updated block only on passes two through four recovers 6.29 of the 6.52-point gain.
- **Caveat:** It is math-only at 1B scale. The one-pass advantage disappears without input
  injection and does not help Ouro at four passes.
- **Why added:** Post-training aimed at the shared block of a retrofitted looped model.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** Equilibrium Reasoners (Huang, Geng, Kolter) is cited in the
  introduction, among works showing that recurrent models can learn genuine iterative
  computation in controlled settings.

### Practice Makes Fluent: Learning to Use Earlier Predictions in Looped Models

[arXiv:2611.33722](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33722) · Code: not stated in the paper (project
page: [msseok96-cpu.github.io/FLUENT](https://msseok96-cpu.github.io/FLUENT/))

**TL;DR:** FLUENT teaches looped models to stop at earlier loops through practice, with no labels
or rewards, and halves real-robot episode time while raising success.

- **Setup:** Ouro-1.4B and Ouro-2.6B agents in Crafter, the recurrent action head of RD-VLA on
  LIBERO and a real 6-DoF arm, and Ouro-1.4B on language benchmarks. Baselines are on-policy
  distillation and RL methods adapted to deep-teacher, shallow-student training.
- **Mechanism / Method:** The frozen initial model's final-loop prediction is distilled into
  early loops. The current final loop is anchored to the same teacher, because shared weights
  couple the loops. An exit gate trades disagreement with the final loop against depth cost.
- **Results:**
  - On LIBERO it reaches 93.5% success at 3.97 iterations, against 91.9% at 6.27.
  - On the real robot, success rises from 62.3% to 74.6% and mean time falls from 69 s to 31 s.
  - On language tasks the average is held at 65.4 while mean loops fall by 18.7%.
- **Caveat:** The frozen initial teacher bounds what can be learned, and with Ouro's dense
  readout an early exit saves compute only under a ragged key-value cache.
- **Why added:** Learned early exit over a reused loop block.

### Where to Approximate: Understanding and Accelerating Looped Multi-View Reconstruction

[arXiv:2611.33948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33948) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** The paper traces how cheap approximations at single steps of a looped 3D
reconstructor propagate, and turns the findings into a faster per-step schedule.

- **Setup:** The 117M-parameter looped reconstructor Déjà View on DTU, ETH3D, 7-Scenes,
  ScanNet++ and nuScenes, against stacked reconstructors (VGGT, π3, DA3) and accelerated
  baselines.
- **Mechanism / Method:**
  - Paired clean and perturbed runs show that later steps absorb errors, that spatial
    approximations are cheapest early, and that reusing attention outputs compounds errors.
  - The resulting schedule runs coarse-grid steps first, then key-value-pooled steps, then full
    steps interleaved with reuse. It is adapted by self-distillation with time-interpolated LoRA.
- **Results:**
  - Skipping the worst single step costs a median of 0.18 AUC points in the looped model,
    against 48.8-82.2 points in stacked models.
  - The balanced schedule beats a latency-matched plain baseline by 4.73 AUC@30 points.
  - In a tied-versus-untied control the tied model scores 53.2 AUC against 46.2 and 47.7.
- **Caveat:** It studies a single looped model, and losses concentrate on some datasets, such as
  ETH3D.
- **Why added:** An analysis of error propagation through a reused block, plus loop-specific
  acceleration.

### Loop4D: Embodied World Modeling with 4D VAEs and Looped Diffusion Transformers

[arXiv:2611.34199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34199) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** Loop4D predicts future RGB and 3D pointmaps of robot manipulation with 4D VAEs and a
diffusion Transformer that loops its pretrained middle blocks within each denoising call.

- **Setup:** Robot4DGen simulated and real manipulation tasks, with 1.3B and 5B models compared
  against large video generators, 4D generators and non-looped versions of the same backbone.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Separate RGB and geometry streams share the looped middle blocks and
  exchange information through a learned connection. Each loop is supervised, and a penalty
  stops the robot region from degrading between loops. All loops run at one noise level inside
  one denoising call.
- **Results:**
  - At 5B, looping lowers gFVD from 618.0 to 511.2 against the non-looped model.
  - In simulation, Loop4D-1.3B reaches gFVD 449.3 against 502.8 for LTX-2.3 22B.
  - Simulated grasp success averages 33.3%, against 16.7% for Robot4DGen.
- **Caveat:** Gains stop after two loops and looping adds about 66% compute. Some numbers
  disagree between tables, and the robot evaluation uses 10 trials per task.
- **Why added:** Block reuse within a single denoiser call, applied to embodied world modeling.

## Near misses

- **Does the Mixer Matter?** ([2611.30623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30623)): mostly about
  experts and mixers in tiny bilingual models. Reusing a block three times is one small
  ablation that replicates earlier looped-Transformer results.
- **Quaternion-Tucker ZigZag Networks** ([2611.33983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33983)): layers
  share a factorized basis but keep distinct per-layer cores and run once each; an actual loop
  appears only in a toy ablation.
- **On the Importance of Hidden-State Refinement in Masked Diffusion Language Models**
  ([2611.32416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32416)): carries hidden states across denoising steps
  rather than reusing layers within one pass.
- **Yggdrasil** ([2611.29954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29954)): a sequence recurrence with
  routed recurrent experts; it does not feed computed states back through the same layers.
