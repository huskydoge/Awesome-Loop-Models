# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-11-04

## Outcome

Seven papers were added. Two release new pretrained looped language models (Dory1.0-4B and
ARLoop), and three make existing looped models cheaper to serve (LoopVQ, LoopKV and an
exactness-graded serving study). Best-of-Depth changes how the loop is trained, and CoReMatch
brings a weight-shared recurrent update to robotic feature matching. ARLoop cites Benhao
Huang's *Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II* when describing its Parcae baseline, as
evidence that input injection helps looped transformers.

## Added

### Dory1.0-4B: A Looped Normalized-GPT
[arXiv:2611.03998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03998) · Models: [huggingface.co/collections/nvidia/nemotron-labs-dory](https://huggingface.co/collections/nvidia/nemotron-labs-dory)

**TL;DR:** A 4B looped language model whose single checkpoint serves different compute
budgets; with two or more loops it beats larger open models on a 12-benchmark average.

- **Setup:** A normalized-Transformer (nGPT) backbone with interleaved sliding-window and full
  attention, pre-trained on 6T tokens (the first 4.5T in NVFP4 as a non-looped model), then
  extended to 256K context and post-trained with SFT and RLVR.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A 16-layer input block, a shared 40-layer recurrent block and a
  16-layer output block; the recurrent block runs k times with all parameters shared and the
  input re-injected before each loop. Loop counts are sampled from 1-4 during the looped
  continuation, and an RLVR curriculum routes harder prompts to deeper loops. Keys and values
  are cached only from the last loop.
- **Results:** The 12-benchmark average is 35.17 at one loop, 39.67 at two and 40.60 at four,
  against 32.21 for Qwen3.5-9B and 36.73 for Gemma 4 26B-A4B. From one to four loops,
  SWE-Bench Verified rises from 50.3 to 61.8 and Terminal-Bench v2.1 from 12.5 to 22.2. Four
  loops generate 1.3-2.5x fewer tokens on agentic tasks.
- **Caveat:** There is no non-looped baseline trained the same way at matched parameters or
  compute, and loops were added only after a long non-looped pretraining run. Gains saturate
  beyond four loops.
- **Why added:** A large open looped model that reuses one learned block several times per
  forward pass.

### ARLoop: Fully-Open Pre-trained Looped Transformers with Attention Residuals
[arXiv:2611.05234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05234) · Code: [github.com/OSU-NLP-Group/ARLoop](https://github.com/OSU-NLP-Group/ARLoop)

**TL;DR:** Lets each layer of a looped transformer attend over earlier depth blocks instead of
summing them into one residual stream, and releases fully open 0.6B and 1.2B looped models.

- **Setup:** Controlled runs at 140M and 370M parameters on FineWeb-Edu with 1-5 recurrences,
  then 0.6B (180B tokens) and 1.2B (500B tokens) models with three recurrences on a
  SmolLM-like recipe.
- **Mechanism / Method:** The whole stack is looped with all layers shared. Block attention
  residuals give each effective sublayer a learned query over residual blocks across depth;
  a sliding window keeps the embedding and the three most recent blocks.
- **Results:** At 140M with three recurrences, validation perplexity is 16.38 versus 16.91 for
  a PreNorm loop; at 370M it is 12.17 versus 12.48. ARLoop-0.6B beats PreNormLoop-0.6B on 7 of
  8 tasks (GSM8K 40.9 vs 35.7). ARLoop-1.2B scores 63.1 on GSM8K, 37.8 on MATH and 52.1 on MBPP,
  against 26.1, 14.1 and 33.3 for Huginn-3.5B.
- **Caveat:** An untied 24-layer model with the same depth has much lower full-history
  perplexity (13.65 vs 16.07), Ouro-1.4B beats ARLoop-1.2B on most tasks, and accuracy does not
  improve beyond the trained recurrence count.
- **Why added:** A residual design for weight-tied, whole-stack looped transformers.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** *Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II: Rethinking at Fixed
  Points* (Huang et al., arXiv:2610.06833), cited in the Parcae baseline description as
  evidence that input injection benefits looped transformers.

### LoopVQ: Sub 1-bit KV Cache Quantization for Looped Transformers
[arXiv:2611.05304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05304) · Code: [github.com/mscheong01/LoopVQ](https://github.com/mscheong01/LoopVQ)

**TL;DR:** Quantizes a looped model's KV cache jointly along the loop axis, keeping most of
the BF16 accuracy at 0.5 bits per element.

- **Setup:** Ouro-1.4B and Ouro-2.6B (four loops), their Thinking variants, and Huginn-3.5B
  (32 core iterations), evaluated on general, long chain-of-thought and long-context tasks
  and served in SGLang.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Because every loop step's cache comes from the same weights,
  adjacent steps are highly correlated. One vector-quantization code covers channels of four
  consecutive loop steps, with codebooks fit by Fisher-weighted k-means and then fine-tuned
  to match the BF16 model's outputs.
- **Results:** On Ouro-1.4B at 0.5 bits, the average is 68.29 for LoopVQ and 73.58 combined
  with TaSQ, against 75.67 for BF16 and 43.62 for TaSQ alone. Decode latency roughly halves at
  32K context, and peak serving throughput rises up to 4.3x on Ouro-1.4B and 6.1x on Ouro-2.6B.
- **Caveat:** Speedups are measured against BF16 only, there is no accuracy comparison with
  earlier KV quantizers for looped models, and calibration data come from GPQA.
- **Why added:** A KV-cache method built around the loop structure of weight-tied models.

### LoopKV: Loop-Aware KV Cache Compression for Serving Looped Transformers
[arXiv:2611.04137](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04137)

**TL;DR:** Chooses 4-bit, 2-bit or final-loop KV reuse separately for each loop and layer of
Ouro, and serves the plan with one fused vLLM kernel.

- **Setup:** Ouro-1.4B and Ouro-2.6B with four loops, plus Dory-4B in its last-loop mode, on
  RTX PRO 6000, RTX PRO 4500, A100 and H100 GPUs; benchmarks include RULER, LongBench,
  MATH500, GSM8K and a latency-bounded AIME serving workload.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A one-time offline profile measures damage for each (loop, layer)
  cell; a knapsack planner assigns each cell a storage option under damage and memory
  budgets. At decode time each loop reads the high 2 bits of each value and fetches the low
  bits only where its own attention needs them.
- **Results:** LoopKV-M stores 35% less KV than uniform 4-bit KIVI and sustains 1.7-2.2x its
  goodput under a latency target with 16K maximum length. On Ouro-1.4B it decodes 3.37x
  faster than BF16 at 8K. No suite average falls significantly below BF16.
- **Caveat:** Only Ouro is served end to end, and LoopKV-M loses 21.2 points on RULER-32K
  variable tracking. Code is promised but not yet linked.
- **Why added:** A serving system specialized to per-loop KV caches of a weight-tied model.

### What Sets the Serving Speed of Looped Language Models? Measurements and Optimizations Graded by Exactness
[arXiv:2611.05150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05150)

**TL;DR:** Finds that the many small per-loop kernels, not loop submissions, set looped-model
serving speed, and grades each optimization by whether it keeps outputs bitwise identical.

- **Setup:** Ouro-1.4B (depth 4) and Huginn-0125 (depth 32) in the open-source vllm-rlt engine
  on GH200 and RTX 5090, with random bursts, coding-agent traces and prefill-decode
  disaggregation.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Changes range from exact (fused normalization, RoPE and activation
  kernels with rounding fences, storing only live KV slots) to ones that need an accuracy
  check (approximate fusion, packed prefill).
- **Results:** Fusing loop submissions alone leaves batch-1 Ouro throughput at 0.99x. Exact
  kernel fusion cuts kernels per token from 5689 to 2885 and raises throughput 1.11-1.59x with
  1014/1014 requests identical. Released defaults serve Huginn 1.77-3.16x and Ouro 1.65-2.15x
  faster than the base engine, and disaggregated serving reaches up to 3.3x.
- **Caveat:** Small models on few machines at fixed depth, many single-run cells, and no
  comparison with a non-looped model of matched quality.
- **Why added:** A systems study of serving models that reuse one block many times per token.

### Best-of-Depth: Learning Exploratory Dynamics in Looped Transformers
[arXiv:2611.04663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04663)

**TL;DR:** Trains a looped transformer with the loss at its best depth, so the recurrent
trajectory covers several valid solutions instead of converging on one.

- **Setup:** A small looped transformer trained at 16 loops and read out to 64 on N-Queens
  completion and graph 3-coloring, plus fine-tuning Huginn-3.5B on code at depth 32.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A shared transformation is applied repeatedly with input
  injection, and the loss is the minimum over depths of the per-depth loss, with no
  architectural change or depth-specific labels.
- **Results:** N-Queens coverage reaches 91.03%, versus 45.75% for final-depth training; graph
  coloring coverage is 72.16% versus 44.39% for a soft-depth objective. Fine-tuned Huginn reaches
  pooled code success of 37.8 on HumanEval and 44.2 on MBPP, versus 34.8 and 39.2.
- **Caveat:** Small combinatorial tasks and one pretrained model; coverage assumes an external
  verifier, and code gains come from pooling programs generated at separate depths.
- **Why added:** A training objective for the recurrent trajectory of weight-tied looped
  models.

### CoReMatch: Recurrent Continuous Coordinate Refinement for Real-Time Robotic Feature Matching
[arXiv:2611.03932](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03932) · Code: [github.com/edge-cv/CoReMatch](https://github.com/edge-cv/CoReMatch)

**TL;DR:** Refines feature correspondences directly in continuous coordinates with a
recurrent update whose parameters are shared across iterations, giving real-time matching
on embedded hardware.

- **Setup:** Trained on MegaDepth and evaluated on MegaDepth, ScanNet, HPatches and KITTI
  visual odometry, with timing on a Jetson AGX Orin.
- **Mechanism / Method:** At the coarse stage, a recurrent update operator with a persistent
  hidden state re-samples features at the current coordinates and predicts coordinate
  offsets; its parameters are shared across iterations, so the step count can change at
  runtime. A lightweight regression head then sharpens localization.
- **Results:** CoReMatch-L reaches MegaDepth AUC@5/10/20 of 59.5/74.4/84.8 at 639 ms, versus
  56.4/72.2/83.5 at 2067 ms for Efficient LoFTR; CoReMatch-S gets 57.4/72.8/83.7 at 177 ms. With
  the same model, reprojection AUC@3px rises from 29.2 with no iterations to 95.9 with three.
- **Caveat:** At most three iterations are used, and the fine stage runs once.
- **Why added:** A RAFT-style weight-shared recurrent update applied to 3D geometry.

## Near misses

- **LINDA** ([2611.04233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04233)): unrolls a small Liquid
  Time-Constant cell for four Euler steps on a pooled feature vector to condition a
  segmentation decoder; the loop is an ODE solve on one global vector rather than a reused
  block refining the representation, and weight sharing is not stated.
- **Attending to Memory Writes Reduces Seed Variability in Recurrent Length Generalization**
  ([2611.04902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04902)): studies recurrence across sequence
  positions in a scan-based memory, not reuse of layers over depth.
- **Anchor** ([2611.04327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04327)): shares one recurrent memory
  state across layers of a linearized transformer, but no layer or block is applied repeatedly.
