# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-11-08

## Outcome

Four papers were added. Two are about the looped block itself (how a looped stack weights its
past updates, and how to retrofit a loop into a pretrained decision model), one is about
serving looped language models, and one is a vision paper that redesigns the token mixer for
weight-shared depth. Two of them cite Benhao Huang's work. RELOAD cites *Equilibrium
Reasoners* as related work on adaptive computation and follows the input-injection design of
*Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part I* in its ablations. LoopDuet cites *Towards Looped
Models Done Right, Part II* as background on sharing one KV cache across loops.

## Added

### Loopback Transformer: Towards a Unified View of Residual Looped Transformers
[arXiv:2611.07226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07226) · Code: [yancyou/Loopback](https://github.com/yancyou/Loopback)

**TL;DR:** Loopback reads the update each block application adds, not its full output, and
weights those updates with geometric decay and a learned attention reader. It reaches lower
validation loss than standard Transformers with 40-75% of their Transformer parameters.

- **Setup:** Pretraining on ClimbMix at 191M, 374M, 778M, 1.4B and 3B reference scales, with
  20 tokens per parameter. Training tokens and executed depth are matched against a standard
  untied Transformer and seven looped residual designs (Plain Loop, HC, mHC, iHC, Hyperloop,
  MHAR, AttnRes).
- **Mechanism / Method:** Huginn's prelude-core-coda layout, in which a shared middle stack is
  visited a fixed number of times. A full block output also contains its input, so weighting it
  reweights the history the input already carries. Loopback instead keeps the shared stack's
  input on a separate path and stores each application's update. Geometric decay sets the total
  update weight and adds a recency prior, and a zero-initialized single-head softmax reader with
  a learned query per application decides how that weight is spread across updates.
- **Results:**
  - Validation loss against the standard Transformer: 2.6187 vs 2.6234 at 191M (40.0% of its
    parameters) and 1.9849 vs 2.0005 at 3B (75.0%).
  - Thirteen-task mean accuracy: 46.50 vs 46.33 at 1.4B and 49.45 vs 48.56 at 3B.
  - At 191M, 374M and 778M it has the lowest loss and highest task mean of all looped designs;
    the closest, mHC, trails by 0.006-0.011 nats.
  - Overhead is 0.04-0.08% of FLOPs, and 3B training throughput is about 6% lower.
- **Caveat:** One dataset, and the large-scale comparisons are single runs. At 191M the gain
  over the standard model is not consistent across seeds, and the 3B downstream gain (+0.89
  points) is about twice its bootstrap standard error. Extra visits beyond training did not
  improve loss, and parameter savings need not reduce latency or peak memory.
- **Why added:** A tied middle stack is reused within one forward pass, and the paper's
  contribution is how that loop's residual stream is stored and read.

### Not All Loops Cost the Same: Phase-Dependent Depth for Serving Looped Transformers
[arXiv:2611.07211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07211)

**TL;DR:** LoopDuet serves looped language models with fewer core loops for ordinary prompt
tokens than for decoded tokens and runs the remaining loops on compact batches, giving Huginn
19% more HotpotQA throughput at the same accuracy criterion.

- **Setup:** Huginn-0125 and recurrent OLMo-2 retrofits, served with vLLM in bf16 on one
  A100-80GB, on full HotpotQA (distractor) plus GSM8K and ARC-Challenge. Baselines are uniform
  loop depths and fixed prefill-depth controls.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Training-free. A prefill depth and a decode depth are chosen together
  from offline quality-throughput sweeps. Prompt-chunk endpoints keep the decode depth so the
  tokens that start decoding see full-depth state. Tokens that still need loops are gathered
  into compact batches over one shared KV plane per core layer, and a queue-age controller lowers
  or restores the prefill depth as load changes.
- **Results:**
  - Huginn: 33.69 EM at 1.82 req/s against 33.52 EM at 1.54 req/s for the fastest qualifying
    uniform setting (+19% throughput).
  - A faster operating point gains 6.04 EM over uniform depth 8 at 97% of its throughput.
  - On two OLMo arrival traces, adaptive prefill gains 0.92 and 1.35 EM over the most accurate
    fixed-depth controls at 100% attainment of a 1 s time-to-first-token target.
- **Caveat:** Gains need long prompts and short answers: GSM8K wall-time ratios stay at
  0.98-1.01, and ARC loses accuracy. Single-GPU experiments only, KV entries from shallower
  prefill only approximate full-depth context, and a 20/80 burst trace reaches only 87.5%
  attainment. No code release is stated.
- **Why added:** A systems paper about scheduling the recurrent core of looped language models,
  whose shared block runs repeatedly within each forward pass.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** *Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II: Rethinking at Fixed
  Points* (arXiv:2610.06833), cited as background on cache layouts that reuse one KV cache
  across loops and in related work on fixed-point shaping for terminal KV sharing.

### Extending Reasoning in Typed Decision Models via Looping
[arXiv:2611.07736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07736)

**TL;DR:** RELOAD fine-tunes pretrained encoders and decoders with a damped, weight-tied loop
over their middle block plus a per-input pass controller, improving accuracy and calibration
most on reasoning chains longer than any seen in training.

- **Setup:** Six typed decision models that score a fixed set of answer options in one forward
  pass: Von (395M ModernBERT-large), Ettin-400M and Ettin-1B encoders, and Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B
  decoders. They are fine-tuned on a mixture of classification, QA and synthetic reasoning
  sets, and evaluated in distribution, on longer StepGame chains (6-10 statements) and on
  out-of-distribution logic benchmarks.
- **Mechanism / Method:** About the middle third of the layers forms a looped block with no new
  parameters. Each pass is an explicit-Euler step with step size one over the budget, so one
  pass recovers the original network. All budgets from one to four passes are trained at every
  step with cross-entropy plus a Brier loss. A CPU logistic-regression controller on
  prefix-output features picks the number of passes per request before the loop runs.
- **Results:**
  - On longer reasoning chains, encoders gain 10-15 points and Qwen3 models 2.5-10. On chains
    longer than any seen in training, encoders gain 17-23 points.
  - Von goes from 70.6 to 85.7 on longer reasoning, and its Brier score drops from 0.404 to
    0.198.
  - Untied copies of the block for passes two to four (+331M parameters at the same inference
    cost) reach 84.3 against 85.7. Twelve single-pass epochs at matched training compute stay
    more than 10 points behind.
  - The controller stays within one point of four passes in every group at about three quarters
    of their compute.
- **Caveat:** Little gain on knowledge-heavy multiple choice, and decoders gain much less than
  encoders. Longer-reasoning tests are templated or synthetic. Most controls use Von and one
  seed, and some out-of-distribution sets get worse. No code release is stated.
- **Why added:** A pretrained middle block is reused with tied weights within one forward pass,
  with learned depth selection and an untied control at the same inference depth.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** *Equilibrium Reasoners: Learning Attractors Enables Scalable
  Reasoning* (Huang, Geng, Kolter; ICML 2026), cited in related work on fixed-point reasoners
  that stop once a learned fixed point is reached. *Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part I*
  (arXiv:2610.23036): its input-injection design is followed and ablated. Injection did not
  reliably help in any variant tested, and the authors note they do not test looped models
  trained from scratch as Part I does.

### Beyond Quadratic Attention: Separable Gaussian Operators for ODE Vision Transformers
[arXiv:2611.08236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08236)

**TL;DR:** SSoG replaces dense self-attention with a learned mixture of axis-factorized
Gaussian kernels. The mixer is globally Lipschitz, which suits blocks applied many times, and
it improves CIFAR-100 accuracy in standard, looped and Neural ODE Vision Transformers.

- **Setup:** ViTs at three widths trained on CIFAR-100 at 224x224, plus one ImageNet-1k table,
  in three settings: 12 untied blocks, one shared block run 12 times, and an ODE-ViT whose vector
  field reuses the same parameters at every function evaluation. The only baseline is dense
  attention.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Swapping dot-product attention for a separable sum-of-Gaussians
  operator cuts the per-layer cost from quadratic to subquadratic in token count. With fixed
  kernels the mixer is globally Lipschitz, so by Picard-Lindelöf the continuous-depth model has a
  unique solution over the whole integration interval.
- **Results:**
  - CIFAR-100 Top-1 rises 5.4-8.1 points in the looped setting, and 2.2-4.1 points in the
    untied and continuous settings with 8.8-16.6% fewer parameters.
  - At up to 1024x1024 inputs, the ODE model reaches up to 154% higher throughput and over 60%
    lower peak memory than dense attention.
- **Caveat:** Results come mainly from CIFAR-100 with a single seed. On ImageNet-1k the looped
  SSoG model trails dense attention (73.37 vs 73.72), and the looped variant uses more memory
  than dense attention. The mixer is adapted from an earlier operator; the paper's own
  contribution is its analysis and its use in weight-shared and continuous-depth models.
- **Why added:** Its looped and ODE settings reuse one learned block within a forward pass, and
  the token mixer is analyzed for exactly that repeated application.

## Near misses

- **LatentRec** ([2611.07866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07866)): a small thought module
  iterates over the LLM's hidden state only during training and is discarded at inference, so
  the deployed model reuses no layer.
- **RecSA: Recursive Sparse Attention** ([2611.07232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07232)):
  "recursive" refers to a tree of KV summaries; one pooler per layer is shared across tree
  levels, but no layer or block is re-applied to a hidden state.
- **Truncated BPTT turnpike** ([2611.07415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07415)): gradient
  theory for ordinary sequence-recurrent networks, without depth reuse within one forward pass.
