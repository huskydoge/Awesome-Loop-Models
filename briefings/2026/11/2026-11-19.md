# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-11-19

## Outcome

Three papers were added. STRM shows that a TRM-style puzzle solver does not need two states and
nested loops if its single state averages the shared network's outputs at many timescales.
LoopCache makes wave-style speculative decoding of looped Transformers practical at long context
by cutting the KV-cache traffic it multiplies. The third is a smaller vision study that ties
repeated EfficientNet blocks to shared kernels after training. *Simpler Tiny Recursive Models*
cites Benhao Huang's *Equilibrium Reasoners* as related work on attractor-based recursive
reasoners.

## Added

### Simpler Tiny Recursive Models

[arXiv:2611.23677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23677) · Code: not yet released (the authors plan
to release it on acceptance)

**TL;DR:** STRM loops one small network over a single state whose channels are moving averages
at different rates, and at TRM's parameter count and compute it reaches 92.1% on Sudoku-Extreme
against 88.3% for a tuned TRM.

- **Setup:** Sudoku-Extreme (1,000 training puzzles), six 10x10 puzzle types from PPBench,
  Maze-Hard and ARC-AGI-1/2. STRM and TRM share the same two-block network (5.03M parameters on
  Sudoku), training recipe, deep supervision and number of network calls per segment.
- **Mechanism / Method:** TRM alternates updates of a latent state and an answer state in two
  nested loops. STRM applies one shared network K times to one state, mixing each output in as
  s ← (1−a)⊙s + a⊙f(RMSNorm(s)+x) with fixed, log-spaced per-channel rates a that are jittered
  in training. The answer and halting heads read only the slow channels, so fast channels act as
  working memory.
- **Results:** Sudoku-Extreme best checkpoint 92.1% vs 88.3% for TRM; final weights 91.2% vs
  77.3%, because TRM's held-out accuracy collapses late in training. PPBench test 84.7% vs 76.3%
  at the best evaluation. Maze-Hard is about even. ARC-AGI-1/2 pass@2 50.0% and 8.3% vs TRM's
  published 44.6% and 7.8%. Without the averaging (all rates 1), Sudoku-Extreme accuracy falls
  to 55.0%.
- **Caveat:** Sudoku and Maze results use two seeds and the PPBench TRM baseline one seed.
  ARC-AGI is compared with published TRM numbers, not rerun.
- **Why added:** A single learned network is applied many times within one forward pass of a
  TRM-family recursive reasoner; the paper changes what state that loop carries.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** Cites *Equilibrium Reasoners* (Huang, Geng, Kolter) in related work,
  as an attractor-based successor of TRM.

### LoopCache: Accelerating Long-Context Self-Speculative Decoding for Looped Transformers

[arXiv:2611.24301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24301) · Code:
[zZzZ9zZ9/LoopCache](https://github.com/zZzZ9zZ9/LoopCache)

**TL;DR:** LoopCache drafts tokens from every intermediate loop of a looped Transformer and
cycles through loop-specific KV caches so that each step reads only one, averaging 10.25x and
14.56x speedups over WaveFront Decoding and LoopSpec on long contexts.

- **Setup:** Six looped models (Ouro-1.4B and 2.6B with 4 loops; Huginn-3.5B and three Raven
  models with 32 loops) on LongBench v1/v2, RULER and LongReason at 4K-128K context on eight
  A100s. Baselines are autoregressive decoding, draft-then-verify, DFlash, WaveFront Decoding
  and LoopSpec.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Each loop of the shared block keeps its own KV cache, so wave-style
  decoders that batch tokens at different loops read up to R caches per step. LoopCache (1)
  replaces an in-flight draft when an intermediate loop predicts a different token with a
  large enough logit gap, (2) rotates through the loop caches so each step reads one full
  cache while verification is batched at the matching cycle, keeping outputs lossless, and (3)
  keeps separate caches only for the first two layers, whose KV states change most across loops.
- **Results:** Average speedups of 5.12x over autoregressive decoding, 2.52x over
  draft-then-verify, 10.25x over WaveFront Decoding and 14.56x over LoopSpec. Ablations credit
  each of the three components with additional speedup.
- **Caveat:** Per-model and per-length speedups are shown only in figures, and the largest
  gains are over wave-style baselines that the paper itself shows slowing down as context grows.
- **Why added:** It is a decoding system built on the shared recurrent block of looped
  Transformers and on the per-loop KV caches that reuse creates.

### Where Global Context and Weight Sharing Work in Compact Vision Backbones

[arXiv:2611.23446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23446) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** Tying repeated MBConv blocks of a trained EfficientNet-B0 to two alternating shared
templates with per-pass BatchNorm removes 10.9% of parameters and costs 1.84 ImageNet points
after one epoch of continued training.

- **Setup:** EfficientNet-B0 on ImageNet-1k, trained for 88 epochs and evaluated on all
  50,000 validation images. Half of the paper tests attention adapters; the other half tests
  weight sharing across repeated blocks, starting from the trained checkpoint.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Repeated blocks in a stage reuse the convolution and
  Squeeze-and-Excitation kernels of one block, while each pass keeps its own BatchNorm
  (+1,536 parameters per pass). The tested patterns are one template per stage ([B1, A, A, A])
  and two alternating templates in Stage 6 ([B1, A, B, A]), initialized by copying a block.
- **Results:** Against an unshared control at 72.76%, single-template tying of Stages 4-6
  (-27.6% parameters) recovers to 66.10% after one epoch, and the alternating Stage 6 pair
  (-10.9%) recovers to 70.92%. Averaging the kernels of the blocks instead of copying one drops
  accuracy to 17.03%. The attention adapters add 0.954 points over a matched plain B0.
- **Caveat:** Single seed, a one-epoch warm-start budget, and a shortened EfficientNet recipe;
  sharing is applied after training rather than learned from scratch.
- **Why added:** One learned block is reused at several depths of the same forward pass, and
  the paper measures what that costs in a compact CNN.

## Near misses

- **Rethinking Fixed-Horizon Latent Reasoning** ([2611.24001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24001)):
  adaptive stopping for Coconut, CODI and SIM-CoT latent chain-of-thought, which feeds hidden
  states back through the full model as new inputs rather than reusing internal layers.
- **Evaluating invariance training with certified answer-changing controls in neural graph
  algorithms** ([2611.23907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23907)): tests invariance training of a
  graph transformer on minimum spanning trees; the model is not a shared processor that is
  executed repeatedly.
