# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-11-18

## Outcome

Five papers were added. One changes what state a parallel looped Transformer passes between
loops so that it keeps improving up to eight loops. Two compress existing looped models: LoopZip
drops visual tokens in looped vision-language models, and LoopPrune prunes looped diffusion
Transformers with one mask shared by every loop position. One replaces the deep-supervision
target of TRM-style refiners when a puzzle has many correct answers. The last is a controlled
study of when looped transformers compose two stored facts. None of the five papers cites
Benhao Huang's work.

## Added

### InfuseLoop: Cross-Trajectory Latent Injection for Scalable Parallel Looped Transformers

[arXiv:2611.22426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22426) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** InfuseLoop gives each loop of a parallel looped Transformer the hidden states that
later token trajectories have already computed, not just its own previous loop state. This
removes training-loss spikes, and at eight loops it lowers perplexity by 3.4-17.1% over vanilla
parallel loops.

- **Setup:** A Qwen3-0.6B-shaped model (28 layers, width 1,024) trained from scratch on 6B
  FineWeb-Edu tokens, with 4 or 8 loops. It is compared with a non-looped model, a sequential
  looped Transformer and a vanilla parallel looped Transformer (PLT), all with the same backbone,
  on WikiText-2 and LAMBADA perplexity and seven downstream benchmarks.
- **Mechanism / Method:** In a PLT the whole 28-layer stack is reused, and the loops of
  successive tokens are pipelined so that different tokens run different loop stages at once.
  InfuseLoop combines the shallower-loop outputs that are already available from later
  trajectories through a small attention residual (a learned query per receiving loop) and uses
  the result as the loop input. It adds about 7K parameters, keeps token-parallel training and
  needs only a small rolling buffer of hidden states.
- **Results:** At eight loops, perplexity falls by 3.4-17.1% against vanilla PLTs and by
  10.5-37.3% against the non-looped model, and average downstream accuracy rises by 0.84 and
  1.99 points. Validation loss is 2.9058 against 2.9365 for PLT-8 and 2.8775 for a sequential
  8-loop model. InfuseLoop closes 52-61% of the gap between PLT and the sequential loop, while
  the sequential 8-loop model decodes about 7.7x slower. Decoding costs 0.3% more time than a
  vanilla PLT and 23.3% more than the non-looped model.
- **Caveat:** Only dense ~0.6B models are tested, gains from 4 to 8 loops are small on
  downstream accuracy, and the sequential looped model still has lower loss. Looped and
  non-looped models share parameter counts, not compute.
- **Why added:** It reuses one Transformer stack across pipelined loops and proposes a new form
  of state passing between loop iterations, filed under recurrent state and folding depth into
  time.

### Proximal Targets: Training Looped Refiners on Problems with Many Correct Answers

[arXiv:2611.22737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22737) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** When a puzzle has many correct answers, training every pass of a TRM-style refiner
toward one stored answer sets a loss floor and blends answers into invalid grids. Training each
pass toward the correct answer nearest its own input instead lets the same model solve 98.8% of
multi-answer Sudoku puzzles.

- **Setup:** A TRM-style network (two Transformer blocks of width 256, two latent states, 8
  passes; 1.7M parameters) on Sudoku with 2-64 answers, sparse Sudoku with over 10,000 answers,
  the Nandwani et al. benchmark, planted 3-colouring and Scrabble, with 5 seeds. Baselines
  include the standard target, MinLoss, GRAM and ConsFormer.
- **Mechanism / Method:** The same small network is applied repeatedly to a draft answer. The
  proximal target for each pass is the correct answer closest to that pass's input, found by
  scanning an answer list, by a search solver started from the draft, or within a quality band
  set by a knob input. Every correct answer becomes a fixed point, and the loop converges as long
  as each pass makes fewer errors than its input.
- **Results:** Multi-answer Sudoku: 98.8% solved against 2.5% for the standard target. Sparse
  Sudoku: 93.8% against 0.0%, with no answer list. 200-node 3-colouring after training on at most
  50 nodes: 90.9% against 36.9%. Against GRAM, one run is correct 98.8% of the time against
  57.7%. Untying the weights across passes drops Sudoku to 82.9% at the same width and 23.0% at
  matched parameters.
- **Caveat:** Training needs a full answer list, a solver that can start from a draft, or a
  ranking. Models are at most 6.9M parameters, and GRAM stays slightly ahead on its own colouring
  benchmark (50.2% against 47.2% of colourings found).
- **Why added:** It is a supervision method for looped refiners that reuse one network over
  passes, with a tied-versus-untied ablation.

### LoopZip: Visual Token Compression for Efficient Looped Vision-Language Models

[arXiv:2611.22728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22728) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** LoopZip shortens the visual sequence of looped vision-language models without
training, choosing tokens that matter to more than one call of the same shared layer. LoopVL-1B
keeps 97.16% of its accuracy and runs 2.01-2.47x faster end to end.

- **Setup:** Two looped VLMs, LoopVL-1B and SenseNova-Loop-9B-Preview, on eleven benchmarks at
  three pruning ratios, against nine token-pruning baselines (FastV, VisionZip, CDPruner, QViD
  and others), measured in Hugging Face Transformers, vLLM and SGLang.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Visual tokens are first merged into a compact candidate pool. Tokens
  are then kept by averaging instruction attention over the first two calls of one shared
  attention layer. A single value correction, fitted jointly to both calls' outputs, reduces the
  deviation that pruning causes in every later call of the shared weights.
- **Results:** On LoopVL-1B, LoopZip keeps 97.16% of the original average score (59.02 against
  60.58), above QViD at 94.62%, and exceeds the strongest baseline by 5.36% at the most
  aggressive ratio. On SenseNova-Loop-9B it keeps 96.02%. Peak GPU memory falls by
  58.36-58.98%.
- **Caveat:** It is a training-free add-on tested on only two looped VLMs, and it does not
  compare looped and untied models.
- **Why added:** It is a deployment method designed around shared layers called several times
  in one forward pass.

### LoopPrune: Recurrent Pruning for Looped Diffusion Transformers

[arXiv:2611.22757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22757) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** LoopPrune prunes the shared block of looped diffusion Transformers with one mask for
all loop positions and then recovers each loop position against a dense teacher. Looped-DiT
B/16 keeps 93.14% of its dense accuracy at 30% structured pruning.

- **Setup:** Four looped diffusion models (Looped-DiT B/16 and B/32 with four loops, SDXSv3 and
  Surjo-Image-Preview with two) under structured, unstructured and 2:4 pruning, on six
  text-to-image benchmarks including GenEval and DPG-Bench, against Wanda, SparseGPT, EcoDiff,
  OBS-Diff and other pruning methods.
- **Mechanism / Method:** The shared block is called several times within each denoising step.
  Importance scores combine the effect of removing a unit at every loop position with how much
  that effect varies across positions. Retained weights are compensated with input statistics
  pooled over positions, then trained against a fixed dense teacher on complete denoising
  predictions at each loop position.
- **Results:** At 30% structured pruning, Looped-DiT B/16 averages 67.36 against 72.32 dense,
  9.49 points above the best baseline. Pruned SDXSv3 uses 34.36-44.64% less peak GPU memory and
  denoises 1.46-1.74x faster.
- **Caveat:** Recovery uses only 256 COCO captions, some margins over baselines are small
  (+0.04 on Looped-DiT B/32 at 50% unstructured), and the paper has no limitations section.
- **Why added:** The loop is block reuse inside one denoising network evaluation, not the
  diffusion sampling steps, so it is a systems paper on looped models.

### Latent Fact Composition under Next-Token Training

[arXiv:2611.21921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21921) · Code: not stated in the paper

**TL;DR:** In a synthetic two-hop task, a plain looped transformer, a standard transformer and
DiscoLoop answer almost no questions whose bridge entity never appears in two-hop training.
Baton looped transformers, which hand the bridge to the second loop as a token embedding, answer
about 99% of them, but next-token training makes that accuracy rise and then decline.

- **Setup:** Small models (width 256; a 4-block stack looped twice with 3.43M parameters, against
  an 8-block untied stack with 6.59M) trained on a two-graph corpus of 10,000 atomic facts and
  10,000 two-hop sentences, tested on 2,000 questions from a graph whose bridges are never seen
  in two-hop form, under answer-only and next-token losses.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Baton adds a slot stream that holds token embeddings in loop 1 and,
  between the loops, is replaced by a soft decode of a hidden state through the tied embedding
  matrix. The authors propose three design conditions for success and test them one at a time,
  then split the next-token gradient into three terms.
- **Results:** The baselines stay at or below 1.2% on these questions. Under answer-only
  training, Baton-H ends at 0.99 and Baton-Q0 at 0.99. Under next-token training, Baton-Q0 peaks
  at 0.78 and ends at 0.49. Removing the two training-set-dependent gradient terms raises the
  peak from 0.78 to 0.93 but does not stop the decline, which happens in the second hop while
  the first hop still recalls the bridge.
- **Caveat:** One synthetic template, two hops and models of a few million parameters; the
  diagnostic targets need the known data generator, and the cause of the decline remains open.
- **Why added:** It is a controlled analysis of what looped transformers (the same blocks
  applied twice in one forward pass) need in order to compose facts, including a negative result
  under next-token training.

## Near misses

- **Large Language Models as Nonlinear Dynamical Systems** ([2611.21983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21983)):
  a broad dictionary that maps nonlinear-dynamics ideas onto ordinary language models across
  layers, training and generation. Its depth clock iterates over untied layers, and it does not
  study models that reuse a block.
- **RICoT** ([2611.22382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22382)): a prompting framework that carries
  a reading summary across repeated LLM calls over a page stream; these are repeated model calls,
  not reused internal layers.
