# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-11-09

## Outcome

Two papers were added: a new recurrent reasoner in the HRM/TRM line, and a stereo-matching
method that changes what a weight-shared refinement step sees. Two further in-scope papers, a
looped mixture-of-experts language model and a medical-segmentation study, were judged too
preliminary or too small in effect to list. One of the additions cites Benhao Huang's work: Energy-Based Reasoners use *Equilibrium Reasoners* as a
main baseline, evaluating its released checkpoint, and discuss it as related work on attractors

## Added

### Energy-Based Reasoners: Your Recurrent Reasoner Should Be an Energy-Based Model
[arXiv:2611.08977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08977) · Code: [wwillchen/ebr](https://github.com/wwillchen/ebr) · Project: [energy-based-reasoners.github.io](https://energy-based-reasoners.github.io)

**TL;DR:** An EBR learns a scalar energy over a latent reasoning state and loops by taking
gradient steps on it, so one function drives refinement, stopping and candidate selection. It
reaches 99.9% on Sudoku-Extreme with 345x less inference compute than the next-best model.

- **Setup:** Sudoku-Extreme and Maze-Hard (1,000 training examples each), plus the
  multi-solution N-Queens and Graph Coloring benchmarks. Baselines are TRM, PTRM, Equilibrium
  Reasoners, FPRM, GRAM and Looped Flows; results are averaged over three seeds.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A two-block Transformer with input injection, as in TRM, followed by
  an energy head. Each loop step is a projected Langevin step down the gradient of that energy,
  with the latent kept on a sphere. Training uses TRM-style deep supervision with truncated
  backpropagation through second-order gradients, randomized step counts and step sizes, and a
  convergence loss that ties stationarity to a correct answer. A state-based Explorative
  Modeling scheme keeps several trajectories per input and supervises the one that best matches
  a valid solution. At inference, a trajectory stops when the gradient norm falls below a
  threshold, and the lowest-energy sample is returned.
- **Results:**
  - Single-sample accuracy: 99.4% on Sudoku-Extreme and 87.4% on Maze-Hard with 1.8M
    parameters, against 94.2% / 87.0% for FPRM, 93.0% Sudoku for Equilibrium Reasoners and
    97.9% / 86.7% for Looped Flows.
  - Five samples: 99.9% and 88.3%. With three samples it already passes Equilibrium Reasoners'
    99.8% at 128 samples.
  - Coverage of distinct valid N-Queens solutions rises from 57.5% (GRAM) to 92.0%.
  - Swapping the energy-gradient update for a directly learned transition drops single-sample
    Sudoku accuracy from 98.96% to 60.05%; removing the convergence loss drops it to 45.32%.
- **Caveat:** Each step needs an energy gradient, and training needs second-order gradients
  (the authors wrote custom kernels). Gains on Maze-Hard are small, and all tasks are small
  structured puzzles.
- **Why added:** The same energy network is evaluated and differentiated at every step of one
  inference trajectory, so a single learned operator is reused as depth, and the paper competes
  directly with looped reasoners on halting, test-time scaling and diversity.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** *Equilibrium Reasoners* (Huang, Geng, Kolter) is a main baseline,
  evaluated from its released checkpoint for accuracy and inference compute, and is discussed
  as related work on attractors and fixed-point reasoning.

### Left-Right-Consistent Iterative Stereo Matching via Cross-Injection
[arXiv:2611.09458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09458) · Code: [facebookresearch/stereo_crossinjection](https://github.com/facebookresearch/stereo_crossinjection)

**TL;DR:** Cross-injection gives each step of a RAFT-style stereo refiner the signed
disagreement with the other view's current disparity, which makes the two views agree far more
often and also lowers single-view error.

- **Setup:** Fine-tuning public FoundationStereo and S²M² (L and XL) checkpoints, compared with a
  matched fine-tune without cross-injection, on Middlebury, ETH3D, KITTI-12/15 and Booster.
- **Mechanism / Method:** The left pass and the flipped-and-swapped right pass share the
  weight-shared ConvGRU refiner. At every step, each pass warps the partner's detached disparity
  into its own frame, subtracts it, masks out pixels not visible in both views, and feeds this
  signed residual and the mask through a zero-initialized encoder into the GRU. A left-right
  consistency loss with per-pixel gradient routing is added in training.
- **Results:** Left-right disagreement on Booster is more than halved for FoundationStereo and
  reduced by about a third for both S²M² sizes. Consistency improves on every benchmark against
  the matched control. At equal latency, cross-injection with fewer iterations beats the baseline
  with more.
- **Caveat:** Each step costs extra for the warp and mask, and the two passes still start from
  independent initial disparities. The arXiv comment reports WACV 2027 acceptance; the catalog
  keeps arXiv until an official record appears.
- **Why added:** The contribution is a new per-step input to a learned GRU update operator that
  is reused across refinement steps within one forward pass, the RAFT pattern the catalog
  already files under flow, stereo and 3D.

## Near misses

- **AlterNet** ([2611.08741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08741)): in scope (a routed pool of
  single-block experts re-entered at every iteration, with learned halting), but an early,
  undertrained study whose adaptive model still trails fixed depth (54.3 vs 40.1 C4 perplexity).
- **Recurrent refinement for medical segmentation** ([2611.09436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09436)):
  in scope (a shared CRF, Hopfield or ConvGRU block iterated on U-Net logits), but clean-data gains
  are at most 1.4 Dice points and the few-shot results vary widely across seeds.
- **PHiT** ([2611.09006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09006)): supervises thought tokens supplied
  in one causal prefill and explicitly removes recurrent hidden-state feedback, so no layer is
  reused.
- **Adaptive depth aggregation in Graph Transformers** ([2611.08500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08500)):
  reweights the residual contributions of an untied stack; no layer or block is applied twice.
- **MIRA** ([2611.09412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09412)): studies the state transition of
  linear recurrent language models, which is ordinary sequence recurrence.
- **Provable state estimation with recurrent models** ([2611.09217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09217)):
  theory for sequence-recurrent filters, without depth reuse within one forward pass.
