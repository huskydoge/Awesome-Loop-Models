# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-11-10

## Outcome

Four papers were added. One is about deciding when a looped block should stop (an RL policy
that learns its own halting), one is about how to spend test-time loops in a recurrent-depth
language model, one applies shared recurrent blocks to wireless channel modeling, and one is a
theory paper whose depth law and CLRS-30 results rest on looped pair layers. None of today's
additions cites Benhao Huang's work.

## Added

### Learning When to Think: Adaptive Internal Computation for Reinforcement Learning
[arXiv:2611.10822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10822) · Code: none stated

**TL;DR:** AIC is an RL policy that refines its latent state with one weight-shared residual
block and learns, from the environment return, when to stop and act; it runs 3.3 of at most
eight refinement steps on average and cuts per-decision latency by 31-44%.

- **Setup:** Twelve benchmark tasks covering control, robotics manipulation (e.g. PickCube,
  StackCube), navigation (AntMaze, FourRooms), a synthetic RareEvent task and language inputs
  through a frozen GPT-2 encoder. Baselines are fixed-depth policies trained at 1, 2, 4 or 8
  steps, four untrained stopping rules, ACT-ponder and a learned non-value-of-computation
  threshold, all with the same encoder and refinement block.
- **Mechanism / Method:** An encoder maps the state to z_0, then one residual MLP block,
  z_d = z_{d-1} + MLP(z_{d-1}), is applied up to eight times with the same parameters. Each
  depth has action, value and halting heads. Stopping depth and action are treated as one
  decision of a joint PPO policy, with an expected-depth penalty and value predictions at every
  depth as auxiliary supervision.
- **Results:** 3.3 refinement steps on average across the twelve tasks; 31-44% lower
  per-decision latency than a fixed eight-step policy on four control and robotics tasks; mean
  reward above the best fixed-depth policy on seven tasks and below it on four. Against ACT-ponder
  and the learned threshold, it matches or exceeds reward on six of seven tasks while executing
  fewer steps on all seven.
- **Caveat:** The looped block is a small residual MLP (about 2M parameters in total), so this is
  evidence about learned halting rather than about large looped models. FourRooms is a clear
  failure case. The NeurIPS 2026 acceptance is stated by the authors and is not yet recorded as
  the venue.
- **Why added:** Adaptive halting over a single reused block within one forward pass, with
  credit for the stopping decision taken from reward; filed under depth control, with
  embodied and RL applications.

### Depth as a Candidate Source: Portfolio Allocation for Recurrent Test-Time Scaling in Large Language Models
[arXiv:2611.12293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12293) · Code: anonymous repository linked in the paper

**TL;DR:** Running Huginn for more loops can lose answers it found at fewer loops, so the paper
treats each loop count as a separate source of candidate answers and splits a fixed budget
across them, raising oracle coverage by up to 9.25 points at matched candidate count and cost.

- **Setup:** A frozen Huginn recurrent-depth model on GSM8K, SVAMP, MultiArith and MBPP, with
  loop counts from 8 to 32. Candidates are combined by a Borda selector over latent reward
  models.
- **Mechanism / Method:** A preliminary analysis decodes answers at 13 loop counts and shows that
  the sets of questions solved at different depths are non-nested (77 of 78 depth pairs on
  MultiArith, 78 of 78 on MBPP), even after controlling for each depth's accuracy. Depth
  Portfolio Allocation then picks how many candidates to draw at each depth under a budget
  measured in recurrent steps, either as a fixed portfolio or with a PPO policy that chooses the
  next depth or stops.
- **Results:** At a fixed nominal cost of 144 recurrent steps, a portfolio over depths 8-24 lifts
  oracle coverage over nine draws at depth 16 by 9.25 points on GSM8K, 7.70 on SVAMP and 5.66 on
  MBPP. PPO-guided allocation reaches 47.62% (GSM8K) and 92.12% (MultiArith) at a 320-step cap,
  against 32.60% and 75.00% for a single trajectory, and generally matches or improves uniform
  allocation, sometimes leaving budget unused.
- **Caveat:** One frozen model and four small benchmarks; the latent-state diagnostics use twenty test
  questions per task, and the cost measure counts recurrent steps but excludes decoding and selection.
- **Why added:** A test-time scaling study of a recurrent-depth language model that documents a
  failure mode of "loop longer" stopping rules and proposes an alternative use of loop counts.

### WiFo-R: Wireless Foundation Model with Recurrent Channel Representation
[arXiv:2611.11275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11275) · Code: none stated

**TL;DR:** WiFo-R loops two shared Transformer blocks over learnable channel queries, adding new
latent tokens each pass, so one wireless channel model offers several accuracy-cost operating
points; it uses 3.3x fewer parameters than untied stacking and generalizes better.

- **Setup:** Pretraining on heterogeneous channel state information (CSI) across many antenna,
  time and frequency configurations, then evaluation on temporal and frequency-domain channel
  prediction, channel estimation, multi-rate CSI feedback, localization and LoS/NLoS
  classification, in and out of distribution.
- **Mechanism / Method:** CSI patches are read by cross-attention from K = 16 channel queries. A
  recurrent encoder of two Transformer blocks with shared parameters runs for up to six steps;
  each step refreshes the existing latent tokens and adds 16 new ones, so depth s gives 16s
  tokens. The decoder is a single block also applied six times with shared weights. A
  multi-depth, multi-mask reconstruction loss supervises every depth, so any depth can be
  selected at inference.
- **Results:** Against independently parameterized stacking of the same depth, the shared
  model drops parameters from 87.85M to 26.40M and improves OOD NMSE by 2.22 dB (temporal
  prediction) and 0.91 dB (frequency prediction). Going from 1 to 5 steps lowers in-distribution
  NMSE by 2.75 dB for temporal prediction, and localization RMSE falls from 1.191 m to 0.805 m
  (1,000 labels) as depth grows from one to six. One feedback model serves six payloads from 160
  to 960 bits.
- **Caveat:** Training without the dynamic-depth objective degrades accuracy sharply, so the
  multi-depth loss, not the sharing alone, carries much of the result. The paper states no code
  release.
- **Why added:** A weight-tied recurrent encoder and decoder inside one forward pass, applied to
  an external domain with an explicit looped-versus-untied comparison at equal depth.

### Attention Is Only as Good as Its Witnesses: When Higher-Order Layers Help, and When They Do Not
[arXiv:2611.11937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11937) · Code: [NeoVand/witnesses](https://github.com/NeoVand/witnesses) · Project: [neovand.github.io/witnesses](https://neovand.github.io/witnesses)

**TL;DR:** The paper views each attention-style layer as summing over a family of "witnesses" and
reading the sum at a temperature, proves that L pair layers, or one layer looped L times, reach
exactly 2^L - 1 search steps, and shows a single looped 186K-parameter block matching a 73M-
parameter model on most CLRS-30 graph tasks.

- **Setup:** Graph search on synthetic graphs (trained up to 49 vertices, tested on 125 and 250),
  the card game SET, the twelve graph tasks of CLRS-30 without hints (train on up to 16 nodes,
  test on 64), plus language modeling and chess as negative controls. Proofs are checked in Lean.
- **Mechanism / Method:** Two laws. The depth law: over two-step walks, L layers, stacked or one
  layer looped L times, reach exactly 2^L - 1 steps, and no network of the class reaches further
  from anonymous initial states. The size law: reading existence correctly at every size needs
  inverse temperature times margin of at least log(number of candidates). New layers built on
  these laws include a propagator that solves for walks of every length in one linear solve and
  looped OR-join and power-mean pair layers.
- **Results:** One looped block of width 128 (186K parameters, eight iterations at 64 nodes)
  matches or beats FloydNet (about 73M parameters, 32-80 layers) on 9 of 12 CLRS-30 tasks of the
  official test set, for example 98.3% vs 96.2% on Floyd-Warshall. Looped OR-join and looped
  triangle attention trained at 49 vertices answer at least 99.6% and 99.2% of questions on
  125-vertex graphs at every lookahead. On depth-first search, accuracy rises with the loop count.
- **Caveat:** Positive results are on synthetic and algorithmic tasks with small models, and pair
  layers add little in language modeling and chess. The FloydNet comparison crosses training
  protocols (FloydNet scores are taken from its paper), and the headline 250-vertex search result
  comes from the non-looped propagator layer.
- **Why added:** Theory and experiments on what a looped pair layer can compute, including an
  exact reach bound for looping and size generalization from extra iterations.

## Near misses

- **Graphene** (2611.11139): each Ising refinement block runs three mean-field updates built
  from its own attention logits, with a separate local system in every block; no learned layer is
  reused across depth.
- **STAIRS** (2611.10260): an unrolled MRI reconstruction method whose contribution is a
  schedule of Fourier supports across cascades; the paper does not describe a reused learned block.
- **Patching Up Circuit Learning via Parameter Sharing** (2611.12436): a recursive parameter-sharing
  construction for probabilistic circuits over image patches; sharing across circuit parts is not a
  layer reused in a loop.
- **Lost in Rounding** (2611.12127): studies bf16 rounding of the recurrent state in hybrid
  state-space reasoning models; ordinary sequence recurrence is out of scope.
- **Mental Imagery That Matters** (2611.10423): latent visual reasoning in multimodal LLMs that
  feeds generated latents back as inputs, without reusing internal layers.
